## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter in HP mustard, what do they look like, what weather favours them, and what is the HP evidence?

### Takeaway
The only HP-specific quantitative incidence number retrieved is for Alternaria blight (average severity 47.1% in HP, second-highest of the states listed, 2004-2020 AICRP survey). Recommended vision classes for HP: Alternaria blight, white rust (with downy mildew as its look-alike and the "stag head" flower symptom), powdery mildew, mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi, seen as curled leaves, honeydew, sooty mould), and a "chewed/shot-holed seedling leaf" class covering mustard sawfly, painted bug and (unconfirmed for India) flea beetle; Sclerotinia stem rot, bacterial blight/black rot and club root are whole-plant or root classes needing a different photo. HP-specific data for aphids, painted bug, sawfly, flea beetle, white rust and powdery mildew were not found.

### Cited Findings

**Alternaria blight (Alternaria brassicae)**
- HP evidence: Alternaria blight severity in HP averaged 47.1%, second only to Uttarakhand (48.5%) and ahead of Punjab (43.7%) and Bihar (38.9%); survey period 2004-2020; the abstract states the disease can cause up to 35% yield loss; source data are AICRP-Rapeseed-Mustard annual reports; sampling sites and scoring method in HP are not described on the page — JOR-AB-map
- B. napus (26.0%) and B. carinata (22.1%) were the least affected species in that national summary **[snippet]** — same JOR-AB-map record as surfaced by search summary; the figures were not on the abstract page I opened
- Symptoms: starts on lower leaves as small circular brown necrotic spots which slowly enlarge; many concentric spots coalesce into large blighted patches and defoliation in severe cases; circular to linear dark brown lesions on stems and pods, later elongating; infected pods give small discoloured, shrivelled seeds — NIPHM-Mustard
- Weather: moist (>70% RH) with warm weather (12-25 C) and intermittent rain; seed-borne (externally and internally); survives on plant debris and weeds — NIPHM-Mustard
- Hisar observation: Alternaria leaf blight "appears early but remains severe at the time of pod initiation stage"; symptoms of Alternaria and white rust on the same leaf are "quite common" — HAU-AB-WR
- Reported yield losses (older literature cited within): 17-48% from Alternaria blight, 23-54.5% from white rust (leaf and stag-head phases) — HAU-AB-WR

**White rust (Albugo candida) and downy mildew (Hyaloperonospora)**
- White rust symptoms: local infection gives white creamy-yellow raised pustules on leaves that later coalesce into patches; systemic infection in humid weather (often mixed with downy mildew) causes swelling and distortion of stem and floral parts ("stag head") — NIPHM-Mustard
- Weather: moist (>70% RH) with 12-25 C and intermittent rain; survives as oospores in tissue and soil; secondary spread by sporangia/zoospores — NIPHM-Mustard
- Downy mildew symptoms: grey-white irregular necrotic patches on the LOWER leaf surface, later brownish-white fungal growth on spots; conspicuous symptom is infected inflorescence with hypertrophied peduncle (stag head). Favoured by 10-20 C and >90% RH — NIPHM-Mustard
- Hisar: white rust "usually appears early and becomes severe on lower leaves at the time of flowering"; combined downy mildew + white rust infection was rarely observed because of dry cool weather there — HAU-AB-WR
- HP-specific white rust incidence: none retrieved (see Gaps). CSK HPKV Kangra ran a DST-funded project (2004-2007) on white rust and Alternaria blight of mustard in HP using plant extracts (details in KQ4/KQ6) — HPKV-Kangra

**Powdery mildew (Erysiphe)**
- Symptoms: dirty-white, circular, floury patches on either side of leaves; under favourable conditions whole leaves, stems, floral parts and pods covered by powdery mass; survives as cleistothecia in crop debris — NIPHM-Mustard
- Weather (opposite of the downy diseases): 15-28 C with low humidity (<60%) and low or no rainfall with wind — NIPHM-Mustard

**Mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi)**
- Appearance: wingless females yellowish-green, grey-green or olive-green with white waxy bloom, pear-shaped with a pair of cornicles; winged females have dusky green abdomen; eggs white along veins — NIPHM-Mustard
- Damage: nymphs and adults suck sap from leaves, buds and pods; infested leaves may curl; at advanced stage plants wither; plants stunted; sooty mould on honeydew. Attack generally from the 2nd-3rd week of December and continues till March (national package, not HP) — NIPHM-Mustard
- HP-specific aphid population dynamics: none retrieved. A search summary said that Western Himalayan hills act as a source of mustard aphids for the Indo-Gangetic plains **[snippet, not opened]**

**Painted bug (Bagrada hilaris)**
- Adult 5-7 mm shield-shaped, black/white/orange markings; nymphs bright orange to red with dark markings; eggs barrel-shaped, white turning orange; activity starts from September; adults and nymphs suck sap from all plant parts, young plants wilt and wither; adults excrete resinous substance that spoils pods; 31% yield loss cited — NIPHM-Mustard

**Mustard sawfly (Athalia lugens proxima)**
- Larva greenish-black, wrinkled, falls and feigns death when touched; adults orange-bodied; active at seedling stage October-November; larvae nibble leaves from margin toward midrib, causing numerous shot holes and riddled leaves; devour shoot epidermis causing seedlings to dry; yield loss 5-18%; severe seedling attack may force resowing — NIPHM-Mustard

**Other leaf problems in the national package**
- Leaf miner: linear whitish mines on leaf surface; attacked leaves wither; more prominent on older leaves — NIPHM-Mustard
- Diamondback moth: pale yellowish-green caterpillar feeding on foliage, leaves withered then holed; bores into pods — NIPHM-Mustard
- Bihar hairy caterpillar: young larvae feed gregariously on the underside of leaves; in severe infestation whole crop defoliated; eggs in clusters of 50-100 — NIPHM-Mustard
- Bacterial blight/black rot: yellowing in a V-shaped area with base toward the midrib, veins brown to black, dark streaks on stem from ground level, stem hollow by internal rot; warm humid climate — NIPHM-Mustard
- Club root: stunting, club-shaped outgrowths on roots, pale/yellow leaves and wilting; humid weather and high soil moisture — NIPHM-Mustard
- Sclerotinia stem rot: elongated water-soaked lesions on stem near the crown with cottony growth; plant looks whitish from a distance at internodes or base; premature ripening, stem shredding, wilting; black sclerotia later. Favoured by 90-95% RH, 18-25 C, wind — NIPHM-Mustard
- The package marks as "pests of regional significance" (region not named in the extracted text): Bihar hairy caterpillar, white rust/downy mildew, diamondback moth, cabbage head borer/leaf webber, bacterial blight, club root, Sclerotinia stem rot — NIPHM-Mustard
- Sclerotinia has been studied at SAREC Kangra (HP): 59 rapeseed-mustard genotypes showed no resistance under artificial field conditions; ten Brassica carinata genotypes were moderately susceptible (NRCG-104, Pusa Swarnim, Kiran, PBC-9221 among them) — Kangra-Scl
- Flea beetle (Phyllotreta): the NIPHM mustard package does not list it. General (non-Indian) guidance from UK/US extension sites says shot-hole damage is worst on seedlings, adults are 2-3 mm and jump when disturbed, and that Indian mustard is a preferred host in one Asian study **[snippet, non-HP, from RHS / Kentucky / UMass search results]** — [RHS](https://rhs.org.uk/advice/profile?pid=510)

### Inferences
- Recommended leaf-photo classes: (1) Alternaria blight, (2) white rust, (3) downy mildew (optional merge with white rust, since NIPHM and HAU both note they co-occur and both give stag heads), (4) powdery mildew, (5) aphid colony/curl/sooty mould, (6) chewed seedling leaves (sawfly/flea beetle/painted bug feeding). Whole-plant classes needing a different photo prompt: Sclerotinia (stem near crown), bacterial blight, club root.
- Key look-alike pairs: Alternaria (concentric brown rings, necrotic, lower leaves) vs. white rust (raised creamy-white pustules, not necrotic rings) vs. downy mildew (grey-white patches on UNDER surface, angular/irregular) vs. powdery mildew (floury white on BOTH sides, appears in warm dry conditions). Because white rust and Alternaria often coexist on one leaf (HAU-AB-WR), the app should allow multi-label output. Sawfly (larvae with shot holes from the margin, October-November seedlings) vs. flea beetle (small round pits, tiny jumping beetles, unconfirmed for HP mustard) vs. Bihar hairy caterpillar (hairy larvae, groups under leaf).
- Weather contrast useful for the app logic: cool humid spells with rain (12-25 C, >70% RH) favour Alternaria and white rust, >90% RH at 10-20 C favours downy mildew, whereas warm dry weather with wind (15-28 C, <60% RH) favours powdery mildew. This is a direct restatement of the NIPHM temperature/humidity ranges, not an HP measurement.

### Gaps
- No HP incidence/severity data for white rust, powdery mildew, aphid, painted bug, sawfly, flea beetle, or Sclerotinia (Kangra-Scl reports genotype screening only, no incidence percentages in the abstract).
- No altitude-wise (low/mid/high hill) incidence data.
- Flea beetle on Indian mustard: no Indian/HP source retrieved; only generic overseas guidance from search snippets.
- B. napus/B. carinata Alternaria numbers (26.0%, 22.1%) are snippet-only.
- NIPHM package does not name which region the "regional significance" flag refers to.

## KQ2. Cultural practices (sowing date, seed treatment, rotation, spacing, varieties, trap crops, sanitation)

### Takeaway
The best HP-specific sowing evidence is from CSK HPKV Palampur: gobhi sarson sown 20 October out-yielded 30 October and 10 November (seed yield fell from 2.43 to 1.71 t/ha at 20 Oct, and from 1.79 to 1.32 t/ha at 10 Nov across the seasons). The national NIPHM package gives the standard cultural toolkit (early/timely sowing, one irrigation in week IV after sowing, sanitation, rotation, resistant varieties, debris and stag-head destruction, summer ploughing), but none of its dates are HP-specific.

### Cited Findings

**Sowing date and varieties (HP evidence)**
- Palampur, rabi 2010-11 to 2014-15: five gobhi sarson varieties (GSL-1, ONK-1, HPN-1, HPN-3, Hyola-401 hybrid) at three sowing dates (20 Oct, 30 Oct, 10 Nov); seed yield declined with delay in sowing; the page states "from 2.43 to 1.71 t/ha for 20 October sowing, and from 1.79 to 1.32 t/ha for 10 November sowing" (range across seasons/varieties); ONK-1 sown 20 and 30 October was the most resource-efficient. The abstract mentions no pests or diseases — HPKV-GS-sowing
- A mid-hill HP study (1990-91, 1991-92) reported the highest seed and oil yield with sowing on 25 September at 30 cm row spacing **[snippet; from search summary of an ISA-India Indian Journal of Agronomy PDF, not opened]** — [pub.isa-india.in](https://pub.isa-india.in/index.php/ija/article/download/3618/3303)
- HPKV on-farm demonstrations (1997-1999, 40 sites): Hyola-401 out-yielded Varuna by 58.9% — HPKV-Kangra
- Sclerotinia, B. napus package at Kangra: row spacing 45 cm; removal of 2-3 lower leaves at 80 and 100 days after sowing; plus one spray of a product named "companion" at 0.2% (the abstract does not identify what "companion" is; it may be a conventional fungicide brand, so do not list it as a natural method) — Kangra-Scl

**Sowing date, irrigation and sanitation (national package, not HP)**
- Timely sowing between 10-25 October is listed for Alternaria blight and club root; "early sowing" is listed for aphid, painted bug and sawfly escape; the DBM/sawfly row says "Sow crop before 20 October" — NIPHM-Mustard
- Irrigate in the 4th week after sowing (aphid ecological-engineering table); apply irrigation at seedling stage for sawfly because most larvae die by drowning; irrigation at seedling stage also listed for painted bug larvae — NIPHM-Mustard
- Deep summer ploughing to expose soil-borne pathogens and hibernating stages and to destroy painted-bug eggs and sawfly pupae — NIPHM-Mustard
- Level, well-drained field to reduce Sclerotinia; destroy plant debris — NIPHM-Mustard
- White rust/downy mildew: certified seed of resistant/tolerant varieties, timely sowing, field sanitation, crop rotation, destruction of crop debris particularly stag heads of the previous year's crop, avoid over-irrigation or water stagnation, apply potash at the recommended dose — NIPHM-Mustard
- Alternaria: certified/resistant seed, timely sowing 10-25 October, field sanitation, remove weeds particularly collateral hosts, recommended potash — NIPHM-Mustard
- Sclerotinia: deep summer ploughing, certified resistant/tolerant seed, timely sowing, sanitation, rotation with non-host crops "like rice and maize" — NIPHM-Mustard
- Club root: long-term rotation, destroy cruciferous weeds, improve drainage; soil amendment with lime at 1 kg/m2 to raise soil pH to 7.2, or neem cake at 0.5 kg/m2 — NIPHM-Mustard
- Sawfly and Bihar hairy caterpillar: burn the remains of the mustard crop so insect stages do not carry to next year's crop; quick threshing; collect and destroy sawfly larvae in morning and evening; collect egg masses and gregarious early-instar caterpillars twice a week — NIPHM-Mustard
- Painted bug: jerk bugs off leaves and stems and kill in kerosene water (kerosene is a petroleum product; note for natural-farming users) — NIPHM-Mustard
- Pre-planting nutrients: FYM at 4.0 t/acre or vermicompost at 2.0 t/acre; seed treatment with Azotobacter culture at 240 g/acre; half the N at sowing; basal sulphur 16 kg/acre — NIPHM-Mustard

**Varieties tolerant to specific problems (national list)**
- White rust: Pusa Karishma (LES-39), Jawahar Mustard-1, PBR 91. Alternaria blight and aphids: Coral-432, NRCHB 5-6, NPJ 112 (Pusa Mustard 25), NRCDR 601, RYSKS-2, DMH-I. Alternaria: Saurabh. Aphids: RH-7846, RH-7847, RH-9020, RWAR-842. The package adds that for details the nearest KVK/SAU should be contacted — NIPHM-Mustard
- None of these were verified as released or adapted for HP hill zones.

**Trap, border and attractant plants (national package)**
- Leaf miner and Bihar hairy caterpillar trap crops: Jatropha, papaya, cowpea, castor, Crotalaria on field bunds — NIPHM-Mustard
- Attractant (natural-enemy) plants: wild mustard (Sinapis arvensis), white mustard (Sinapis alba), wild radish, wild turnip (Brassica rapa); insectary plants include Crotalaria, castor, cowpea, sunflower, maize, sorghum, French bean — NIPHM-Mustard
- Raise flowering plants on field borders with shorter plants toward the main crop; do not uproot weeds like Tridax, Ageratum, Alternanthera which give nectar to natural enemies; reduce tillage intensity to save hibernating natural enemies — NIPHM-Mustard
- Yellow sticky traps/yellow water pans set 15 cm above canopy at 4-5 per acre for monitoring aphids; set yellow sticky traps before the onset of flowering; empty tins painted yellow and greased may be used — NIPHM-Mustard
- Light traps 1 per acre, operated 6 pm to 10 pm; pheromone traps for diamondback moth, leaf webber, leaf miner at 4-5 per acre — NIPHM-Mustard
- A safflower-bordered plot had the fewest aphids and the highest yield, followed by maize, in a field study on eco-friendly aphid management **[snippet; from search summary of a KrishiKosh record, not opened, non-HP]**

### Inferences
- For HP hill zones the evidence-backed date guidance is "sow gobhi sarson around 20 October at mid-hill Palampur conditions; later sowing loses yield". It did not measure disease or aphid load, so the aphid/disease-escape argument for early sowing in HP rests on the national NIPHM text, not on HP data.
- Rotation with rice and maize (Sclerotinia) is feasible in the HP Shivalik/low-hill rice-maize belts but this fit is my inference; no HP rotation trial was found.
- Seed treatment with a cow-dung/urine mix (Beejamrit, KQ5) is the natural-farming counterpart of the seed-borne Alternaria advice, but no mustard trial of Beejamrit was found.

### Gaps
- No HP-specific recommendations for toria and brown sarson (B. campestris) sowing dates, seed rate or spacing were retrieved; the official CSK HPKV package of practices was not found online.
- No HP source on intercropping mustard with wheat/gram/pulses, though NIPHM lists "intercropping with wheat/pulses/sugarcane" in a row for weeds/aphids/painted bug.
- Mustard-free interval for Sclerotinia sclerotia (survival "more than 4 years" per DRMR-Scl) means rotation alone cannot clean a field quickly; no HP rotation data.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses (Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Beauveria, Verticillium lecanii, predators, traps)

### Takeaway
Biological inputs have real but modest efficacy against mustard foliar diseases, and none of the foliar-disease trials was in HP. The best-documented bio-input is Trichoderma harzianum for Sclerotinia rot (seed treatment 5-10 g/kg, soil application 2-2.5 kg/ha or 2 g/kg FYM, foliar 5 g/l or 0.2%). For Alternaria blight and white rust the bio-only treatments "showed evidence of effectiveness upto some extent", while the numerically strong results in the Hisar trial came from combinations with a fungicide spray. No dose was found for fungal insect-killers on mustard aphid.

### Cited Findings

**Trichoderma / Pseudomonas / garlic: Alternaria blight and white rust (Hisar, Haryana, non-HP)**
- Treatments tested (Indian mustard cv. Varuna, sown Nov 15 2007 and Nov 4 2008): seed treatment with aqueous bulb extract of Allium sativum at 1% (w/v), Trichoderma harzianum at 10 g/kg seed; foliar sprays at 50-60 DAS of garlic bulb extract 1% (w/v), T. harzianum (oil based) at 10 g/l water, Pseudomonas fluorescens (oil based) at 10 g/l water — HAU-AB-WR
- Result (stated in text): combinations of seed treatment and foliar sprays of garlic extract, T. harzianum and P. fluorescens "also showed evidence of their effectiveness in controlling white rust and Alternaria blight upto some extent". The headline numbers (Alternaria leaf blight reduced up to 43.6%, white rust up to 39.5%, yield up to 26.4% higher) were for T. harzianum seed treatment followed by a Ridomil MZ 72 WP (metalaxyl + mancozeb) spray, i.e. NOT a natural-only treatment — HAU-AB-WR
- The table of bio-only treatments in the PDF extracted in garbled form, so per-treatment percentages for bio-only combinations were not read and are not quoted here.

**Trichoderma harzianum: Sclerotinia rot (DRMR Bharatpur, Rajasthan, non-HP; single-season trial 2007-08, cv. Rohini, pH 8.0 loam)**
- Formulations of four T. harzianum isolates (GR, P, SI-01, SI-02) in 1:9 and 1:19 ratios; seed treatment at 5 g/kg seed; soil application at 2 g/kg FYM; foliar spray 5 g/l; garlic bulb extract 2% (w/v) as seed treatment or spray — DRMR-Scl
- Sclerotinia reduction over control: foliar spray T. harzianum isolate GR (1:9) 5 g/l = 69% (actual value); soil application with FYM of isolate SI-02 (1:9) 2 g/kg = 60.8%; foliar spray garlic bulb extract 2% w/v = 60.8%; soil application GR (1:9) 2 g/kg = 57.7%; seed treatment GR (1:9) 5 g/kg = 53.6%. Yield was highest (3156 kg/ha) with GR or SI-01 as seed treatment — DRMR-Scl
- The same paper's seed-treatment with garlic 2% (w/v) gave 40.2% reduction, and mancozeb spray 2.5 g/l 22.7%, carbendazim seed treatment 2 g/kg 33.0% in the actual-value column — DRMR-Scl
- Multi-location NCIPM validation: Trichoderma mixture (T. viride + T. hamatum, 2x10^9 cfu/g) as seed treatment at 10 g/kg + soil application at 2.5 kg/ha pre-incubated in FYM + 0.2% foliar sprays at 50 and 70 DAS; incidence reduced to a minimum of 11.1% against 26.9% in control; a related ICAR record of T. harzianum (2x10^6 cfu/g) with the same schedule gave 4.2% incidence and validated across 188 locations in Haryana and Rajasthan **[snippet; search summary of krishi.icar.gov.in records, not opened, non-HP]** — [ICAR record](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/75054)
- HP angle: SAREC Kangra reported T. koningii isolate JMA-11 gave 98.5% in vitro inhibition of S. sclerotiorum mycelial growth (isolate DMA-8 90.4%); this is an in-vitro result with no field dose in the abstract — Kangra-Scl

**Trichoderma and Pseudomonas, general (NIPHM)**
- The package recommends Trichoderma spp. and Pseudomonas fluorescens as seed/seedling/planting-material treatment and soil application and notes that biopesticides produced by farmers for their own use do not need registration, but it gives NO dose for mustard — NIPHM-Mustard

**Predators, parasitoids and fungi on aphids (NIPHM, non-HP)**
- Named aphid natural enemies: ladybirds (Coccinella septempunctata, Menochilus sexmaculata, Hippodamia variegata, Cheilomenes vicina), syrphid flies, the braconid parasitoid Diaeretiella rapae, the lacewing Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi, the wagtail Motacilla cospicua; "a number of entomogenous fungi, Cephalosporium spp., Entomophthora and Verticillium lecanii infect aphids"; the package states an adult ladybird beetle "may feed an average of 10 to 15 adults/day" (as printed) — NIPHM-Mustard
- No spore concentration or product dose is given for Verticillium lecanii in the package — NIPHM-Mustard
- Painted bug: conserve the egg parasitoid Alophora spp. (tachinid fly); sawfly: conserve the larval parasitoid Perilissus cingulator and the bacterium Serratia marcescens; Bihar hairy caterpillar: conserve spiders, mantids, lacewings, ground beetles, Trichogramma/braconids, green muscardine fungus; leaf miner: conserve Diglyphus isaea, Gronotoma micromorpha, Halticoptera circulus, Opius phaseoli — NIPHM-Mustard
- Botanical-spray side effect: in the Meghalaya mid-hill trial botanicals were "much less toxic" to non-target species whereas chlorpyrifos and imidacloprid were highly toxic to honey bees and coccinellid beetles — NEH-Aphid

**Traps**
- Yellow sticky/water pan traps 4-5 per acre at 15 cm above canopy (monitoring; sticky cards may also reduce leaf miner density); light trap 1 per acre 6-10 pm; pheromone traps (DBM, leaf webber, leaf miner) 4-5 per acre, lures changed every 2-3 weeks — NIPHM-Mustard

### Inferences
- If the app suggests a biological option for Alternaria blight/white rust, the honest wording is "partial suppression, best started at seed treatment; do not rely on it alone in a wet spell". The 43.6%/39.5% figures should not be quoted as natural-method results because a metalaxyl+mancozeb spray was part of that package.
- For Sclerotinia, the trial rates (T. harzianum seed 5-10 g/kg, soil 2-2.5 kg/ha or 2 g/kg FYM, spray 5 g/l or 0.2%) are the only dose-bearing biocontrol evidence for mustard in the retrieved material. A farmer-made Trichoderma product would have different spore counts, so label-dose equivalents cannot be inferred.
- For aphids in HP, conserving ladybirds/hover flies/Diaeretiella (avoid broad-spectrum sprays, use neem, grow border flowers) is supported by NIPHM, but without a dose-based fungal product.

### Gaps
- No dose for Verticillium (Lecanicillium) lecanii or Beauveria bassiana on mustard aphid retrieved (two searches on this were rejected by the search tool and not retried).
- No Pseudomonas fluorescens dose beyond the Hisar oil-based 10 g/l foliar spray; no seed-treatment dose for P. fluorescens on mustard.
- No HP field trial of Trichoderma/Pseudomonas on mustard foliar diseases or on Sclerotinia (Kangra-Scl has only in-vitro data for Trichoderma).
- No Trichogramma release dose given in the NIPHM text I read for mustard.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
Neem products are the best-supported botanical for mustard aphid: NIPHM lists 2% neem oil and 5% NSKE, and the Hisar trial measured neem oil 3% at 76.4% and NSKE 5% at 71.4% aphid reduction seven days after application versus 93.2% for dimethoate. Botanicals from hill plants (Melia, Lantana, etc. at 5%) gave only about 50-62% reduction in a Meghalaya mid-hill trial. For white rust and Alternaria, HP researchers at CSK HPKV found local plant extracts effective, but no concentration is published on the page I could open.

### Cited Findings

**Aphid**
- NIPHM: "2 per cent Neem oil and 5 per cent Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE) effective against the mustard aphid" — NIPHM-Mustard
- Hisar 2016-17, 13 botanicals/insecticides tested, reduction in aphid population over control seven days after application: dimethoate 30 EC at 625 ml/ha 93.2%; azadirachtin 10000 ppm at 1.0 ml/l 81.6%; azadirachtin 1500 ppm at 1.0 ml/l 78.49%; neem oil 3% 76.4%; NSKE 5% 71.4%. Benefit-cost ratio: dimethoate 1:7.6, azadirachtin 1500 ppm 1:5.4, azadirachtin 10000 ppm 1:4.1, NSKE 5% 1:3.1. Authors recommend azadirachtin 1500 ppm at 1.0 ml/l as the economical eco-friendly alternative — Hisar-Aphid (azadirachtin is a commercial formulation; whether it is acceptable under a given natural-farming protocol is a separate question)
- Meghalaya mid hills (Umiam, cv. Varuna, 8 treatments, sprays at 15-day intervals), 5% aqueous solutions of Melia azedarach (bakain) leaf, Lantana camara leaf, turmeric rhizome powder, marigold leaf, vasaka (Adhatoda vasica) leaf: Melia azedarach was the best botanical with 50.5-61.9% aphid reduction after the first spray and 50.7-57.5% after the second; vasaka next, then turmeric, lantana, marigold; chlorpyrifos 20% EC and imidacloprid gave the greatest reduction — NEH-Aphid
- Other aphid botanical results **[snippet, non-HP, not opened]**: lantana leaf extract 1% best among botanicals in a field study on eco-friendly aphid management ([KrishiKosh record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/cbfa1edd-02d4-47c7-95f8-f1261a290f33)); neem oil 0.3% + a chemical in Bangladesh (excluded, includes insecticide)

**Diamondback moth / leaf webber / hairy caterpillar**
- Application of 4% NSKE is listed under DBM and under the cabbage head borer/leaf webber/hairy caterpillar row — NIPHM-Mustard

**White rust, downy mildew**
- Treat the seeds with freshly prepared garlic bulb extract at 2% (w/v) — NIPHM-Mustard
- Hisar (non-HP): garlic bulb extract at 1% (w/v) as seed treatment and spray at 50-60 DAS gave partial control (see KQ3) — HAU-AB-WR
- CSK HPKV Kangra (HP), DST project 2004-2007: aqueous extracts of Azadirachta indica, Agave americana, Eucalyptus citriodora and Zanthoxylum alatum gave "85.8-92.0% reduction in severity" of white rust; the page does not give extract concentration, whether pot or field, or replication — HPKV-Kangra

**Alternaria blight**
- CSK HPKV Kangra: aqueous extracts of Agave americana, Zanthoxylum alatum, Aegle marmelos, Sapindus mukorossi and Terminalia belerica gave "64.9-79.9% control of Alternaria blight of mustard in pots through induced resistance", with raised PAL and peroxidase activity and phenolics; concentration not given — HPKV-Kangra
- The page also lists extracts of Cnicus wallichii, Eupatorium adenophorum, Ipomoea cornea, Eucalyptus citriodora, Ficus roxburghii, Agave americana and Thuja orientalis as effective against oilseed diseases/pests among 189 North-West Himalayan plant samples (2001-2004) — HPKV-Kangra

**Sclerotinia**
- Kangra in-vitro: Vitex negundo extract inhibited S. sclerotiorum 100% at 5%, 96.7% at 2.5%, 55.6% at 1%; Melia azedarach extract 98.3% at 5%, 91.7% at 2.5%, 57.6% at 1% (laboratory plates only, not a field result) — Kangra-Scl
- Garlic bulb extract 2% (w/v) foliar spray: 60.8% Sclerotinia reduction at Bharatpur (see KQ3) — DRMR-Scl

**Other**
- Bitter gourd seed oil emulsion as an antifeedant for sawfly (no concentration given) — NIPHM-Mustard
- Soil amendments: neem cake 0.5 kg/m2 or lime 1 kg/m2 for club root — NIPHM-Mustard

### Inferences
- Neem is the only botanical with both an official-package concentration (2% oil, 5% NSKE) and a measured field result (Hisar); even so, it gave about 71-76% reduction versus 93% for dimethoate in a single season, so heavy aphid infestations (a colony covering the flowering raceme) may exceed what neem alone controls. This "limited control at high pressure" conclusion is my reading of the Hisar and Meghalaya numbers, not a statement from the authors.
- Plants flagged by CSK HPKV (Agave americana, Zanthoxylum alatum "timur", Melia azedarach "dharek", Vitex negundo "banna", Eucalyptus, Aegle, Sapindus) are locally available in HP lower hills, so they are candidate "HP botanicals" for the app, but the app must not display any concentration for them because none was found.

### Gaps
- No published concentration/dose for the CSK HPKV extracts (white rust and Alternaria); need the original Guleria et al. papers.
- No botanical dose or efficacy data for painted bug, sawfly, flea beetle, or powdery mildew on mustard.
- Hisar and Meghalaya trials were run in single seasons and are not HP; no HP aphid botanical trial found.
- Whether azadirachtin commercial formulations are acceptable inside HP PK3Y rules was not checked.

## KQ5. Natural-farming formulations (Jeevamrit, Beejamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra, Brahmastra, Khatti Lassi, Dashparni) with doses; HP PK3Y / UHF Nauni / CSK HPKV sources

### Takeaway
No HP PK3Y/UHF/CSK HPKV document with mustard-specific doses was found. The HP-origin UHF Nauni article gives preparation (not always dilution) for Beejamrit, Jeevamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra and Brahmastra; the ICAR-NEH Meghalaya article gives complete recipes with spray dilutions (non-HP); a Hamirpur farmer story gives a lassi spray rate. The recipes differ between sources (batch sizes and dilutions), and some printed numbers look like typos, so the app should present each recipe with its source or defer to the local PK3Y/KVK instruction.

### Cited Findings

**Beejamrit (seed treatment)**
- UHF Nauni (HP): take 5 kg cow dung in a cloth bundle and hang it in 20 litres of water up to 12 hours; separately add 50 g lime to 1 litre water overnight; next morning squeeze the dung bundle in the water thrice; add a handful of soil; add 5 litres desi cow urine and the lime water; spread over seeds, mix by hand, dry well and sow — UHF-NF (the article's two-column layout interleaves lines; the sequence above is my reading of the steps and the quantities are as printed)
- ICAR-NEH (Meghalaya): fresh cow dung 200 g, cow urine 200 ml, lime 2 g, water 800 ml, soil 8 g; "mainly used for seed treatment at the rate of 25 L/kg of seed" (as printed; the rate looks like a typographical problem since 25 L per kg of seed is implausible); seeds soaked about one hour and shade-dried; transplants dipped 1-2 h — NEH-NF
- Hamirpur farmer (HP): 200 g cow dung, 200 ml cow urine, double the amount of water and 10 g limestone; seeds left 24 h in summer or 48 h in winter; large seeds washed in the solution and small seeds coated with a paste — KJ-Hamirpur
- Claimed role: "suppression of soil- and seed-borne pathogens" (NEH-NF) and "equally effective in controlling seed borne diseases" (UHF-NF). No mustard trial of Beejamrit against seed-borne Alternaria was found.

**Jeevamrit**
- UHF Nauni (HP): 10 kg cow dung and 10 litres cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour and a handful of soil from the bund, water added to make 200 litres in a barrel; stir and ferment in shade for 48 hours — UHF-NF
- ICAR-NEH: water 200 L, fresh cow dung 10 kg, cow urine 5-10 L, jaggery 2 kg, pulse flour 2 kg, a handful of soil, 48 hours; stored up to 15 days; in field crops applied with irrigation water and as a foliar feed at 200 L/acre at fortnightly intervals — NEH-NF
- Hamirpur farmer: 1 kg cow dung, 1 liter cow urine, 100 g sugar, 100 g jaggery, 10 liters water; spray 0.5 liter in 10 liters water every 15-21 days for crops of 60-90 days; soil use about 50 kg dried cow dung per bigha — KJ-Hamirpur
- Claimed role: UHF-NF says it "prevents fungal and bacterial plant diseases"; a farmer (success story 3, location not stated) in the same article reported Jeevamrit and Khatti Lassi "effective in controlling fungal diseases" in apple/peas/vegetables (not mustard) — UHF-NF
- No Jeevamrit-on-mustard disease or pest data found.

**Neemastra (soft-bodied sucking pests, small caterpillars)**
- UHF Nauni (HP): 100 litres water in a drum, 10 litres cow urine, 2 kg cow dung, 5 kg small neem twigs cut with leaves; stir clockwise, cover with a gunny bag, shade, 48 hours, stir morning and evening; useful for "whitefly, aphid, jassids, sap sucking insects, small caterpillars" — UHF-NF (spray dilution not stated in the extracted text)
- ICAR-NEH: 200 L water, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg local cow dung, 10 kg finely ground neem leaf paste or 10 kg neem seed pulp; stir twice daily; ready after 48 h; storable up to 6 months; the same paragraph says it "should not be diluted with water" and also "foliar spray at 3% concentration" against jassids, aphids, whiteflies, small caterpillars, mealybugs, which is internally inconsistent as printed — NEH-NF
- Hamirpur farmer calls a mixture of 20 L water, 1 kg cow dung, 1 liter cow urine, 100 g sugar, 100 g besan plus 50-100 g chopped neem or similar leaves and 50-100 g garlic/ginger/chilli chutney "Neemastra / Agniastra" and describes diluting dung and urine "20 times their volume in water" — KJ-Hamirpur (informal, mixes two names, treat with caution)

**Agniastra (sap-suckers, small caterpillars, pod borers)**
- UHF Nauni (HP): crushed neem leaves 2 kg, tobacco powder 500 g, green chilli chutney 500 g, garlic chutney 250 g in cow urine 20 litres, boil on low flame covered; keep 48 hours, stir morning and evening — UHF-NF (dilution not stated in the extracted text)
- ICAR-NEH: same plant ingredients plus 200 g turmeric powder, but "Pour 200 L of cow urine" (probably a printing error versus 20 L in UHF-NF); store up to 3 months; foliar spray at 2% concentration, "6-8 L of agniastra diluted in 200 L of water" per acre; lighter ratios for lower pressure: 100 L water + 3 L, 15 L water + 500 (as printed) and 10 L water + 300 (as printed) — NEH-NF

**Brahmastra (sucking pests, pod/fruit borers, caterpillars)**
- ICAR-NEH: 20 L cow urine with 2 kg each of neem, karanj, custard apple, castor and datura leaf paste; heat until it foams twice; cool 48 h; strain; storable up to six months; foliar spray at 3% by diluting 6-8 L in 200 L water on standing crops; adjusted ratios 100 L water + 3 L; 15 L water + 500 ml; 10 L water + 300 ml — NEH-NF
- UHF Nauni (HP): "any 5 plants viz. Darek, Sita Phal, Castor, Papaya, Mango, Guava, Datura, Duranta" as leaf sauce at 200 g per plant, dipped in cow urine (4 litres), cooked on low flame, cooled 48 hours, "mix 2.5-3 litres of solution in 200 litres of water" and spray; attribution of this last paragraph to Brahmastra rests on column layout — UHF-NF
- Datura-containing preparations are toxic; handling advice was not in the sources.

**Khatti lassi (sour buttermilk) and Dashparni ark**
- Khatti lassi: Hamirpur (HP) farmer reports "300 g bitter lassi (yoghurt drink) in 10 liters water" as a fungicide spray, most useful in winter and the rainy season — KJ-Hamirpur (farmer story in a magazine; not a PK3Y protocol; the article calls it "bitter lassi")
- UHF-NF and SPNF-HP mention Khatti Lassi as a PK3Y concoction used against fungal disease but give no preparation or dose; the CETARA-NF checklist names "Khatti Lassi, Sapt Dhan Ankur Ark, Neemastra, Agniastra/Brahmastra/Dashparni Ark" without quantities — UHF-NF; SPNF-HP
- UHF Nauni reports woolly apple aphid in a Kinnaur orchard "controlled by the spray of Dashparni ark" (apple, no dose) — UHF-NF
- Dashparni ark recipe/dilution (from commercial or NGO web pages, non-HP, not opened) **[snippet]**: about 200-500 ml per 10 litres of water (organicmandya, a commercial seller); a ten-species batch of about 24 kg leaves with cow urine 5 litre, cow dung 2 kg, water 170 litre in a 200-litre drum (INFORSE/WOTR recipe) — [organicmandya](https://www.organicmandya.com/farming/methods/dashparni-ark/), [INFORSE](https://inforse.org/evd/presentation/present_solution.php?id=130)
- A search summary suggested "5 litres sour buttermilk per 200 litres water" for khatti lassi; that figure was the search tool's recollection, not tied to any source, and is NOT used here.

**Mustard under natural farming (the only crop-specific data)**
- ICAR-NEH Umiam (acid hill soils, Meghalaya, non-HP) reports mustard under natural farming produced seed yields of 706.0, 590.6 and 442.6 kg per unit area on lower, middle and upper terraces, which was 16.5%, 18.3% and 44.3% higher than control plots; mustard and pea responded positively while maize, black gram and tomato gave lower yields than control; no pest/disease data were reported — NEH-NF
- HP: SPNF/PK3Y farmers are scored on use of Beejamrit, Jeevamrit, Ghanjeevamrit, Wapsa, mulching and concoctions; PK3Y started in 2018 and by 2023 had 1.71 million farmers, about 99% of panchayats (UHF-NF; farmer counts vary by source) — SPNF-HP; UHF-NF

### Inferences
- A defensible app message for mustard, given the evidence: use Beejamrit at sowing (seed-borne Alternaria), Jeevamrit as soil/foliar tonic, Neemastra for aphids (the only mustard-relevant pest formulation that overlaps with the neem evidence from NIPHM and Hisar), Brahmastra/Agniastra for caterpillars (DBM, hairy caterpillar), and Khatti Lassi/Dashparni as "unproven preventive" for mildews. Only neem products and garlic extract have field-trial support in mustard in the retrieved evidence; Jeevamrit, Agniastra, Brahmastra, Dashparni and Khatti Lassi have none for mustard.
- Because UHF-NF and NEH-NF differ (Neemastra with 5 kg versus 10 kg neem; different batch water volume) and NEH-NF contains apparent typos, the app should store the recipe as "as given by source X" and prefer the local PK3Y/KVK booklet when available.

### Gaps
- The official HP PK3Y training booklet or the CSK HPKV natural-farming recommendations (with Khatti Lassi and Dashparni quantities and spray dilutions) were not retrieved; spnfhp.in documents opened contained no recipes; the NITI Aayog natural farming portal page for HP could not be reached (connection refused) and the NITI toolkit returned HTTP 403.
- UHF Nauni Neemastra, Agniastra spray dilutions are not in the extracted text.
- No data on Jeevamrit/Beejamrit effect on mustard Alternaria, white rust or aphids; no trial of any PK3Y formulation on mustard in HP.
- Dashparni and lassi doses outside the Hamirpur farmer story are snippet-only or absent.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional practices (ITK)

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed or official document on traditional HP hill-farmer pest or disease practices for sarson/toria was found. The nearest HP-origin evidence is CSK HPKV's testing of locally abundant Himalayan plants (Agave, Zanthoxylum, Melia, Vitex etc.) as extracts, and one Hamirpur farmer's natural-farming story. The only documented mustard ITK survey found is from Rajasthan.

### Cited Findings
- Rajasthan (Pali district, 120 farmers, non-HP): summer ploughing 79%, green manuring 75%, ash dusting for insect pests/rodents/birds 37.50%, white flags against rodents 45.83%, burning mustard stalks in place 38.33% — Pali-ITK
- HP (Kangra): the CSK HPKV screening of 189 North-West Himalayan plant samples (2001-2004) and extract trials (2004-2007) used local plants such as Zanthoxylum alatum (timur), Eupatorium adenophorum, Cnicus wallichii, Thuja orientalis, Agave americana, Ficus roxburghii — HPKV-Kangra (research selection, not described as farmer ITK on the page)
- HP Hamirpur: farmer who started natural farming about five years before the article describes neem, guava, papaya and "about 10 other tree varieties" leaves for pest sprays, and root vegetables radish and turnip (brassicas) in his model, not mustard — KJ-Hamirpur
- HP generally: UHF-NF documents PK3Y farmers in Kinnaur, Chamba (Pangi) and others using Bijamrita, Jivamrita, Dashparni ark, Khatti Lassi on apple, peas, cabbage, tomato and other vegetables; mustard is not among the reported crops — UHF-NF

### Inferences
- The ash-dusting and stalk-burning practices recorded in Rajasthan may also exist in HP but no HP evidence was found. Stalk burning conflicts with NIPHM sanitation advice only partly (NIPHM itself advises burning sawfly-affected residues).
- HP ITK for mustard is likely to be oral and undocumented; the app should not claim any "traditional HP remedy" without a local source.

### Gaps
- No HP ITK paper on sarson, toria or gobhi sarson pest or disease management; no HP ethnobotany paper mentioning Brassica juncea as a pest-control plant.
- No documentation of traditional HP mixed-cropping with mustard.
- No HP source on use of ash, cow urine, or local plants specifically on mustard.
