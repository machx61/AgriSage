## KQ1. Which 3-5 leaf-visible problems matter per crop in HP, what do they look like, what weather favours them, and what look-alikes exist?

### Takeaway
No HP incidence data was retrieved for either crop, so the shortlist is based on national/extension descriptions plus climate logic. Recommended vision classes: **carrot** = Alternaria leaf blight, Cercospora leaf spot, powdery mildew, bacterial blight (with aphids and root-knot nematode as non-leaf or whole-plant classes); **spinach** = downy mildew, anthracnose, Cladosporium/Stemphylium leaf spots (a "round brown spot" group), leaf miner, aphids, plus caterpillar damage; white rust is a documented look-alike of downy mildew but is not described in the NIPHM spinach package. Carrot rust fly and bacterial soft rot are not leaf-diagnosable and carrot rust fly was not shown to occur in HP.

### Cited Findings

**Carrot**
- No HP-specific carrot disease survey was found. A search-summary lists a Rajasthan (Jodhpur) survey of Alternaria leaf blight (highest incidence 24.02% at Tinwari, lowest 11.74% at Balesar) **[snippet] [non-HP]**; the associated MSc thesis is on KrishiKosh — [KrishiKosh thesis, Alternaria leaf blight of carrot](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/0a8fde09-92d9-4aa4-b255-37934e6957c5)
- A survey of medicinal and aromatic plants in HP found Alternaria spp. the most prevalent cause of leaf spot and blight, followed by Phyllosticta, with Cercospora among the frequent foliar pathogens; carrot is not among those crops **[snippet]** — [AGRIS record, Major diseases of medicinal and aromatic plants recorded in HP](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dee00bb766d82b18feb333)
- **Alternaria leaf blight** (*Alternaria dauci*; *A. alternata* in the Rajasthan work): early lesions on older leaves, brown spots with a yellow margin; later dark brown to black irregular lesions on blades and petioles; leaves die when spots merge or petiole lesions develop; advanced infections look scorched; also causes seedling damping-off — UCIPM-Alt. KAU adds: spots first appear at the leaflet margin, dark brown to black and irregular; petiole/stem lesions can merge and girdle; older foliage is hit hardest in dense canopies — KAU-Alt. SKNAU-Neemcake describes small irregular dark brown to black spots along margins and tips of leaflets, greenish-brown petiole lesions that enlarge, girdle and kill the leaf.
- Weather for Alternaria: spread by air and splashing water; leaf wetness from rain or overhead irrigation favours it; "optimum temperature for growth and infection is 82°F" (about 28 °C), infection possible roughly 57-95 °F — UCIPM-Alt
- **Cercospora leaf spot** (*C. carotae*): lesions "round, grayish-brown and are more prevalent on younger foliage", versus Alternaria "irregular, dark brown to black spots which typically show up on older leaves first"; both start at leaflet margins, leaflets wither as spots multiply, and similar lesions can occur on stems and petioles — Rutgers. TNAU lists Cercospora leaf spot among carrot diseases but the opened page held only bacterial blight — TNAU-Carrot.
- Powdery mildew (called *Erysiphe polygoni* by KAU): superficial white powdery growth on stems and leaves; lower leaves first and most often; small white cottony spots spread across the leaf; heavily infected leaves turn yellow, then dry and brown, tissues may crinkle; most common in winter; favoured by high humidity, sunny days, cool nights and dew — KAU-PM. (Compare NIPHM coriander: white powdery patches on young parts; favoured by high humidity and moderate temperature, cloudy weather; most severe in shaded areas — NIPHM-Coriander.)
- Powdery mildew on carrot is more severe on drought-stressed or shaded plants, and spores do not need free water to germinate (sprinkler irrigation can even reduce severity); favoured by evening/morning humidity and 55-90 °F **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UC IPM, Carrot powdery mildew](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/carrot/powdery-mildew/)
- **Bacterial blight** (*Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *carotae*): irregular brown spots on leaves, dark brown streaks on leaf stalks (petioles), blighting of floral parts; foliar lesions start as small yellow spots whose centres dry out, becoming brittle with an irregular halo — TNAU-Carrot
- Aphids on umbellifers (NIPHM coriander, proxy for carrot): infest tender shoots and leaf undersides; curling and crinkling, stunted growth, black sooty mould from honeydew — NIPHM-Coriander
- Root-knot nematode (*Meloidogyne incognita*) on carrot: a 2023 RPCAU Pusa pot study found plants inoculated two weeks after germination more sensitive than those inoculated four weeks after germination **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [Krishi ICAR record 82352](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/82352). The nematode symptom is below ground (galled, forked roots), not leaf-visible; above-ground it only shows as stunting/yellowing (this last phrase is general knowledge, not from an opened source).

**Spinach (NIPHM-Spinach, national, not HP-specific)**
- National pest list: leaf miner (*Liriomyza trifolii*), spinach crown mite, beet armyworm (*Spodoptera exigua*), tobacco caterpillar (*S. litura*), *Helicoverpa armigera*, cutworm (*Agrotis ipsilon*), amaranthus weevil, aphid (*Aphis gossypii*); diseases: downy mildew (*Peronospora farinosa* f.sp. *spinaceae*), anthracnose (*Colletotrichum dematium*), Cladosporium leaf spot, Stemphylium leaf spot, damping off/root rot (*Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*) — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Downy mildew**: initial dull to bright yellow spots on cotyledons and leaves of all ages; later spots enlarge, brown and dry; "close inspection of the underside of the leaf often reveals the purple growth of the fungus"; extensive disease makes leaves curled, distorted and blighted. Host range spinach, *Chenopodium* weed, *Beta*. Favoured by heavy canopy of dense planting, RH >90%, high soil moisture, frequent rain; spread by wind and splashing water — NIPHM-Spinach. UC IPM adds pale green to yellow angular patches on the upper surface, older leaves first, cool damp conditions **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UC IPM, downy mildew on spinach](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/downy-mildew-on-spinach/)
- **Anthracnose**: small circular water-soaked lesions on young and old leaves; later brown, thin and papery; "tiny black fruiting bodies (acervuli) form profusely" in diseased tissue; RH >90%, high soil moisture, frequent rain; seed-borne inoculum and debris; spread by rain/sprinkler splash — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Cladosporium leaf spot**: round brownish spots rarely exceeding 0.2 inch; dark green spores/mycelium in centres, which distinguishes it from anthracnose and Stemphylium; heavy spring rain favours; seed-borne — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Stemphylium leaf spot**: small (0.1-0.2 inch) circular to oval grey-green spots turning brown, older spots coalesce, dry and become papery; "visual signs of fungal growth are generally absent"; "symptoms resemble the brownish, circular spots caused by pesticide or fertilizer toxicity"; high humidity favours; seed-borne — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Damping off/root rot**: poor germination, pre/post-emergence death, stunting, yellowed lower leaves, wilting; roots water-soaked or brown-black; severe on clay or poorly draining soils; humidity, cloudiness, temperatures below 24 °C for a few days and crowded seedlings favour — NIPHM-Spinach
- **White rust** (*Albugo occidentalis*): white pustules on the lower, sometimes upper, leaf surface and petioles, leaf top turns yellowish; "warm days followed by cool, dew-forming nights favor disease"; also infects weedy *Chenopodium*; usually minor damage — PNW-WhiteRust. Not in the NIPHM spinach package, and no HP/India spinach record of it was retrieved.
- **Cercospora leaf spot of spinach**: the NIPHM variety table lists "Cercospora leaf spot: Ooty (Sp) 1" as tolerant, but the package gives no description of the disease — NIPHM-Spinach. A US extension source says *C. beticola* survives in residue, weed hosts and seed **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [Greenlife, Cercospora leaf spot of spinach](https://www.greenlife.co.ke/cercospora-leaf-spot-of-spinach/). No India field or HP data on true spinach was retrieved.
- **Leaf miner** (*Liriomyza trifolii* in NIPHM): leaves with serpentine mines; maggot scrapes chlorophyll between epidermal layers; severe cases leaf drying and dropping; warm weather favours; pale yellow adult fly 1.5 mm; life cycle about 3 weeks — NIPHM-Spinach. The beet/spinach leaf miner *Pegomya hyoscyami* is a different fly: eggs white on leaf underside, larvae form blotch-like mines; *Liriomyza* has yellow eggs/markings **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UMD Extension, beet and spinach leafminers](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/beet-and-spinach-leafminers); [Bob Vila summary](https://www.bobvila.com/lawn-and-garden/how-to-get-rid-of-leaf-miners.md). An Indian page describes beet leafminer mines as serpentine in outer layers with leaves turning pale yellow, crumpling and drying **[snippet]** — [KAU, beetroot leafminer](https://celkau.in/crops/vegetables/beetroot/leafminer.php)
- **Aphid** (*A. gossypii*): tender shoots and leaf undersides; curling and crinkling, stunted growth, black sooty mould from honeydew — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Caterpillars**: *Helicoverpa* larvae hollow out leaf tissue and push the head in while feeding; the package also lists beet armyworm and tobacco caterpillar — NIPHM-Spinach
- **Crown mite**: damage appears as deformed leaves or small holes in expanding leaves at the heart of the plant; it is also a vector of *Pythium* and *Rhizoctonia* — NIPHM-Spinach

### Inferences
- Look-alike pairs that matter for a leaf-photo model. **Carrot**: (1) Alternaria (older/lower leaves, dark brown-black irregular, yellow halo, scorched) vs Cercospora (younger leaves, round grey-brown spots) vs bacterial blight (small yellow spots, brittle dry centres, dark petiole streaks) — all three start as brown flecks and bacterial blight is the one where a copper (or none) rather than a fungal bioagent logic applies; (2) powdery mildew (white dust, wipes off) vs Alternaria-scorched leaves late in the season. **Spinach**: (1) downy mildew (yellow patch above, purple fuzz below) vs white rust (white pustules below, yellow above; chalky not fuzzy) vs virus yellowing; (2) round brown spots: anthracnose (black acervuli, water-soaked start) vs Cladosporium (dark green sporulation) vs Stemphylium (no visible growth, resembles chemical burn) vs Cercospora; the app should ask about recent sprays/fertilizer before labelling Stemphylium; (3) leaf miner (mines) vs Stemphylium/Cercospora blotches.
- HP hill-zone timing is an inference, not a cited finding: the monsoon (July-September) is the high-risk window for the humid-weather foliar diseases (Alternaria, Cercospora, downy mildew, anthracnose, bacterial blight); cool dry winters and polyhouses favour powdery mildew; spinach downy mildew and white rust fit cool damp spring/autumn conditions.
- Carrot rust fly (*Psila rosae*) is a temperate European/North American pest; I found no record from India or HP, so it should not be a class. Carrot soft rot and root-knot are root problems, not leaf problems.

### Gaps
- No HP altitude/district incidence for any listed problem on either crop.
- No source describing carrot Cercospora or powdery mildew from HP or the NIPHM; no source on spinach Cercospora or white rust from India.
- Bacterial soft rot of carrot (root problem) was not described in any opened source (the TNAU page listed it but only bacterial blight text loaded).
- Whether *P. hyoscyami* or *L. trifolii* is the main spinach leaf miner in HP is unknown.

## KQ2. Cultural practices (sowing date, seed treatment, rotation, drainage, thinning, sanitation, varieties)

### Takeaway
The best documented spinach package is NIPHM's (raised beds more than 35 cm, line spacing 20 cm with thinning to 10-12 cm, overnight water soak, FYM 10 t/acre, guard rows, rotation with a non-host). For carrot the evidence is general: clean/indexed seed or hot-water seed treatment (50 °C, but the minutes conflict between sources: 15 vs 25), 2-year rotation, residue incorporation, lower plant density, furrow irrigation. No HP sowing-date study for either crop was retrieved.

### Cited Findings

**Carrot**
- Hot-water seed treatment: "Soak seed in hot water (122°F; 50°C) for 25 minutes. Soaking longer can damage the seed" — UCIPM-Alt; contradicted on duration by "Seed treatment with hot water at 50°C for 15 minutes is recommended" — KAU-Alt. A vegetable promotion council page for Fusarium rot likewise quotes 50 °C for 15 minutes **[snippet]** — [VFPCK package of practices](https://vfpck.org/package_of_practices.asp?ID=12). The 15 vs 25 minute conflict is unresolved; the pathogen is seed-borne (Alternaria-indexed seed is the first line).
- Rotation and residue: 2-year rotations, avoid continuous carrot and sites next to blighted fields; till or plough under residue; susceptibility differs among cultivars; furrow irrigation may reduce disease compared with sprinkler — UCIPM-Alt
- Spacing and weeds: wider spacing and lower seeding rates improve airflow so foliage dries faster; keep rows weed free; avoid fields with a history of foliar blights; for organic growers prefer blight-tolerant varieties "if available" (Bolero, Calgary, Carson named; US cultivars, not HP) — Rutgers
- Remove and destroy severely infected leaves — KAU-Alt (also practice sheet snippet from a UC page)
- Powdery mildew cultural measures: maintain plant health, rogue out infected plants — KAU-PM; also avoid drought stress, excess nitrogen and shade; 'Stefano' showed 24% leaf area affected vs 51% for 'Ricardo' in an Australian trial **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UC IPM, carrot powdery mildew](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/carrot/powdery-mildew/)
- Sowing season generalities (not HP-validated): season August-January, ridges about 45 cm apart, seeds about 10 cm apart in rows, FYM 20 t/ha basal, harvest 70-85 days after sowing, yield 20-25 t/ha **[snippet] [non-HP, Kerala body]** — [VFPCK](https://vfpck.org/package_of_practices.asp?ID=12). Another summary says early sowing can give poor germination and white, multiple roots **[snippet]** (source not opened).
- HP variety: **Solan Shresth**, a temperate carrot from Dr YS Parmar UHF Nauni, approved by the Central Variety Release Committee in November 2024, recommended for Zone IV; average root weight 255-265 g, marketable yield 225-275 q/ha; news reports say it is "resistant to common diseases and pests" without naming them **[snippet]** — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/two-vegetable-varieties-of-himachal-varsity-get-national-recognition--20241118141506); [Krishi Jagran](https://krishijagran.com/news/nauni-varsity-s-vegetable-varieties-solan-shresth-carrot-lakshami-french-bean-recognized-nationally-for-outstanding-performance/)
- Bacterial blight: no cultural measure was on the opened TNAU page (only an early copper oxychloride 0.25% spray) — TNAU-Carrot
- Rajasthan pot trial: organic amendments mixed in soil one month before sowing reduced Alternaria leaf blight (see KQ4) — SKNAU-Neemcake

**Spinach (NIPHM-Spinach)**
- Pre-sowing: deep summer ploughing; timely sowing; field sanitation and rogueing; destroy alternate hosts; rotate with a non-host crop; sow ecological-engineering plants (attractants include carrot, cowpea, mustard, buckwheat, French bean, dill, coriander-type umbellifers; repellents Ocimum, peppermint, marigold); 4 rows of sorghum/maize/bajra all around as guard crop; trap crops castor (for *S. litura*), marigold (for *H. armigera*), tomato (for leaf miner)
- Soil: "Apply FYM @ 10 t/acre and incorporate in the soil 2 to 3 weeks before sowing"; "Apply neem cake/pongamia cake @ 100 Kg/acre or press mud @ 2 t /acre" (soil-borne pathogens, nematodes, resting stages)
- Drainage: "Excessive watering and poorly drained areas of field should be avoided and use raised beds: more than 35 cm height is better for water drainage for the management of damping off"
- Sowing: "Seeds are soaked in water overnight before sowing"; "Line spacing is at 20 cm and thinning within lines at 10-12 cm"; "Irrigation at 4-5 days interval in summer and 8-10 days interval in winter"; healthy certified weed-free seed; basal 8 kg N, 16 kg P, 8 kg K per acre; top dressing 12 kg N/acre at 30 days after sowing
- Leaf miner and other pests: avoid excess nitrogen; inter-plant with beans to reduce attack
- Vegetative stage sanitation: collect and destroy crop debris; avoid waterlogging; collect and destroy diseased and infested plant parts; hand-pick caterpillars; yellow sticky traps 4-5 per acre; light trap 1 per acre, 6-10 pm; pheromone traps 4-5 per acre; bird perches 20 per acre; reflective silver mulch to deter aphids
- Anthracnose: harvest/cut leaves at regular intervals to reduce spread; reduce leaf moisture by avoiding sprinkler irrigation. Cladosporium/Stemphylium: after seed crops incorporate residues, plough volunteer plants
- Varieties: downy mildew tolerance — Tyee, Olympia, Melody, Regal; Cercospora leaf spot and aphids — Ooty (Sp) 1 (national list; availability in HP not verified)
- White rust: bury infected residue by deep ploughing, do not plant spinach in consecutive years (three-year rotations may be needed), control susceptible weeds — PNW-WhiteRust (US)
- Downy mildew (US general advice, **[snippet] [non-HP]**): raised beds and drip rather than sprinkler; a California study reported drip cut incidents four to five times vs sprinkler — [UC IPM, How to manage pests](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/PMG/GARDEN/VEGES/DISEASES/spdownymildew.html)
- CSK HPKV Palampur's opened varieties page lists no spinach or carrot varieties (it lists Palampur Green Chinese cabbage, peas, tomato, etc.) — [CSK HPKV varieties](https://hillagric.ac.in/edu/coa/vegetables/varities_developed.htm)
- UHF/KVK Solan lettuce (leafy vegetable) recommended transplanting in February, April, August and October for year-round production in Solan hills **[snippet]** — Lettuce-Solan (not spinach)

### Inferences
- Raised beds: 35 cm (NIPHM spinach) is much higher than the 15-20 cm figures in other NIPHM packages; for hill terraces a card should say "raised, well-drained bed" and quote 35 cm only as the NIPHM figure for damping-off.
- Overhead/sprinkler irrigation is disfavoured for Alternaria (carrot), spinach anthracnose and downy mildew by independent sources; a card advising morning irrigation or furrow/drip watering is well supported across them, though the HP-specific effect size is unknown.
- The 25 vs 15 minute hot-water conflict should be resolved with a certified lab/KVK before printing a number; 50 °C is common to both.

### Gaps
- No HP sowing-date study, seed rate, or thinning schedule for carrot; no HP-validated carrot variety list with disease reactions (Solan Shresth resistances unnamed).
- No UHF Nauni/CSK HPKV package-of-practices text for carrot or spinach could be opened.
- No HP spinach variety (palak) list retrieved.
- Carrot Cercospora cultural management beyond rotation/spacing/residue was not found in an opened source.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses (Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Beauveria, traps)

### Takeaway
Verified numeric doses exist for NIPHM spinach (Trichogramma 25,000 or 20,000 per acre, green lacewing 4,000 per acre, sticky/pheromone/light traps) and for Trichoderma in a coriander package (500 g in 100 kg FYM, incubated 15 days, per acre). For carrot, Trichoderma/Pseudomonas have no opened field dose: the Pusa carrot nematode pot study (Pseudomonas putida 10 g/pot) is a snippet. No Beauveria or Lecanicillium dose was found for either crop.

### Cited Findings

**Spinach (NIPHM-Spinach)**
- Trichoderma and Pseudomonas: "Apply *Trichoderma viride/harzianum* and *Pseudomonas fluorescens* as seed treatment and soil application" is recommended at sowing, but no dose is printed in the spinach package; farmer-made biopesticides need no registration, commercial ones need label claim
- Caterpillars/beet armyworm: release egg parasitoids *Trichogramma chilonis* and *T. pretiosum* @ 25,000 per acre; cutworm: release of *Trichogramma* spp. @ 20,000 per acre
- Aphid: release first-instar larvae of green lacewing (*Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi*) @ 4,000 per acre
- Traps: yellow sticky/pan traps 4-5 per acre (15 x 7.5 cm; locally, tins painted yellow and coated with grease/Vaseline/castor oil); pheromone traps 4-5 per acre for *Helicoverpa*, *S. litura*, *S. exigua*, *Agrotis*, *Hymenia*, lures changed every 2-3 weeks, one foot above canopy; light trap 1 per acre run 6-10 pm; bird perches 20 per acre; bonfire at 7-8 pm
- Conserve natural enemies: leaf miner parasitoids *Gronotoma micromorpha*, *Diglyphus*, *Halticoptera circulus*, *Opius*, *Chrysocharis*, *Neochrysocharis formosa*; aphid parasitoids *Aphidius colemani*, *Aphelinus*, *Diaeretiella*; predatory mites, *Orius*, *Oligota* for crown mite. Avoid chemical spray when 1-2 larval parasitoids are observed.
- Neem/pongamia cake as a soil amendment: 100 kg/acre (or press mud 2 t/acre) — NIPHM-Spinach

**Carrot (and Apiaceae proxy)**
- Apiaceae proxy (NIPHM coriander): "Incubate Trichoderma @ 500 g in 100 Kg FYM for 15 days prior to its application in one acre field"; Trichoderma and Pseudomonas as seed/seedling treatment endorsed without a dose; Trichogramma @ 20,000/acre — NIPHM-Coriander
- Proxy crop coriander Alternaria blight (Allahabad, UP; in-vitro and in-vivo): disease intensity under bioagents sprayed at concentrations printed as *T. viride* + *P. fluorescens* @ 5%/l 43.73, *T. viride* @ 5%/l 47.17, *P. fluorescens* @ 5%/l 46.61, neem oil @ 5%/l 64.50, with the fungicide Calibre @ 0.2% best at 32.14; the authors concluded that the bioagent combination had "a great impact" (formulation of the 5% not specified) **[proxy crop] [non-HP]** — [Ravishankar & Tiwari 2018, J. Pharmacognosy & Phytochemistry 7(6):1867-1869](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2018/vol7issue6/PartAG/7-5-578-424.pdf)
- Carrot Alternaria alternata (Jodhpur, in vitro): *Trichoderma viride* gave 77.13% inhibition of mycelial growth; none of the cultivars was resistant **[snippet] [non-HP, in vitro]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/0a8fde09-92d9-4aa4-b255-37934e6957c5)
- Carrot *M. incognita* (RPCAU Pusa pot trial): *Pseudomonas putida* at 10 g/pot most effective, followed by neem cake at 10 g/pot **[snippet] [non-HP, pot]** — [Krishi ICAR record 82352](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/82352)
- *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade ASO) foliar sprays are acceptable in certified organic carrot for Alternaria — UCIPM-Alt (no dose given); sulphur and *B. subtilis* products are the organic options for carrot powdery mildew **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UC IPM carrot powdery mildew](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/carrot/powdery-mildew/)
- Neem cake + *T. harzianum* integrated nematode work exists for tomato, mint and turmeric (turmeric: talc formulations 50 g/bed, neem cake 1 kg/bed; best result from *Pochonia chlamydosporia* 74% soil / 86% root reduction, not neem cake) **[non-HP, other crops]** — [Pervez et al. 2024, IJAgS 94(4):422-426](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/140716/54375/410575)
- Cabbage package (proxy for Brassica leafy crops): no Beauveria or Lecanicillium dose is printed; foliar neem is the only biological foliar option listed — NIPHM-Cabbage

### Inferences
- The only unit-level doses a card can safely print are the spinach Trichogramma, lacewing and trap numbers and the soil cakes (100 kg/acre). Trichoderma for carrot can borrow the NIPHM-Coriander FYM-incubation recipe only if labelled "proxy (Apiaceae)".
- Trichogramma releases suit spinach caterpillar eggs; they are not a leaf-disease measure and will not help in a small HP kitchen-garden plot; pheromone/sticky traps are more realistic there.
- Biocontrol sprays against carrot Alternaria/Cercospora or spinach downy mildew have no HP trial; the evidence is in vitro (Jodhpur), proxy crop (coriander), or foreign.

### Gaps
- No dose for Beauveria, Lecanicillium, Metarhizium, *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (spray or seed) or *Bacillus subtilis* on carrot or spinach in any opened source.
- No HP field trial of any bioagent on carrot or spinach.
- Seed-treatment dose for Trichoderma on carrot or spinach was not retrieved (NIPHM-Spinach says "apply" without a number).
- The lettuce/Pusa/Jodhpur/Allahabad items are snippet or proxy-crop evidence and should not be presented as carrot-in-HP results.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
NIPHM-Spinach gives NSKE 4%, neem oil 5 ml/L plus 0.5 ml/L sticker, and a tobacco decoction (1 kg in 10 L boiled 30 min, made up to 30 L plus 100 g soap) for aphids. For carrot the only concentration-bearing non-chemical item is soil-applied neem cake at 2.5 t/ha (37.61% disease control in a pot trial), and mineral sulphur appears only in chemical-recommendation tables. For downy mildew of spinach, anthracnose, Cercospora and white rust, no botanical with a verified concentration was found: say so.

### Cited Findings

**Spinach (NIPHM-Spinach)**
- Leaf miner (sowing stage): "Spray NSKE 4%"; vegetative stage: "Spray neem oil @ 5 ml/l of water + 0.5 ml/l sticker"
- Caterpillars/beet armyworm: "Spraying NSKE 4% against eggs and first instar larva"
- Aphid: "Spraying with tobacco decoction (1 Kg tobacco boiled in 10 l of water for 30 minutes and making up to 30 l + 100 g soap)"; prune out infested leaves if few; reflective silver mulch
- Downy mildew, anthracnose, Cladosporium/Stemphylium, damping off: the package lists only cultural measures plus Trichoderma/Pseudomonas; no botanical or other spray is recommended for any of them
- NSKE 5% and neem oil are also "usually the best method of control" for whitefly/aphids on coriander — NIPHM-Coriander; NSKE 5% or azadirachtin 0.03% neem oil based WSP @ 1000-2000 ml in 200-400 L water/acre for diamondback moth on cabbage — NIPHM-Cabbage (proxy crops; NSKE concentration differs from the 4% in the spinach package)
- Beet leaf miner (India, **[snippet]**): a spray of neem oil, garlic paste and bar soap in water is advised (no concentrations in the summary), plus destroying fallen leaves and debris — KAU beetroot leaf miner page. US sources: spinosad works best, neem oil can prevent egg-laying but is less effective, row covers keep egg-laying females off **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/beet-and-spinach-leafminers); [Colorado State](https://agsci.colostate.edu/agbio/?p=47975)
- Neem 5% reduced mustard white rust (*Albugo candida*) by about 47.6% (vs about 43% for Ridomil MZ 2 g/L) and *Trichoderma viride* by 63.4%, in an Indian in-vivo study **[snippet] [proxy crop] [non-HP]** — [Krishi ICAR record 76934 / related listing](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/76934). This is mustard, not spinach.
- Spinach downy mildew in organic production: an industry report states that there is no known solution for organic spinach, so control rests on prevention **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [Growing Produce](https://www.growingproduce.com/?p=61192). White rust: copper offers "limited control" / "poor control as a stand-alone product" — PNW-WhiteRust.

**Carrot**
- Neem cake soil amendment (Rajasthan, pot, carrot, *Alternaria alternata*): mixed in soil one month before sowing; neem cake 2.5 t/ha gave disease intensity 35.72% (37.61% control over untreated 57.25%); castor cake 2.5 t/ha 38.68% (32.44%); mustard cake 2.5 t/ha 40.05% (30.04%); cotton cake 2.5 t/ha 42.50% (25.71%); poultry manure 5.0 t/ha 48.00% (16.16%); FYM 15.0 t/ha 50.05% (12.58%); recorded 55 days after sowing; cage-house pot test, three replications, CD (P=0.05) 4.64 [non-HP] — SKNAU-Neemcake. Note: disease intensity after neem cake was still 35.7%, i.e. a partial effect.
- Powdery mildew sulphur: the KAU carrot table gives "Wettable sulphur 80 WP, 2 g per litre of water, 20 g per 10 L knapsack, 400 g per acre in 200 L" under chemical control and no organic option — KAU-PM [non-HP]. NIPHM-Coriander lists "Sulphur 85% DP @ 6-8 kg/acre" for coriander powdery mildew under chemical control [proxy crop]. Sulphur is a mineral generally acceptable in organic systems, but whether to list it in a "natural" app is a product decision. A Croatian trial found sulphur the weakest of the tested fungicides on carrot powdery mildew **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [UC IPM carrot powdery mildew summary](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/carrot/powdery-mildew/)
- Bacterial blight: "Spraying early with Copper oxychloride 0.25 %" (the only management on the TNAU page; copper is a mineral fungicide/bactericide, often allowed in organic standards with limits) — TNAU-Carrot
- Alternaria: KAU page lists no organic or biological product (copper oxychloride 2 g/L etc. are chemical entries) — KAU-Alt; UC IPM states *B. subtilis* products are organic-acceptable — UCIPM-Alt
- Milk/whey: no carrot or spinach data. The only quantitative milk data are for cucurbits (milk ≥10% twice weekly controlled powdery mildew in a Brazilian greenhouse) **[snippet] [non-HP, other crop]** — [agrar.de Bettiol summary](https://www.agrar.de/agenda/bettiol.htm), with a contrary view in [Vegalab](https://www.vegalab.com/crop-science/knowledge/myths/milk-spray) — not transferable to carrot without a trial.
- Baking soda: a UK garden guide suggests 1 teaspoon per litre for spinach downy mildew **[snippet] [non-HP, home-garden advice, unvalidated]** — [Suttons garden planner](https://gardenplanner.suttons.co.uk/plant-diseases/uk-and-europe/spinach-downy-mildew/)

### Inferences
- Botanical sprays have no verified activity on any of the four main fungal leaf diseases of these crops (carrot Alternaria/Cercospora, spinach downy mildew/anthracnose); the honest card text is "prevention, resistant variety and sanitation; botanicals are not shown to work".
- Aphids and leaf miner on spinach are the cases where NSKE 4% and neem oil 5 ml/L have a printed NIPHM recommendation; neem cake in soil is the one carrot-disease input with a number, though from a Rajasthan pot trial.
- The tobacco decoction in NIPHM-Spinach is a leaf crop eaten fresh; nicotine residues and the Solanaceae-virus carry-over concern (documented in the capsicum note) argue for not featuring it on a food leaf crop; this is my judgement, not a cited finding.

### Gaps
- No concentration for garlic, chilli, karanj, whey, soap or baking-soda sprays validated on carrot or spinach.
- NSKE preparation recipe (kernel kg per litre) not in an opened source; 4% (spinach) vs 5% (other NIPHM packages) is an unresolved discrepancy.
- Percent efficacy of NSKE or neem oil on spinach leaf miner or aphids not given in the package.
- No organic option with efficacy data for carrot Cercospora or spinach downy mildew, anthracnose, Cercospora or white rust.
- Whether sulphur dust/spray is phytotoxic or acceptable on spinach (edible leaf) was not addressed.

## KQ5. Natural-farming formulations (PK3Y/SPNF) with doses for these crops

### Takeaway
Recipes for Beejamrit, Jeevamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra and Brahmastra are documented by UHF Nauni (HP) and ICAR-NEH (non-HP), and dilutions are inconsistent between sources. **No source gave a crop-specific dose for carrot or spinach, nor any efficacy measure against their leaf diseases.** The HP evidence is a farmer's statement that Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi controlled fungal diseases on a farm that includes carrot, and a Nauni lettuce trial using 3% sour buttermilk (a nutrition trial).

### Cited Findings

**HP (UHF Nauni, Indian Farming 2023, UHF-NF)**
- Bijamrita: 5 kg cow dung in a cloth tied and soaked; lime water added; spread over seeds, mixed by hand, dried well and sown; the article says it is "equally effective in controlling seed borne diseases" (no data)
- Jivamrita: 10 kg cow dung + 10 litres cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg pulse flour + a handful of bund soil; described as preventing "fungal and bacterial plant diseases" (no data)
- Neemastra: 100 litres water, 10 litres cow urine, 2 kg cow dung, 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves; cover with gunny bag in shade 48 h; stir clockwise 1 minute morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars (no spray dilution stated)
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves + 500 g tobacco powder + 500 g green chilli chutney + 250 g garlic chutney in 20 litres cow urine, boiled on low flame under a lid, then 48 h with stirring; for sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (no spray dilution stated)
- Brahmastra (1 acre): any 5 plants (Darek, Sita Phal, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta etc.) at 200 g leaf paste per plant in 4 litres cow urine, heated to a boil, cooled 48 hours, then "Mix 2.5-3 litres of solution in 200 litres of water and spray it on the crop"
- Farmer testimony: Ganga (Shimla, trained by Palekar at Kufri in 2018) practises natural farming on apple, peas, carrot, cucumber, French bean, coriander and radish; "She noticed that Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases" (anecdote, no measurement, crop not specified); Atma Ram (Kinnaur) reports woolly apple aphid on apple "controlled by the spray of Dashparni ark" — UHF-NF
- Khatti Lassi and Dashparni: the UHF article gives no recipe or dilution for either

**HP experimental**
- Leaf lettuce, Solan (UHF Nauni/KVK Solan): best treatment Ghanjeevamrit 1 t/ha + Jeevamrit drench 15% + Saptadhanyankur 3% + sour buttermilk 3% (yield, quality and net returns; no pest/disease observations on the page) — Lettuce-Solan. Not carrot/spinach but the nearest HP leafy-vegetable trial.
- Tomato, UHF Nauni: Ghanjeevamrit 1.5 t/ha with 15% jeevamrit spray gave highest yield **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh listing](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/4eaa9e68-017c-4e1f-b5f8-941c0439ed06)
- Cutworm (*Agrotis ipsilon*; off-season vegetables in HP; lab): Dashparni ark had the highest ovicidal action; direct larval mortality was low to moderate in early instars and very low in later instars at the "recommended 10% concentration"; a CSK HPKV Palampur thesis ranked toxicity Dashparni ark (LC50 8.28, 9.82, 11.69% for 1st-3rd instar) > Brahmastra > Agniastra > Neemastra **[snippet]** — [Springer, Int. J. Tropical Insect Science 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42690-023-00966-z); [KrishiKosh thesis, bioformulations against Agrotis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/57d75274-6368-4820-8762-3d079f7e3218). Relevant to cutworm in spinach/carrot seedlings only as a lab lead.
- CSK HPKV Palampur review: "10% of 15-day seasoned cow urine can be used as a protection to all crops" **[snippet]** — [NAHEP KMS, Cow based bioformulations for pest management](https://nahep.icar.gov.in/KMS/UploadKC1/b06b7c48-1f85-4697-a602-6b0f34c7fa74/Cow%20based%20bioformulations%20for%20pest%20management.pdf) (page unreachable in this session; claim from search summary)

**Non-HP recipes with dilutions (ICAR-NEH Meghalaya 2025, NEH-NF; note differences from UHF)**
- Beejamrit: fresh cow dung 200 g, cow urine 200 ml, lime 2 g, water 800 ml, soil 8 g; seeds soaked about one hour, shade dried; seedlings dipped 1-2 h before transplanting; printed as "seed treatment at the rate of 25 L/kg of seed" (this quantity looks implausible and is quoted as printed)
- Jeevamrit: water 200 L, fresh cow dung 10 kg, cow urine 5-10 L, jaggery 2 kg, pulse flour 2 kg, handful of soil, 48 h; storable up to 15 days; applied with irrigation and foliar @ 200 L/acre at fortnightly intervals
- Neemastra: 200 L water, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg local cow dung, 10 kg neem leaf paste or neem seed pulp; 48 h; stored up to 6 months; "it should not be diluted with water" and is applied directly; spray at 3% concentration for jassids, aphids, whiteflies, small caterpillars, mealybugs (the source says both "should not be diluted" and "foliar spray at 3%" — internally inconsistent)
- Brahmastra: 20 L cow urine + 2 kg each neem, karanj, custard apple, castor, datura leaf paste, boil with foaming, cool 48 h; storable 6 months; "3% concentration by diluting 6-8 L of Brahmastra in 200 L of water"; or 100 L water + 3 L; 15 L + 500 ml; 10 L + 300 ml
- Agniastra: 200 L cow urine (the HP article uses 20 L) with 2 kg neem leaf paste, 500 g tobacco powder, 500 g green chilli paste, 250 g garlic paste and 200 g turmeric powder; stored up to 3 months; "2% concentration, applied at the rate of 200 L/acre. For spraying, 6-8 L of agniastra is diluted in 200 L of water" (6-8 L in 200 L is 3-4%, not 2%; the source's own small-batch table lists 3 L per 100 L)
- TNAU variants for comparison (from the sibling capsicum note, not re-opened here): Neemastra diluted to 100 L per acre; Brahmastra 2-2.5 L diluted to 100 L per acre — [TNAU Agritech organic plant protection](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_ofk_pltprotection.html)

### Inferences
- Dilutions conflict across sources (Neemastra "undiluted" vs 3% vs 100 L/acre; Agniastra 2% vs 3-4%). A card should show a spray strength only with its source and flag it as unverified for carrot/spinach; the safest wording for leafy vegetables is "start at the lowest listed strength (3% Brahmastra) on a few plants first". This precaution is my own and not a cited recommendation.
- For fungal diseases, the only HP support is farmer anecdote (Jivamrita + Khatti Lassi); Neemastra/Agniastra/Brahmastra are labelled by the sources for insects, not for leaf spots or mildews.
- Agniastra contains tobacco powder; as a precaution on an edible leaf crop it is better to leave it off spinach cards (judgement, not cited). Nicotine residue on fresh leaves at harvest is not discussed in any source I opened.

### Gaps
- PK3Y/SPNF official (spnfhp.nic.in) doses for Khatti Lassi, Dashparni, Sonthastra and per-bigha Jeevamrit schedules for carrot/spinach: not retrieved (the site and NITI HP webinar proceedings were unreachable/ECONNREFUSED).
- No HP trial quantifying any natural-farming input against carrot Alternaria/Cercospora/powdery mildew or spinach downy mildew/anthracnose/leaf miner/aphids.
- Khatti lassi: 3% (Lettuce-Solan) is a nutrition-trial dose; the NAHEP summary of 10-20x dilution of 15-20 day old buttermilk comes from the sibling note and was a snippet there too.
- Dashparni recipe and spray strength: an NGO page quotes 3 L in 100 L (3%) and another site 200-500 ml per 10 L; neither is an HP official source and neither was opened by me, so no dose is stated here.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional practices

### Takeaway
Documentation for carrot or spinach in HP is essentially absent. What exists is a Kullu farmer's cow-urine plus two local plants spray for cabbage/cauliflower/vegetables (validated on okra at BHU), the Kinnaur Nako-village Neemastra method, and general cow-urine and ash practices from other regions.

### Cited Findings
- Kullu, HP (Kishan Singh, Consolation Award 2019): powdered roots and leaves of two local plants (names withheld for IP reasons) with cow urine, used against insects and fungus in cabbage, cauliflower and other vegetables; effective at low volumes; storable in cool dry place for 5-6 months. BHU validation on **okra** (not carrot/spinach): 67.2% and 69.6% mite reduction (first and second sprays), 59.63% shoot borer reduction, 58.1% and 64.55% fruit borer reduction, 42.1% and 47.1% whitefly reduction, 47.86% leafhopper reduction, 78.4% yield increase "at farmer's dose" (dose not stated) — NIF-Kullu
- Kinnaur (Nako village): Neemastra prepared with 100 L water, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg cow dung, 5 kg neem twigs with leaves — UHF-NF
- Ash is used by tribal farmers against pumpkin beetles and aphids **[snippet] [non-HP]** — [J. Pharmacognosy & Phytochemistry 2020 SP9(2)](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020/vol9issue2S/PartC/S-9-2-44-884.pdf); wood ash dusting on nursery seedlings acts as an insect repellent **[snippet]** — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_tomato_nursery.html)
- Farmers' natural-farming practice with carrot in Shimla (Ganga: carrot, peas, apple under SPNF/PK3Y) — UHF-NF; no carrot-specific input or yield data given
- Kullu farmer's practice and Dashparni results (see KQ5) are the only HP-labelled pest-related items found

### Inferences
- For carrot and spinach an "HP traditional practice" card is not supportable from documented evidence; a generic "cow urine + local bitter plants" card can cite NIF-Kullu but cannot give plant names or dilution.
- Pine-needle mulch, ash dusting, hand-picking and buttermilk are plausible HP practices but nothing I opened ties them to these two crops in HP.

### Gaps
- No Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge or Himachal Journal of Agricultural Research paper on carrot/spinach plant protection was retrieved.
- The two plants in the Kullu spray and the dilution are unknown ("at farmer's dose" only).
- No documentation of buttermilk, ash or pine-needle practice on carrot/spinach in HP.
