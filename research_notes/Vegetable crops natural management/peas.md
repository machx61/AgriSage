## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter in HP pea, what do they look like, and what weather/altitude favours them?

### Takeaway
Recommended vision classes for HP pea: (1) powdery mildew (most important foliar disease in HP; hot spot at high-hill Kukumseri), (2) rust, (3) Ascochyta blight/leaf spot (the Himalayan-region disease in the NIPHM list), (4) pea leaf miner (best-documented HP pest, with HP loss data), (5) pea aphid; plus whole-plant classes root rot/Fusarium wilt/white rot (documented as serious in the dry temperate zone) and pod borer on pods. Downy mildew is listed in the national package only for the Indo-Gangetic plains, so it is a lower-priority class for HP hills.

### Cited Findings

**Regional significance (national package)**
- Pests of national significance: pod borers (*Etiella zinckenella*, *Helicoverpa armigera*), stem fly (*Ophiomyia phaseoli*), pea weevil, powdery mildew (*Erysiphe pisi*), rust, root-knot nematode. Pests of regional significance: pea aphid (*Acyrthosiphon pisum*), leaf miner (*Chromatomyia horticola*), downy mildew (Indo-Gangetic plains), Ascochyta blight (Himalayan Region, Chhattisgarh), white rot *Sclerotinia sclerotiorum* (J&K, Himachal Pradesh), root rot *Fusarium solani*/*Rhizoctonia* (UP, Bihar, WB), Fusarium wilt *F. oxysporum* f. sp. *pisi* (UP, Uttarakhand, HP, J&K) — NIPHM-Pea
- HP pea geography and seasons: pea is grown in Shimla, Chamba, Kullu, Mandi, Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti; HP is the 5th largest pea producer (5.79% of national output); high-hill farmers sell off-season pea (sowing Apr-May, harvest Sep-Oct); low/mid hills sow Oct-Nov, harvest Jan-Feb — UHF-Pea-Econ

**Powdery mildew (Erysiphe pisi)**
- Symptoms: attacks leaves first, faint slightly discoloured specks from which grey-white powdery mycelium develops; spreads over leaf, stem and pod; leaves turn yellow and die; pods do not set or stay small; defoliation; later the pods are covered — NIPHM-Pea
- Early sign: small sporadic light-coloured dots on the upper surface of the oldest and lowest leaves, which become white powdery colonies; conidia mostly on the upper leaf surface; typically appears late in the season and peaks at pod formation; late-sown or late-maturing crops suffer most; rain washes conidia off so wet weather does not favour it — [Kumari 2024, PM-Mandi intro](https://www.plantarchives.org/article/228-Management%20of%20Powdery%20Mildew%20(Erysiphe%20pisi)%20of%20Pea%20using%20Fungicides.pdf)
- Weather: warm (15-25 C), humid (>70% RH) conditions for 4-5 days late in the season during flowering and pod filling favour development; spores spread by air in dry conditions — NIPHM-Pea. Disease is more severe with warm dry days and cool nights — PM-Palampur
- HP evidence: Palampur trials (winters 2017-18, 2018-19) plus a summer 2018 trial at Kukumseri (Lahaul, described as a disease hot spot); disease progressed more severely at Kukumseri, attributed to favourable weather and possibly more virulent races; NW Indian Himalaya described as a natural hot spot; yield loss cited 25-50% — PM-Palampur
- A Mandi (HP) paper calls it "one of the fungus foliar diseases that is most prevalent in the Himachal Pradesh"; 30-40% annual production loss and up to 70% yield loss are quoted there from the literature (not HP survey data) — PM-Mandi
- Untreated control severity in the Mandi rabi 2023-24 trial (var. Goldy): 57.77% disease index — PM-Mandi

**Rust**
- Symptoms: many small orange-brown pustules usually on the lower leaf surface; severely infected leaves wither and drop; larger pustules on stems, isolated pustules on pods; reduced seed size. Frequent rain, plentiful dews and 20-25 C favour it; dry hot weather restrains it. Alternate-host and residue sources (Euphorbia, infected residues); not seed-borne — NIPHM-Pea
- Review detail: aecial pustules creamy-white to bright orange on the underside of older leaves, released spores look like yellow powder; uredial pustules powdery light brown mostly on stems in the Indian subcontinent; warm humid weather favours *U. viciae-fabae*, cool less humid conditions *U. pisi*; yield loss 57-100% (*U. viciae-fabae*) or up to 30% (*U. pisi*) — Rust-review (non-HP; the portion of the page I read did not mention HP, whereas a search summary of the same review claimed HP and Uttarakhand were listed; unresolved conflict)
- No HP rust incidence figure found.

**Ascochyta blight / leaf and pod spot**
- Early symptoms under the canopy on lower leaves, stems, tendrils: small purplish-brown irregular flecks that enlarge and coalesce until lower leaves are blighted; girdling at the soil line (foot rot) with purplish-black lesions; pod lesions under prolonged moisture or lodging; seed- and debris-borne; ascospores wind-borne, conidia rain-splashed; warm humid 15-25 C — NIPHM-Pea
- HP: "New blight of pea from Himachal Pradesh" (Indian Phytopathology 2002) exists, only the title was seen **[snippet]** — [IPPJ 19636](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/19636). A Himachal agricultural university inheritance study used the resistant cultivar Kinnauri as a parent **[snippet]**, source link not retained.

**Downy mildew**
- Grey-white mould on the lower leaf surface with a yellowish area on the opposite side; leaves yellow and die when cool and damp; brown pod blotches; favoured by high humidity and 5-15 C for a few days; primary infection by soil, seed and water — NIPHM-Pea

**Pea leaf miner (Chromatomyia horticola)**
- Damage: serpentine mines between the epidermal layers, leaf drying and dropping in severe cases; warm weather favourable; adult is a 1.5 mm pale yellowish fly; life cycle about 3 weeks; egg hatches in 4 days, larva about 7 days, pupa about 7 days — NIPHM-Pea
- HP loss data: severe infestation can cause up to 90% crop damage; in HP more than 20% avoidable loss in pea grain yield beyond 40% infestation (Mehta et al. 1994, cited within) — Thakur-2017
- HP parasitoids recorded on pea leaf miner at Palampur trial: *Diglyphus* sp. and *Opius* sp.; parasitism 15.31-20.04% in non-chemical treatments vs 2.09-6.61% in synthetic insecticide treatments — Thakur-2017

**Pea aphid**
- Nymphs and adults suck sap from young shoots, underside of tender leaves, inflorescences; curling and distortion of leaves, stunting, pale and drying leaves; honeydew and sooty mould; about a week per generation; parasitoids *Aphidius*, *Diaeretiella rapae*; predators lacewing, ladybirds, syrphids — NIPHM-Pea

**Pod borers and stem fly**
- *Helicoverpa*: early feeding on foliage (sometimes serious defoliation), later bores pods with head inside and body outside; *Etiella*: flower and young pod drop, frass-filled soft rotten patches, brown entry spot on older pods — NIPHM-Pea
- Stem fly: maggots bore into the stem causing withering and drying; adults puncture leaves and injured parts turn yellow; worse on seedlings — NIPHM-Pea
- Pea weevil (*Bruchus pisorum*) larvae are inside the seed and found only when seed is mature — NIPHM-Pea (stored seed problem, not a leaf class)

**Root rot, Fusarium wilt, white rot (whole-plant / stem)**
- Wilt: most pronounced in 3-5 week old plants; cotyledons droop; yellowing of lower leaves, stunting; xylem brown to orange-brown; leaflet margins curl downward and inward; roots look healthy externally; soil 23-27 C favours — NIPHM-Pea
- Root rot: reddish-brown to black streaks on roots, girdled lower stem, red vascular discolouration near the cotyledons, stunting, basal yellowing; cool wet weather, soil 25-30 C — NIPHM-Pea
- White rot (*Sclerotinia*): dry discoloured spot at the infection point (often on stem, starting from fallen petals at flowering), girdles the stem, plant above wilts, tissue whitish and shredded; hard sclerotia survive in soil and debris; moisture and 4-20 C, light needed for apothecia — NIPHM-Pea
- Dry-temperate NW Himalaya survey, Kharif 2006-2013: root rot/wilt complex (*F. oxysporum* f. sp. *pisi*, *F. solani* f. sp. *pisi*, *Rhizoctonia solani*) moderate to high; white rot low to moderate; average incidence 34.0% root rot and 8.4% white rot over eight years **[snippet]** — [IPPJ 54620](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/54620) (page itself behind login)
- CSK HPKV Kukumseri (Lahaul valley) ran a project 2006-2009 on "integrated management of root rot/wilt complex disease of green pea" (no results on page); another KVK/HAREC project note says "Existing pea crop is facing root rot complex so it can be replaced by capsicum & tomato" — [HAREC Kukumseri accomplished projects](https://hillagric.ac.in/research/dr/res_network/rss/harec_kukumseri/accomplished_projects.htm)

**Nutrient and look-alike disorders (leaf photos that are NOT disease)**
- N deficiency: whole plant pale, lower leaves yellow then white/brown; Mg: central interveinal chlorosis with green marginal band on older leaves; Mn: yellow-white leaves with green veins on new growth ("grey speck", marsh spot in seed); Fe: interveinal yellowing; K: dark green leaves with marginal yellowing/fining of lower leaves and downward-cupped leaflets; S: young leaves including veins yellow; B toxicity: tip/margin chlorosis and necrosis on older leaves — NIPHM-Pea

### Inferences
- Look-alike pairs for the vision model: (1) powdery mildew (white powder, mostly upper surface, starts on old lower leaves) vs downy mildew (grey mould on the underside, yellow patch on top) vs rust (orange-brown pustules on underside, yellow-powdery spores that can be mistaken for mildew); ask for an underside photo. (2) Ascochyta flecks (purplish-brown, under canopy, lower leaves) vs rust vs Mn deficiency. (3) Leaf-miner mines (serpentine tunnels) vs stem-fly adult puncture yellowing. (4) Aphid-curled leaves vs virus vs herbicide drift (the NIPHM package describes no pea virus). (5) Fusarium wilt (yellowing lower leaves, downward-inward leaflet curl, brown xylem, healthy-looking roots) vs root rot (dark streaked roots) vs white rot (shredded whitish stem, cottony) vs drought. Wilt, root rot, white rot, pod borer and weevil need a whole-plant, root or pod photo, not a leaf photo.
- Altitude pattern, with caution: the one HP pair of sites (Palampur mid-hill vs Kukumseri high-hill) shows more powdery mildew at the high-hill dry site with warm days and cool nights; the dry-temperate zone also carries 34% average root rot. Rust (20-25 C, dews, rain) and Ascochyta (15-25 C, humid) should be more of a mid/low-hill and monsoon-influenced risk. This is inferred from the weather ranges, not from HP survey data.
- Off-season (Apr-May sown, Sep-Oct harvest) high-hill crop runs through the monsoon, which raises Ascochyta/rust/white rot risk; Oct-Nov sown low/mid-hill crop matures Jan-Feb into the cool dry season where powdery mildew and leaf miner (March) peak. Inference only.

### Gaps
- No HP survey of incidence by district/altitude for rust, Ascochyta, aphid or pod borer was found.
- Pea viruses (enation, bean yellow mosaic) are not in the NIPHM package and no HP source was retrieved.
- IPPJ abstracts are behind a login; the root-rot/white-rot figures are summary-level only.

## KQ2. Cultural practices: seed treatment, sowing date, rotation, resistant varieties, drainage, sanitation

### Takeaway
For HP pea the strongest natural lever is host resistance and sowing date (powdery mildew), then sanitation and rotation (rust, Ascochyta, soil-borne rots). Palam Sumool is the only named HP-bred pea with a documented powdery mildew resistant reaction; Palam Priya, Pb-89 are only moderately susceptible and Azad P-1 is susceptible. Resistance to rust, Ascochyta and wilt in HP-released varieties was not found.

### Cited Findings

**Sowing date and zone**
- Low and mid hills sow Oct-Nov (harvest Jan-Feb); mid-hills wet temperate and high-hill dry temperate sow Apr-May (harvest Sep-Oct) — UHF-Pea-Econ
- Palampur: sowing at end of October ('Pb-89') and end Oct to first week of Nov ('Palam Priya') gave the highest seed yield; sowing on 31 Oct raised seed yield by 13% and 41% over 10 Nov and 21 Nov; 'Palam Priya' out-yielded 'Azad P-1' and 'Arkel' by 38% and 113% **[snippet]** — [Legume Research 37(3):287-293](https://www.arccjournals.com/journal/legume-research-an-international-journal/LR-2903) (attribution of this summary to this exact link is not certain)
- Late sowing and late-maturing varieties suffer more powdery mildew — PM-Palampur; PM-Mandi
- Timely sowing, line sowing, optimum plant population, certified weed-free seed, avoid late sowing — NIPHM-Pea

**Resistant and tolerant varieties**
- National list (as printed; mapping of names to each disease is ambiguous in the PDF text): powdery mildew/rust resistant or tolerant names listed: Rachna, Pant P5, DMR 11, HUP 2, JP 885, KFP 103, Ambika, Shubhra, Aparna, Azad P4, Pusa Panna, Hans, Type 163. Additional PM-resistant dwarfs: HFP 4 (Aparna), DDR 13, HFP 8909, KPMR 114-1, Malviya Matar 15 (also rust resistant) — NIPHM-Pea. NHZ (North Hill Zone) is defined as J&K and the hilly areas of HP and UP, but the package lists no variety specific to NHZ.
- Palampur 48-genotype screen (2017-19): resistant check Palam Sumool; susceptible check Azad P-1; moderately susceptible Palam Priya and Pb-89; ten breeding lines resistant (SP-7, SN-1, SN-6-1, SN-7-1, SN-2, SN-5-2, SN-6-2, SN-10, SN-21, SP-28-1), 27 moderately resistant incl. SP-3, SP-6, SP-22 (breeding lines, not released varieties) — PM-Palampur
- Him Palam Matar-1 (DPP-SP-22, Palam Sumool x Palam Priya): moderately resistant to powdery mildew, suitable for main-season cultivation in low, mid and high hills of HP; Him Palam Matar-2 described as first pea variety with the er2 gene and complete powdery mildew resistance **[snippet]** — search summary of CSK HPKV material, source link not retained
- DPP-68 (CSK HPKV) showed slow disease development and longer incubation than susceptible Lincoln **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh evaluation record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/a1b48eef-9497-4569-9e10-00a2ec8096e0)
- CSK HPKV Palampur trial 2015-17, seven mildew-resistant lines vs checks (Azad Pea-1, Lincoln, Punjab-89, Palam Priya) under conventional and natural farming: Line 1-2-SPS5 resistant and top yielder under both (211.50 q/ha conventional vs 78.00 q/ha natural farming; Line 1-2-SPS11 206.00 vs 72.33 q/ha) **[snippet, abstract]** — [KrishiKosh](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/a1b48eef-9497-4569-9e10-00a2ec8096e0/full)
- Kinnauri used as an Ascochyta-resistant parent in an HP inheritance study **[snippet]** — see KQ1.

**Seed and soil treatment (cultural parts)**
- Seed treatment with Rhizobium cultures at 250 g/acre; starter 10 kg N + 28 kg P2O5 + 25 kg K2O/acre at sowing — NIPHM-Pea
- Apply well-decomposed FYM at 8-10 t/acre treated with Trichoderma at field preparation (the Trichoderma quantity is not printed) — NIPHM-Pea
- Deep summer ploughing (three ploughings at 10-day interval reduces pest population); summer ploughing then leave 15 days; solarisation: after light irrigation cover with transparent polyethylene for 25 days; or cover beds with transparent polythene "100 gauge" for three weeks before sowing — NIPHM-Pea
- Crop rotation with non-host crops; rotate with non-leguminous crops and destroy diseased debris after harvest (rust); mixed cropping — NIPHM-Pea

**Sanitation and cultural controls by problem (NIPHM-Pea)**
- Powdery mildew entry: use resistant varieties, burn infected pea stubble soon after harvest where practicable, avoid sowing pea next to last season's stubble, control volunteer peas, adopt the bower (staking) system.
- Rust entry: destroy all diseased plant debris after harvest; rotate with non-leguminous crops; mixed cropping.
- Entries for the remaining regional diseases (downy mildew, Ascochyta, white rot, root rot, Fusarium wilt; the row-to-entry mapping was lost in the PDF text): remove and burn diseased plants soon after detection; plant a tall crop as a barrier against air-borne inoculum; follow common cultural practices; keep wider spacing and maintain irrigation.
- Leaf miner: remove and destroy leaves with mines and blotches; yellow sticky traps/cards for adults; tomato or marigold as trap crop.
- Aphid: judicious nitrogen; barrier crops such as mustard around the field; tall border crops such as maize, sorghum or millet.
- Stem fly: mulch with rice straw; balanced N and P.
- Pod borer: deep ploughing kills diapausing pupae; hand-pick caterpillars and pupae early; collect and destroy infested pods.
- Pea weevil: early harvest; cut volunteer plants and weeds; sanitise the godown; disinfect gunny bags.
- General: avoid water stagnation; irrigate at critical stages; one to two hand weedings at 25-30 and/or 45 days after sowing; collect and destroy diseased and insect-infested plant parts. Ecological engineering: border/attractant plants (cowpea, buckwheat, carrot, sunflower, mustard, maize, coriander-type flowers), leave nectar weeds, avoid broad-spectrum sprays when pest:defender ratio is favourable (general rule 2:1) — NIPHM-Pea
- Crop-change option used in Lahaul: where pea root rot built up, replacing pea with capsicum/tomato was suggested (2005-2008 project note) — HAREC Kukumseri (link above)

### Inferences
- Sowing date is the cheapest powdery mildew lever supported by HP data: sow at the zone's recommended window and avoid late sowing and late-maturing varieties.
- For a card on powdery mildew resistance, only Palam Sumool (resistant check) and Him Palam Matar-1 (moderately resistant, HP-released) are HP-linked; the national names (Rachna, Pant P5 etc.) must be checked for HP seed availability.
- Pea is a legume: rotation with non-legumes (cereals, brassicas, potatoes) is consistent with the NIPHM "non-leguminous" advice; the exact gap in years for HP was not found.

### Gaps
- No HP-released variety with documented rust, Ascochyta or Fusarium wilt resistance was found.
- UHF Nauni/CSK HPKV package of practices (official variety, seed rate, spacing and seed-treatment lists) could not be opened.
- No HP data on mulch, bower height or drainage design for pea.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
Very little pea-specific biological-control dosing exists. Verified numbers: first-instar green lacewing 4,000/acre for aphid, Rhizobium 250 g/acre, pheromone traps 4-5/acre, light trap 1/acre, yellow sticky traps 4-5/acre, bird perches 20/acre (all NIPHM-Pea). Trichoderma seed-treatment results come from non-HP trials. The NIPHM pea package gives no Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Trichogramma, Beauveria or Bt dose.

### Cited Findings

**Soil and seed bioagents**
- NIPHM-Pea endorses *T. harzianum/viride* and *Pseudomonas fluorescens* for seed treatment and soil application and notes farmer-made biopesticides for own use need no registration, but prints no dose; FYM 8-10 t/acre "treated with Trichoderma" — NIPHM-Pea
- Kumaon hills (Uttarakhand, non-HP), pea: seed treatments *T. viride*, *T. harzianum*, *P. fluorescens*, *T. harzianum* + *P. fluorescens*, *Aspergillus niger*, carbendazim vs untreated check; *T. viride* at 0.4% (as printed on the page) was best: 71.2% seedling emergence, vigour index 1,623, lowest seed and seedling rot (28.9%) and wilt (4.7%); foliar sprays of bioagents promoted growth but had no significant effect on leaf blight or powdery mildew severity — [IJAgS 3081 abstract page](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/3081)
- Dry temperate NW Himalaya pea (kharif 2012-13; HP zone, not confirmed which state) **[snippet]**: *T. viride* at 8 g plus carboxin (a chemical fungicide) at 2.5 g/kg seed gave 19.8% root rot and 3.0% white rot vs 50.8% and 9.9% in the inoculated control; *P. fluorescens* + carboxin was reported as best seed treatment; no Trichoderma-alone figure retrieved — [IPPJ 54620](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/54620)
- Soil application of *Trichoderma koningii* (RMA-8) at 2.5 kg per 65 kg FYM/ha reduced pea root rot/wilt **[snippet]**, source page not identified among the pea root-rot results returned.
- Pot/field work with Pusa 5SD (*T. harzianum* seed dressing, 4 g/kg seed) was on chickpea, not pea — [Dubey et al. 2017, IJAgS 87(10)](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/74794/31465/189436); not applicable to pea doses.
- Rhizobium seed treatment 250 g/acre — NIPHM-Pea

**Predators, parasitoids, traps (NIPHM-Pea)**
- Aphid: release first-instar green lacewing (*Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi*) at 4,000/acre; conserve *Aphidius*, *Diaeretiella rapae*, ladybirds, syrphids, predatory mites.
- Leaf miner: conserve *Chrysocharis pentheus*, *Diglyphus isaea*, *Gronotoma micromorpha*, *Neochrysocharis formosa*, lacewing, ladybird, spiders, red ants; yellow sticky traps/cards for adults. In HP (Palampur trial) the local parasitoids were *Diglyphus* sp. and *Opius* sp. — Thakur-2017.
- Pod borer: pheromone traps 4-5/acre for *Helicoverpa* (lures changed every 2-3 weeks, at least 75 ft apart, one foot above canopy); light traps 1/acre, run 6-10 pm; bird perches 20/acre (king crow, mynah); bonfire at 7-8 pm; hand-pick caterpillars and pupae. Egg parasitoid *Trichogrammatoidea armigera* and larval parasitoids *Bracon hebetor*, *Phanerotoma* spp. are listed as conservation targets, with no release dose.
- Yellow pan/sticky traps for aphids 4-5/acre placed 15 cm above the canopy; home-made tins painted yellow and coated with grease, Vaseline or castor oil.
- Stem fly: braconid wasp parasitoid listed; no dose.

**Other-crop leads (non-pea; do not print as pea doses)**
- Soybean *Etiella*: Bt at 50% flowering and *Trichogramma chilonis* at 5 cc/ha plus light traps **[snippet]**, TNAU soybean page returned by search; no pea trial.
- Chickpea/field bean *Helicoverpa*: neem and HaNPV reduced pod damage, close to endosulfan **[snippet]**; no pea trial.

### Inferences
- Biological control that can be printed with a verified dose for HP pea cards is limited to lacewing (aphid), traps and Rhizobium. Trichoderma and Pseudomonas should be presented as "seed/soil treatment, use label dose" until an HP/NIPHM-type pea dose is found; the Kumaon 0.4% (as printed) and the 8 g/kg snippet are not enough to print a dose.
- Foliar bioagent sprays (Trichoderma, Pseudomonas) did not reduce powdery mildew or leaf blight in the Kumaon pea study, so they should not be offered as foliar mildew cures.

### Gaps
- No pea dose found for Trichoderma foliar/soil (NIPHM silent), *Pseudomonas*, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Beauveria*, *Metarhizium*, *Trichogramma*, HaNPV or Bt on pea.
- No HP field trial with Trichoderma alone for pea root rot/wilt was opened.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
HP-based, full-text evidence exists only for the pea leaf miner: aqueous extract of dried neem drupes (kernel/seed) at 10% (100 g per litre) or Melia azedarach drupes at 10% reduced leaf-miner population by about 59-60% and leaflet infestation by about 38%, with parasitoids preserved, but synthetic lambda-cyhalothrin did better on leaflet infestation. For powdery mildew only sulphur has an HP number (wettable sulphur 0.2%, about 54% disease control), and botanical mildew data are from non-HP trials. Natural methods will only partly control leaf miner and powdery mildew in severe attacks.

### Cited Findings

**Pea leaf miner**
- HP, CSK HPKV (field at Palampur, var. Lincoln sown first week of November, sprays from first week of March after pest build-up, three sprays at 10-day intervals, high-volume knapsack to run-off with sticker/spreader): aqueous extracts made from 100 g dried plant part per litre of water (settled overnight, double muslin filtered) were *Azadirachta indica* drupes 10%, *Melia azedarach* drupes 10%, *Artemisia brevifolia* leaves 10%, *Chromolaena adenophorum* leaves 10%, *Vitex negundo* leaves 10%, *Rumex nepalensis* roots 10%, kinnow peel oil 0.3%, cow urine 5% — Thakur-2017
- Results (mean over 2004-2006): reduction in leaf miner population over control, neem 59.50%, Melia 59.46%, *C. adenophorum* 58.86%, all botanicals above 50%; reduction in leaflet infestation, lambda-cyhalothrin 0.08% 50.95%, avermectin 0.05% 45.48%, Melia 38.75%, neem 38.23% (more than 30% for all botanicals), lowest for cow urine 5%; parasitism in non-chemical plots 15.31-20.04% (neem 19.05%, highest) vs 2.09-6.61% in synthetic plots — Thakur-2017
- Kullu (Bajaura) 1995-99: monocrotophos most effective and economical, followed by cypermethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin, fenvalerate; azadirachtin was tested at 0.0012% (its own result not given on the abstract page) — [IJAgS 40238](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/40238)
- Rana & Guleria 2022 thesis, College of Horticulture and Forestry Neri (Hamirpur, HP): ranking spinosad 0.02% > Agniastra 5% > Bt 0.1% > neem oil 5 ml/L > Melia azedarach leaf extract 10% > Melia dubia leaf extract 10%; best treatment cut infestation by 46.77% over control **[snippet]**, search summary; the thesis page returned no text.
- Non-HP/location not stated (IJPP 45(1), rabi 2013-14 and 2014-15): Neemban (azadirachtin 0.15% EC) 5 ml/L gave 13.6% leaf infestation and 42.1 q/ha; 5% mixed leaf extract (bhang, karvi, walnut, kaner, darek) 19.2% and 40.8 q/ha; malathion standard 6.3% and 47.4 q/ha; control 52.0% and 27.4 q/ha — [IJPP 105042 abstract page](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJPP/article/view/105042)
- Non-HP: NSKE 5% is the recommended leaf-miner spray on TNAU's mustard page **[snippet]** — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/mustard/crop_prot_crop_insect_oil_mustard_5.html); NIPHM-Pea itself gives only chemical options for leaf miner and aphid.

**Powdery mildew**
- HP (Abhilashi University, Mandi, rabi 2023-24, var. Goldy; treatments at 0.2% sprays, first spray 10 days after first appearance): wettable sulphur 0.2%, disease index 26.66 vs 57.77% in water-sprayed control; percent disease control 53.85%; other fungicides in the same trial reached 42-62%. The paper's table and text label treatment numbers inconsistently, so treat the sulphur row as approximate — PM-Mandi
- NIPHM-Pea chemical block for powdery mildew includes sulphur 40% WP 2.26-3.00 kg in 300-400 L water/acre, sulphur 52% SC 800 ml in 120-160 L/acre, sulphur 80% WG 750 g-1.0 kg in 300-400 L/acre, sulphur 85% DP 6-8 kg/acre (second spray after 15-25 days depending on product); sulphur 80% WP 1.252 kg in 300-400 L/acre appears in the next block — NIPHM-Pea. Sulphur is a mineral input; check the farmer's certification body before offering it.
- Non-HP (Allahabad, rabi 2016-17): neem leaf extract 10%, NSKE 5%, neem oil 3%, Nimbicidin 0.3%, *P. fluorescens* 0.5% and carbendazim 0.1% were all better than control against powdery mildew and rust; NSKE 5% was the best botanical for powdery mildew and Nimbicidin 0.3% the best for rust (neem oil 3% next); percent figures not on the abstract page — [Abhishek & Simon 2017](https://www.phytojournal.com/archives/2017.v6.i5.1635/eco-friendly-management-of-powdery-mildew-and-rust-of-garden-pea-pisum-sativum-l)
- Non-HP, location not named: rust in pea, neem leaf extract 3% gave the lowest incidence (30.80%) among neem leaf extract 3%, eucalyptus leaf extract 3%, neem oil 3%, *T. harzianum* 3%, *P. fluorescens* 0.2%; two sprays 10 days apart suggested — [J. Pharmacog. Phytochem. 6(5):652-653](https://www.phytojournal.com/archives/2017.v6.i5.1723/efficacy-of-botanicals-and-bio-agents-against-rust-disease-of-garden-pea-pisum-sativum-l)
- Non-HP **[snippet]**: companion trial (Mishra, Lal & Simon 2017) found neem oil the best amendment for powdery mildew and *T. harzianum* 3% worse (42.02% incidence); a Rajasthan thesis found NSKE the best natural product (62.38% control) followed by panchgavya (57.50%) — concentrations not retrieved.
- A plain water spray reduced powdery mildew and raised seed yield in a Kanpur trial (cited inside PM-Mandi, 2000 report); conidia germination is inhibited by free water. This is a literature citation inside the Mandi paper, not a recommendation.

**Downy mildew, rust, Ascochyta**
- NIPHM-Pea gives only chemical options for these (fungicide products); no botanical was listed.

### Inferences
- A defensible HP card for leaf miner: aqueous neem seed/kernel extract at 10% (100 g crushed dried drupes per litre, overnight, strained) sprayed three times at 10-day intervals from the time the mines first build up; expect partial control (about 38% leaflet-infestation reduction in the HP trial) and a safer effect on parasitoids; remove mined leaves and use yellow sticky cards alongside.
- The 10% extract here is a different preparation from "NSKE 5%" used in other crops; do not mix the two numbers.
- Milk/whey sprays: the only evidence in sibling notes is for cucurbits (Brazil), none found for pea; do not transfer.

### Gaps
- No HP trial of neem, milk, whey, cow urine, garlic or other botanicals on pea powdery mildew, rust or Ascochyta was retrieved.
- NSKE preparation recipe and aphid/pod-borer botanical trial on pea (HP or otherwise) not retrieved.
- Meena (Jobner) botanicals paper and Rana (Neri) thesis full texts were not opened.

## KQ5. Natural-farming formulations (PK3Y/SPNF) with doses for pea

### Takeaway
UHF Nauni documents recipes for Beejamrit, Jeevamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra and Brahmastra, with a spray dilution only for Brahmastra (2.5-3 L in 200 L water). No official HP dose for Khatti Lassi, Dashparni or Sonthastra was found. Pea-specific HP data are nutrition trials (Ghanjeevamrit + Jeevamrit) and farmer testimony, not disease-control data; a Palampur trial showed much lower pea yield under natural farming than conventional.

### Cited Findings

**Recipes (UHF Nauni, Indian Farming 2023)** — UHF-NF
- Beejamrit: 5 kg cow dung bound in cloth and hung in 20 L water for up to 12 h; 50 g lime in 1 L water overnight; squeeze the dung bundle three times into the water; add a handful of soil and 5 L desi cow urine and the lime water; coat seeds by hand, dry well and sow.
- Jeevamrit: 10 kg cow dung, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour and a handful of bund soil, made up to 200 L in a barrel, stirred and fermented in shade for 48 h; described as preventing fungal and bacterial plant diseases (no data).
- Neemastra: 100 L water, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg cow dung, 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves; stir clockwise, cover with gunny bag in shade 48 h, stir morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars (no dilution stated).
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves, 500 g tobacco powder, 500 g green chilli chutney, 250 g garlic chutney in 20 L cow urine, boiled on low flame under lid, kept 48 h and stirred morning and evening; for sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (no dilution stated).
- Brahmastra (1 acre): leaf paste of any five plants (darek, custard apple, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta etc.) at 200 g per plant, in 4 L cow urine, boiled and cooled 48 h; mix 2.5-3 L of solution in 200 L water and spray; useful for sucking pests and caterpillars.
- Mulching (acchadana): soil temperature 25-32 C and 65-72% moisture is the target; whapasa described.
- Farmer testimony (Kufri/Shimla, grows peas among other crops): "Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases"; no quantities or measurements. Peas sold worth Rs 10,000 from her farm.

**Pea-specific natural farming data**
- UHF Nauni M.Sc. work, pea cv. Pusa Pragati 2020-21: best growth, quality and soil nutrients with 2 t/ha Ghanjeevamrit plus 10% Jeevamrit at 15-day intervals; highest yield was still under 100% recommended chemical fertiliser **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/f7722ef8-a8b8-4311-8ecd-4188b429cfdf)
- Pea cv. PB-89 with coriander/spinach intercrops, 2021-22: 1.5 t/ha Ghanjeevamrit with 20% Jeevamrit at 14-day intervals plus six foliar Jeevamrit sprays suggested as best nutrient module **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/7d4e5c70-4557-460c-b39d-5b5345af52a4)
- Palampur mildew-line trial: natural farming plot yields 72-78 q/ha vs 206-212 q/ha under conventional for the two best lines **[snippet, abstract]** — [KrishiKosh](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/a1b48eef-9497-4569-9e10-00a2ec8096e0/full)
- Lahaul pea growers report healthier crops after adopting Jeevamrit; anecdotal **[snippet]** — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/lahaul-farmers-adopt-natural-farming-reap-rich-dividend-421220)
- SPNF prescribes decoctions and extracts (neem leaves, chillies, garlic, sour buttermilk, tobacco) as "prophylactic measures"; no recipe or dose in that document — SPNF-Cert
- UHF Nauni agromet advisory tells natural-farming growers to spray Agniastra, Brahmastra and Neemastra for insect pests **[snippet]** — see sibling notes; not re-opened here.
- Agniastra 5% ranked second in an HP (Neri) leaf-miner trial **[snippet]**, see KQ4.

**Recipe variants from other sources (differ from UHF-NF; do not merge)**
- Buttermilk-based spray (TNAU): fermented about seven days, then 500 ml-1 L in 10 L water; not an SPNF recipe **[snippet from search summary]** — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_cropproduction_plantprotection_arappu.html)
- Neemastra 200 L base with 10 L cow urine and 2 kg dung, and Brahmastra 6 L in 200 L water per acre **[snippet]** — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/pest-and-disease-management-in-natural-farming)

### Inferences
- Jeevamrit at 10-20% foliar is documented in HP only as a nutrition measure for pea; its claim to suppress fungal disease rests on testimony, not trials.
- Agniastra contains tobacco; this matters for tomato/capsicum/brinjal in the same garden (tobacco mosaic carry-over concern raised in the sibling vegetable note) rather than for pea itself. No pea-specific tobacco caution was found.
- Cow-urine 5% was the weakest treatment on leaflet infestation in the Palampur-area trial, so a plain cow-urine spray should not be sold as a leaf-miner remedy.
- A farmer card should frame Neemastra/Agniastra/Brahmastra as suppressive for aphid and small caterpillars; for powdery mildew, rust and Ascochyta the natural-farming formulations have no efficacy data.

### Gaps
- Khatti Lassi, Dashparni and Sonthastra: no HP dose or recipe found (PK3Y/SPNF documents opened were certification documents).
- Spray dilution of Neemastra and Agniastra not stated by UHF-NF.
- No trial of any astra against pea powdery mildew, rust, Ascochyta or aphid.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional practices for pea pests and diseases

### Takeaway
No HP-specific traditional (ITK) practice for pea pests or diseases was found in a retrievable source. The nearest documented items are the NIPHM-listed bonfire and bower (staking) practices, rotation/crop change away from root-rot-prone pea in Lahaul, and farmer testimony on Jeevamrit and Khatti Lassi.

### Cited Findings
- Spiti pea study: farmers' knowledge of pea technology was medium; training need highest in sowing method, seed rate, fertiliser and disease management **[snippet]**, source not retained.
- A 2021 Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge paper on ITKs in Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti exists; no pea pest entries were visible in the summary **[snippet]**.
- NIPHM-Pea lists bonfire at 7-8 pm, bird perches, hand-picking of caterpillars in kerosene-water, and the bower system as practices; not described as HP traditions — NIPHM-Pea
- Lahaul crop-change suggestion (pea to capsicum/tomato where root rot complex built up) — HAREC Kukumseri (link in KQ2)
- HP natural-farming adopters (Kinnaur/Shimla, Lahaul) use Jeevamrit and Khatti Lassi in pea-growing systems, anecdotal — UHF-NF; Tribune (snippet, KQ5)
- Ash dusting against aphids and pumpkin beetles is documented from tribal regions and other Indian states, not HP and not pea — see the sibling note capsicum_brinjal_cucumber.md

### Inferences
- A pea traditional-knowledge card would have to be framed as national IKS (ash, cow urine) or HP natural-farming testimony, not as documented HP pea ITK.

### Gaps
- No HP ITK paper naming pea aphid, leaf miner, pod borer, mildew or rust controls was retrieved; the Kinnaur/Lahaul ITK paper was not opened.
- No quantities, timings or efficacy for any traditional practice on pea.
