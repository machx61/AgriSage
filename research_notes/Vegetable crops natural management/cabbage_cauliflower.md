## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter in HP cabbage/cauliflower, what do they look like, what weather/altitude favours them, and what HP incidence data exist?

### Takeaway
For HP the best-evidenced leaf-visible classes are: (1) diamondback moth (DBM) - called a "limiting factor" for HP cabbage; (2) cabbage butterfly (Pieris) and Spodoptera/Helicoverpa-type caterpillars; (3) cabbage aphid; (4) Alternaria leaf spot (48-85% incidence in cauliflower across seven HP districts, snippet); (5) black rot (V-shaped yellowing; serious nationally but NOT listed for HP in the national package); (6) downy mildew. Clubroot and Sclerotinia stalk rot are whole-plant/stem classes: the app should ask for a whole-plant or root photo rather than a leaf photo. Cabbage and cauliflower share all of these; differences are noted per item.

### Cited Findings

**HP relevance (national package state tags)**
- Pests of national significance with HP named: diamondback moth (HP among 15 states), cabbage aphid *Brevicoryne brassicae* (HP, Delhi, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Manipur, Meghalaya, West Bengal), cabbage butterfly *Pieris brassicae* (HP, Bihar, Manipur, Meghalaya, Punjab, West Bengal), and damping off (*Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*; HP, Maharashtra, West Bengal) - NIPHM-CC
- Regional significance with HP named: tomato fruit borer *Helicoverpa armigera* (HP, Karnataka, UP), mustard aphid *Lipaphis erysimi* (incl. HP), cauliflower mosaic virus (HP only), Alternaria spot (Bihar, HP, Rajasthan) - NIPHM-CC
- Black rot is tagged to Delhi, Manipur, Maharashtra, Rajasthan (not HP); clubroot to Bihar, Kerala, West Bengal (not HP); head borer *Hellula undalis* and *Spodoptera litura* are tagged to other states (not HP) - NIPHM-CC. Note: an absence from a state list is not proof of absence in HP.
- DBM in HP: "In Himachal Pradesh also, the pest is a limiting factor in the production of cruciferous vegetables, particularly cabbage"; DBM is reported to cause more than 90% crop loss under favourable conditions (general citation) - IJAgS-DBM

**HP pest abundance and timing (Nauni, 1300 masl, cauliflower PSBK-1, Oct-Mar crop)**
- Pests recorded on HP cauliflower: cabbage aphid, flea beetle (*Phyllotreta*), greenhouse whitefly, DBM, cabbage head borer (*Hellula undalis*), cabbage butterfly (*Pieris brassicae*); cabbage semilooper *Thysanoplusia orichalcea* appeared only in the conventional plots in 2019-20 - IJE-Cauli
- Aphid: first seen in the last week of October (44th standard week); peak in 5th-6th standard week (late Jan to early Feb) at 23.40 and 27.74 aphids/plant (ZBNF and conventional, 2018-19) - IJE-Cauli
- DBM: began in the 1st standard week (early January) and peaked in the 7th-8th week (mid to late February) at 0.54 larvae/plant (ZBNF) and 0.88 (conventional) in 2019-20 - IJE-Cauli
- Cabbage head borer: began in 3rd standard week, peak in 8th - IJE-Cauli
- Cabbage butterfly: larvae began in 6th standard week (first week of February), peak in the 9th (end of February): 6.52 (ZBNF) vs 16.00 (conventional) larvae/plant in 2019; 6.86 vs 16.06 in 2020 - IJE-Cauli
- Whitefly (greenhouse whitefly) appeared in the third week of November and peaked at the end of December (2019-20); flea beetle peaked in the 50th standard week (mid-December) - IJE-Cauli
- Cabbage in mid-hill Solan (1500 masl): DBM larval counts in untreated plots were 3.78 and 4.32 larvae/plant before/after the two spray dates in March-April 2006 (crop transplanted 9 November 2005) - IJAgS-DBM

**Leaf and plant symptoms (national package text, all crops of the group)**
- **DBM** (*Plutella xylostella*): eggs laid singly along veins on the leaf underside at night; larva greenish, 1-1.5 cm, tapering at both ends; pupates in a loose silken cocoon; adult greyish-brown with three diamond-shaped white patches when wings fold. Caterpillars feed on the leaf undersurface, causing "withered appearance of affected leaves" and "skeletonized leaves"; egg 7 days, larva 14 days, pupa 7 days; active throughout the year - NIPHM-CC
- **Cabbage butterfly** (*Pieris brassicae*): velvety green larva about 4.2 cm; gregarious when young, dispersing as they grow; adult with snow-white forewings and black distal margins; they "skeletonise leaves and bore into heads of cabbage and cauliflower"; pupation in damaged leaves - NIPHM-CC
- **Cabbage head borer** (*Hellula undalis*): webbed leaves, caterpillars bore into stem, stalk, leaf veins and the head; holes in head with fecal matter; larva 12-15 mm, greyish yellow with seven purplish-brown stripes - NIPHM-CC
- **Leaf webber** (*Crocidolomia*): webbed leaves with fecal matter, skeletonised leaves; larva with red head and brown stripes; eggs in masses of 40-100 on leaf underside - NIPHM-CC
- **Tobacco caterpillar** (*Spodoptera litura*): early larvae gregarious, scrape the leaf giving a "papery white appearance", later irregular holes then skeletonization leaving veins and petioles; velvety black larva with yellowish-green dorsal stripes, 35-40 mm; egg masses covered with brown hairs; moths active at night - NIPHM-CC
- **Cabbage aphid**: yellowish-green; "infests crucifers in cold season"; nymphs and adults suck sap, loss of vigour, sooty mould on honeydew - NIPHM-CC
- **Painted bug** (*Bagrada*): nymphs reddish, adults yellow with black markings; sap sucking from leaves, shoots, pods causing wilting and loss of vigour - NIPHM-CC
- **Black rot** (*Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *campestris*): "chlorotic or yellow (angular) areas near the leaf margins"; yellow area extends to veins and midrib forming "characteristic 'v' shaped chlorotic spots which later turn black"; veins and veinlets turn brown then black; vascular blackening extends to midrib, petiole, stem and sometimes roots; bacterial ooze; early infection = wilt and death, late infection = soft rot. Seed-borne internally and soil-borne; spread by irrigation water and rain splash. Favoured by RH >90%, high soil moisture, frequent rain - NIPHM-CC. UC IPM adds that atypical symptoms such as small brown specks can mimic other bacterial diseases and that black veins within lesions help identify it but do not always develop - UCIPM-BR (California; non-HP)
- **Alternaria leaf spot** (*A. brassicae*, *A. brassicicola*): small dark spots enlarging into circles, forming concentric rings; spots coalesce and blight leaves; linear spots on petioles, stems and pods; seed-borne (shrivelled seed, poor germination). Favoured by soil temperature around 28 degC, high humidity or persistent dew, moist weather with intermittent showers - NIPHM-CC
- **Downy mildew** (*Peronospora parasitica*): small purplish-brown spots on the underside; small pale yellow angular spots on the upper surface with downy growth below; spots coalesce and leaves shrivel; in cabbage lesions expose heads to soft rot; cauliflower curds look brownish; dark depressed stem lesions. Favoured by 12-27 degC, high soil moisture, soil pH 5-7.0; oospores in debris/soil - NIPHM-CC
- **Powdery mildew** (*Erysiphe cruciferarum*): white powdery spots on the UPPER leaf surface, stems and flower parts; dry conditions with morning RH of 80%, 12-27 degC - NIPHM-CC
- **White rust** (*Albugo candida*): white shiny raised blisters (pustules) on the LOWER leaf surface, stems, flowers; flower distortion; RH >90%, frequent rain - NIPHM-CC
- **Clubroot** (*Plasmodiophora brassicae*): above ground "stunting and yellowing of plants", leaves yellowish and wilt on hot days; below ground club-like swellings of roots; resting spores persist about 10 years; prevalent below pH 7; 12-27 degC (25 degC optimum), high soil moisture, soil pH 5-7.0 - NIPHM-CC
- **Damping off**: pre-emergence kill and post-emergence collar rot, water-soaked soft collar, seedlings topple; favoured by high humidity, high soil moisture, cloudiness, temperature below 24 degC for a few days, crowded seedlings, poor drainage - NIPHM-CC
- **Non-pathogenic look-alikes** for the vision model: whiptail in cauliflower (strap-like leaf blades, deformed growing point, no curd; molybdenum deficiency in acidic soils pH 4-5); browning (hollow stem, brown bitter curd; boron deficiency); buttoning; riceyness; internal tipburn (leaf margin death inside the head, not visible externally); phosphorus deficiency (pigmented old leaves); zinc deficiency (small young leaves) - NIPHM-CC

**HP disease incidence (all [snippet])**
- Alternaria leaf spot of cauliflower: periodic surveys in Hamirpur, Mandi, Bilaspur, Kangra, Una, Solan and Kullu gave incidence 48-85% and disease index 66-69% **[snippet; conference-style abstract surfaced in a search summary]** - [KrishiKosh, Management of Alternaria leaf spot of cauliflower through innovative methods](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/57c74836-dab0-4138-8ae2-4e6a1f2aa4e6) (which of the results listed this figure was not confirmed; verify)
- Sclerotinia stalk rot of cauliflower: first noticed in the Kullu valley in a Snowball-16 seed crop in the early 1970s; average temperate cauliflower seed yield in HP dropped from about 400 to 125 kg/ha, and "in epidemic years the crop loss is complete" **[snippet]** - [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ 12590](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/12590) and the Nauni thesis listed at [KrishiKosh bitstream](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/05b5a908-7b7e-456e-8b56-84d741ec613c/content). A Nauni thesis table "Disease incidence of Sclerotinia rot of cauliflower in Himachal Pradesh" exists but its values were not visible.
- Sclerotinia in Indian mustard (a host, not cole): ICAR-DRMR slides state the disease is a problem in HP and that high RH, low bright sunshine hours and high soil moisture at the critical stage favour it - [Sharma, GCIRC bulletin](https://www.gcirc.org/fileadmin/documents/Bulletins/B28/3._Pankaj_Sharma.pdf) (mustard-specific; weather link may not transfer to cole crops)
- Outside HP, for comparison only: black rot "can result in up to 40% of crop losses" (general citation in a Tripura paper) - [Kumar & Chhetri 2025, IJAgS 95(2)](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/145133/59846/460386); Meghalaya cabbage black rot incidence 52.7% **[snippet]**.

### Inferences
- Recommended vision classes (leaf-photo): DBM/caterpillar "window-and-hole" damage (separate sub-cards for DBM, Pieris, Spodoptera by larva photo and egg location), aphid colony/honeydew, black rot, Alternaria leaf spot, downy mildew. Whole-plant/root classes: clubroot (stunted, midday wilting, galled roots), Sclerotinia stalk rot, damping off. Non-pathogenic class: whiptail/boron/tipburn.
- Critical look-alike pairs: (1) black rot (V-shaped yellow wedge from the margin, black veins, no concentric rings) vs Alternaria (round spots with concentric rings, no vein blackening) vs downy mildew (angular, vein-limited yellow patches, downy growth below) - the NIPHM descriptions support these distinctions; (2) white rust (raised blisters, LOWER surface) vs powdery mildew (powder, UPPER surface) vs downy mildew (grey-white down below a yellow patch); (3) DBM (undersurface windowing, small 1-1.5 cm tapered green larva, wriggles when touched) vs Pieris (large gregarious green larvae, big ragged holes, heads bored) vs Spodoptera (papery-white scraping, then holes) - the larva sizes quoted give the cue; (4) clubroot vs black rot vs nutrient stress (all cause yellowing/wilting): the root is the discriminator; (5) tipburn/whiptail/boron are non-infectious and must not receive a spray recommendation.
- Season mapping in HP mid-hills: pest build-up is winter-to-spring (aphid from late Oct, whitefly Nov-Dec, DBM and head borer Jan-Feb, butterfly Feb); damp, showery spells favour black rot, Alternaria and downy mildew. Dry spells with ~80% morning RH favour powdery mildew.
- Black rot is likely present in HP (it is a seed-borne disease of the same crops), but no HP-specific source was found, so the app should treat it as present-but-unquantified.

### Gaps
- No HP altitude-wise incidence for black rot, downy mildew, clubroot or white rust; no HP clubroot survey found (searches returned Nepal/Brazil/Czech only).
- The Alternaria 48-85% figure and Sclerotinia figures are from search summaries only.
- Pieris and DBM peak timing is from one mid-hill site (1300 masl); no high-hill (Lahaul/Kinnaur) or low-hill data.
- No HP source on CaMV symptoms or incidence (only the state tag in NIPHM-CC).

## KQ2. Cultural practices (nursery hygiene, seed treatment, rotation, lime, trap crops, intercropping, sanitation, varieties)

### Takeaway
The national package gives a complete cultural kit: raised nursery beds, solarization, 2-3 year non-crucifer rotation, sanitation, 2 rows of bold-seeded mustard per 25 rows of cabbage as DBM trap crop, tomato/garlic/coriander/carrot intercrops, fine-mesh nursery netting, hydrated lime for clubroot, and named tolerant varieties. HP evidence at Nauni shows cauliflower intercropped with pea, coriander and mustard (trap crop) had fewer pests and more natural enemies, but that trial changed several things at once. Hot-water seed treatment is supported by a German lab study (50 degC for 30 min), not by any HP or NIPHM-CC source.

### Cited Findings

**Nursery and seed**
- Use disease-free seedlings and quality seed; "a chemical or heat pasteurized planting medium"; avoid excessive watering and poorly drained areas; "raised beds: more than 15 cm height is better for water drainage or use pro trays for raising seedlings" - NIPHM-CC
- Soil solarization: cover beds with "polythene sheet of 45 gauge (0.45 mm) thickness for three weeks before sowing" (units inconsistent in source; verify) - NIPHM-CC
- Keep nursery weed free by hand pulling; avoid carrying weed seedlings with cabbage seedlings - NIPHM-CC
- Fine-mesh netting in the nursery to stop cabbage butterflies reaching the crop and laying eggs - NIPHM-CC
- Seed hot-water treatment: in laboratory trials on carrot and cabbage "50 °C for 30 minutes" gave good effects against *Xanthomonas campestris*; efficacy against *Alternaria* spp. including *A. brassicicola* was above 95% (pooled across crops); seed-borne pathogens could be reduced without significant germination loss at 50 degC for 20 to 30 minutes, up to 53 degC for 10-30 minutes; at higher temperatures the time must be shortened for sensitive crops (German study, cabbage among five vegetables; not cauliflower, not HP) - Orgprints-HWT. UC IPM says seed "can be hot water treated" but that hot water and other treatments are not fully effective and may reduce germination - UCIPM-BR. NIPHM-CC does not give a hot-water protocol.
- Alternaria is seed-borne (shrivelled seed, poor germination) and black rot is internally seed-borne - NIPHM-CC

**Field cultural control**
- Rotation: "Crop rotation for 2-3 years with non-cruciferous crops" (black rot row); "long rotations (3 years) without crucifer crops or cruciferous weeds such as wild mustard", plant later plantings upwind of earlier ones, wide spacing, rows parallel to prevailing winds, not close to hedgerows (the next row in the table, apparently Alternaria; the table layout is ambiguous) - NIPHM-CC. UC IPM: do not plant a crucifer crop more often than every 2 years in an infested field, remove weed and volunteer crucifers, deep plough and bury debris, avoid sprinklers - UCIPM-BR (California)
- Sanitation: removal and destruction of plant remnants, stubbles and debris after harvest and ploughing; destroy infected plant debris; sanitary measures and destruction of weeds; avoid thick sowing, excess moisture, overhead irrigation; allow good air movement - NIPHM-CC
- Deep summer ploughing on bright sunny days in May-June, field exposed 2-3 weeks - NIPHM-CC (Do's and Don'ts)
- Clubroot: "A pH slightly above neutral (usually about pH 7.2) helps to minimize disease"; "Add hydrated lime to soil to increase pH to 7.2 (6 weeks before planting @ 1.5 t/ac)"; use disease-free seedlings; avoid excess irrigation - NIPHM-CC. Liming trade-off: whiptail in cauliflower is corrected by liming "up to 6.5" pH and 1-2 kg/ac sodium or ammonium molybdate - NIPHM-CC
- Nepal cauliflower clubroot trial: boron + lime gave the least incidence and severity (44.45% and 33.33%), then Trichoderma + lime (48.14% and 37.03%) **[snippet; non-HP]** - [Study of P. brassicae virulence and management on cauliflower, JPPS](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/jpps/article/view/47290)
- Trap crop for DBM: "Sowing 2 rows of bold seeded mustard as a trap crop for every 25 rows of cabbage to attract moths to mustard. Plant the first row 12 days before transplanting and the second row 25 days after transplanting" - NIPHM-CC. A DBM review notes Indian mustard trap cropping is among the most promising options, using paired rows per 25 rows of cabbage; however, that scheme used cartap and dichlorvos sprays on the crop and trap crop, so it is not a natural-only package **[snippet]**
- Intercrops: "tomato, garlic, coriander and carrot in alternate rows with cabbage"; intercropping with nasturtium results in fewer butterfly eggs on cabbage - NIPHM-CC
- Insectary/flowering plants to conserve parasitoids and predators: dill, anise, spearmint, buckwheat, yarrow, white clover, tansy, cowpea, fennel, cosmos, mustard, sunflower, marigold, French bean, carrot family; basil as a repellent plant for tobacco caterpillar; castor as an oviposition trap crop at 250 plants/acre for *Spodoptera* - NIPHM-CC
- HP trial (Nauni, 1300 masl): ZBNF cauliflower was intercropped with pea (P89), coriander and mustard as trap crop; conventional cauliflower was sole-cropped. ZBNF "harboured less pest diversity and attracted more natural enemies"; "use of intercrop also repelled certain insect pests" - IJE-Cauli (effect of intercrop and of ZBNF sprays cannot be separated)
- KVK Solan trial: cauliflower (cv. Pusa Snowball K1) intercropped with fenugreek (row spacing 60 x 45 cm main crop; fenugreek at 60 x 7.5 cm between rows) with oat as a border crop; LER 2.09 for fenugreek intercropping; mulching with dry grass - PA-KVK (a yield/LER result; no pest data seen in the text)
- Mulching: black LDPE 30 micron, ends buried 10 cm, for weeds - NIPHM-CC
- Nutrition: avoid over-nitrogen ("crop becomes too succulent and therefore susceptible to insects and diseases") - NIPHM-CC
- Nasturtium and bird perches (cabbage butterfly, tobacco caterpillar); collecting eggs and larvae by hand "usually on the underside of the leaves"; remove and destroy webbed leaves with caterpillars (leaf webber) - NIPHM-CC

**Varieties (national package; verify HP availability)**
- Cabbage: black leg - Pusa Drum Head; black rot - Pusa Mukta. Cauliflower: downy mildew - Pusa Hybrid-2, Pusa Kartik Sankar; black rot - Pusa Shubra, Pusa Snowball K-1, Pusa Snowball Kt-25 - NIPHM-CC
- Pusa Snowball K-1 is also cited as a black rot resistance source under temperate conditions, but resistant cauliflower lines are scarce and strains vary **[snippet]** - [Performance of cauliflower genotypes for resistance against black rot, Indian J. Hort.](https://journal.iahs.org.in/index.php/ijh/article/view/1320)
- Cauliflower grown in HP trials: PSBK-1 (Nauni) and Pusa Snowball K1 (Kandaghat) - IJE-Cauli; PA-KVK

### Inferences
- A card can state: raised nursery bed over 15 cm, pro-tray or pasteurized medium, 2-3 year non-crucifer rotation, wild mustard/volunteer crucifer removal, drip or morning irrigation, destroying debris, lime to pH about 7.2 for clubroot fields. The hot-water seed step ("50 degC for 20-30 min, test germination first") should be labelled as lab-supported but not HP-tested.
- Mustard "trap crop" must be handled with care: NIPHM-CC gives the sowing geometry but no instruction on what to do with the trap rows. Trap rows concentrate DBM and the cited DBM review sprayed them with synthetics; a natural-only card should suggest hand-picking or Bt/neem on the trap rows (inference, not cited).
- Lime to 7.2 for clubroot conflicts with some micronutrient behaviour (boron availability falls at high pH) but whiptail correction uses liming to 6.5; the app should not recommend lime on sandy low-pH fields without a soil test (inference).

### Gaps
- No HP-source lime dose for clubroot, and no HP clubroot-resistant variety.
- No sticky-trap or net mesh size specification in NIPHM-CC for cole nurseries (only "fine-mesh").
- No UHF Nauni / CSK HPKV package-of-practices variety list for cabbage/cauliflower could be opened.
- No data on rotation length or lime effect specifically for HP's soils (mostly acidic), and no source on pine-needle or other organic mulches for cole crops in HP.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
Verified doses: Trichogramma 20,000/acre 4-6 times at weekly intervals (DBM); Diadegma semiclausum 100,000/acre in hills; Cotesia plutellae 20,000/acre (plains); Btk 1 g/L (Halt, 55,000 SU) at Nauni, two sprays 15 days apart, cutting DBM from about 4 to 0.3-0.7 larvae/plant; NPV for Spodoptera 40 LE/acre with jaggery or Robin Blue, thrice at 10-15 day intervals; Trichoderma/Pseudomonas 2 kg/acre in FYM or vermicompost at pre-sowing. No dose was found for Beauveria, Pseudomonas foliar sprays, Bacillus subtilis or Metarhizium on cole crops.

### Cited Findings

**Egg and larval parasitoids**
- DBM: "Release egg parasitoid, T. chilonis/pretiosum @ 20,000/acre 4-6 times at weekly interval" - NIPHM-CC
- DBM: "Release larval parasitoids, Diadegma semiclausum @ 1,00,000/acre (Hills - below 25-27 degC) or Cotesia plutellae (plains) @ 20,000/acre from 20 days after planting" (the hills/temperature text is garbled in the PDF) - NIPHM-CC
- Conserve *Brachymeria*, *Eriborus*, *Diadromus*, *Apanteles*; fungal pathogens *Paecilomyces* and *Zoophthora radicans* are "effective" (no dose) - NIPHM-CC
- Cabbage butterfly: "Release Trichogramma spp." (no dose); conserve *Cotesia glomeratus* and *Pteromalus puparum*; erect bird perches - NIPHM-CC
- Tobacco caterpillar: conserve *Trichogramma chilonis*, *Tetrastichus*, *Telenomus*, *Carcelia*, *Campoletis* - NIPHM-CC
- Aphid: conserve *Aphidius colemani*, *Diaeretiella*, *Aphelinus*, lacewings, ladybirds, syrphids; lady beetle adults eat about 50 aphids/day; syrphid 3rd-instar larvae 80-90 aphids/day - NIPHM-CC
- Natural enemies in HP cauliflower (Nauni): coccinellids (*Coccinella septempunctata*, *Hippodamia variegata*), syrphids (*Episyrphus balteatus*, *Eupeodes frequens*, *Metasyrphus confrator*), the aphid parasitoid *Diaeretiella rapae*, the DBM larval parasitoid *Diadegma semiclausa*, and *Cotesia glomerata* on butterfly larvae (seen only in the ZBNF plots in 2019-20) - IJE-Cauli
- Peak % parasitisation was 13.56% (ZBNF) vs 4.61% (conventional) in 2018-19 and 22.22% vs 6.40% in 2019-20 - IJE-Cauli. A cited-within figure: DBM parasitisation by *D. semiclausam* of 28.94 and 30.77% in another cauliflower study (Sharma et al. 2020, location not stated in the text read) - IJE-Cauli
- Diadegma literature: preferred temperature 15-25 degC; in the tropics found at higher altitudes (fact sheet: not below about 800 m); parasitism above 90% reported where broad-spectrum insecticides are avoided; very sensitive to pyrethroids and organophosphates **[snippet; non-HP]** - [Pacific Pests fact sheet](https://www.pestnet.org/fact_sheets/diadegma_species_285.htm)

**Microbials and traps**
- Btk at Nauni (cabbage var. Pride of India, 1500 masl, transplanted 9 Nov 2005): treatments azadirachtin (Nimbecidine 300 ppm) 2 ml/L, Btk (Halt 5 WP, 55,000 SU) 1 g/L, spinosad (Spintor 45 SC) 0.33 ml/L, *Verticillium lecanii* (Ecocill 10^8 cfu); two sprays at a 15-day interval (27 March and 10 April 2006) in the evening with Triton X-100 at 0.02% in 1000 L/ha. Mean larvae/plant (first spray, second spray): untreated 3.78 and 4.32; Btk 1.08 and 0.32 (overall 0.70); spinosad 0.97 and 0.21 (0.50, "statistically at par with" Btk); *V. lecanii* 1.28 and 0.53 (0.90); azadirachtin 1.57 and 0.60 (1.03). All treatments significantly better than control; the authors suggest alternating spinosad and Btk - IJAgS-DBM. (The table prints the *V. lecanii* dose as "80 g" and the text as "8 x 10^12 conidia/g"; the printed units are inconsistent.)
- Btk also reported at 74% population reduction (0.1%) vs 78% spinosad **[snippet of the same trial]**; Ghana Dipel weekly vs fortnightly sprays **[snippet; non-HP]**
- NPV for tobacco caterpillar: "Spray NPV @ 40LE/ac in combination with jaggery 1 kg, sandovit 100 ml or Robin Blue 50 g thrice at 10-15 days interval on observing the eggs or first instar larvae in the evening hours"; do not apply NPV on late instar larva or in daytime; apply only after 5 pm - NIPHM-CC
- Trichoderma / Pseudomonas: FYM 8-10 t/acre or vermicompost 5 t/acre "treated with Trichoderma spp. and/or Pseudomonas sp @ 2 kg/acre", incorporated 2-3 weeks (FYM) or 1 week (vermicompost) before transplanting; "Trichoderma as seed and nursery treatment and Pseudomonas fluorescens as seed, nursery treatment and soil application" (no rate printed for seed/nursery) - NIPHM-CC
- Neem cake: 100 kg/acre at transplanting "for reducing nematodes and borer damage" - NIPHM-CC
- Biofertilizers (not disease agents): Azotobacter and PSB 8-10 g/kg seed; seedling root dip 250 g/acre seedlings - NIPHM-CC
- Trichoderma / Brazilian cauliflower trial: *T. harzianum* biofungicide with calcined limestone improved healthy root volume and reduced diseased root volume **[snippet; non-HP]** - [Liming and biofungicide for clubroot in cauliflower](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/oai:scielo:S1983-40632017000300303); a Colombian cabbage trial found Trichoderma alone best and the combination with dolomite at 2.1 t/ha worse **[snippet]** - [Agronomia Colombiana](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/agrocol/article/view/46759)
- Stalk rot: culture filtrate of *Trichoderma harzianum* gave 23.6% disease incidence vs 9.7% with the fungicide Bavistin 50% (1.5 g/L) **[snippet; location not stated]** - [IPPJ 17468](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/17468)
- Pheromone traps: install for DBM and *Spodoptera litura* "@ 10-12/fixed field" for surveillance, 75 feet apart, one foot above canopy, lure change every 2-3 weeks (monitoring section); "Install pheromone traps @ 4-5/acre for monitoring" (DBM and tobacco caterpillar rows) - NIPHM-CC. No mass-trapping density for cole crops is given.
- Sticky traps: yellow pan water / sticky traps "15 cm above the canopy... @ 10 traps/ac" for aphid monitoring; the stage table says "12/acre to monitor alates"; home-made yellow-painted tins coated with grease, Vaseline or castor oil - NIPHM-CC
- Light traps 1/acre for leaf webber and tobacco caterpillar adults; bird perches - NIPHM-CC

### Inferences
- Btk (1 g/L, two sprays 15 days apart, evenings) is the only microbial with a dose and an HP field result in the opened sources; efficacy was good (about 80-90% fewer larvae than untreated) but only at one site and year. The card should say "check the product label for potency" because the dose is formulation-specific (55,000 SU/g).
- Diadegma at 100,000/acre is expensive and requires commercial supply; HP evidence shows Diadegma is already present naturally in mid-hill cauliflower, so conservation (no broad-spectrum insecticides; flowering insectary plants) is the realistic farmer message (consistent with IJE-Cauli and the Diadegma fact sheet).
- Because NPV and Trichogramma are produced by labs/biocontrol units, an app card should tie them to "KVK / biocontrol lab availability".

### Gaps
- No dose retrieved for *Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium*, *Lecanicillium* (other than the Nauni *V. lecanii* entry with garbled units), *Pseudomonas fluorescens* foliar/seed rates on cole crops, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Ampelomyces*, or aphid biocontrol releases.
- No HP biocontrol product list (e.g., CSK HPKV / UHF-recommended Bt brands and rates in the official package of practices).
- No black rot biocontrol with a dose.
- The *Trichoderma* seed dose for cole crops (g/kg) is not given in NIPHM-CC; do not borrow the brinjal 5 g/kg figure from another file without a source.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
NSKE 5% is the national recommendation against DBM and against Spodoptera eggs and first-instar larvae; a neem-oil water-soluble product at azadirachtin 0.03% (300 ppm) is given at 1000-2000 ml in 200-400 L/acre. In an HP field test, azadirachtin 300 ppm at 2 ml/L reduced DBM significantly but was the least effective of four biopesticides. No botanical with a concentration and efficacy number was found for aphids or for diseases (black rot, Alternaria, downy mildew).

### Cited Findings
- DBM: "Foliar spray with 5% NSKE or azadirachtin 0.03% (300 ppm) neem oil based WSP @ 1000-2000 ml in 200-400 l of water/acre" - NIPHM-CC
- Tobacco caterpillar: "Spray NSKE 5% against eggs and first instar larvae" - NIPHM-CC
- HP field result: azadirachtin (Nimbecidine 300 ppm) 2 ml/L, two sprays 15 days apart: 1.57 and 0.60 larvae/plant (overall mean 1.03 vs untreated 4.05) - IJAgS-DBM
- Neri (Hamirpur, HP) thesis on DBM biology and management in crucifers: 10% aqueous NSKE gave mean larval mortality of 78.10% and pupal mortality of 83.89%, repellency 84.63% at 10% vs 38.93% at 2%; agniastra was judged the best of the tested formulations for DBM at the larval stage; lowest mortality came from 5% aqueous bhang leaf extract **[snippet; laboratory/bioassay abstract; the agniastra concentration was not visible]** - [KrishiKosh, Biology and management of diamondback moth on crucifers](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/d1ed22b1-e4b6-4cc8-80d2-483ec848d69f)
- Haryana (Gurugram) field survey and test, cabbage and cauliflower: seven neem-based biopesticides vs carbosulfan; NSKE 5% most effective, then Nimbicidine and Neemgold; cost-benefit ratio 1:9.41 (cabbage) and 1:9.53 (cauliflower); neem leaf extract 1:7.72 and 1:6.31; "NSKE and NLE can be recommended against major pests of cabbage and cauliflower" (abstract only; non-HP) - [Indian Journal of Entomology, Population dynamics and farmers' friendly IPM](https://indianentomology.org/index.php/ije/article/view/804)
- Natural-farming plant protection sprays used in HP cauliflower trials: Brahmastra 3% and Agniastra 3% plus Darekastra at 500 L/ha (Nauni) - IJE-Cauli; Agniastra and Brahmastra alternately at 5% fortnightly and khatti lassi 5% three times (KVK Solan) - PA-KVK (see KQ5)
- Limitation noted in the DBM literature: DBM "was the first crop pest in the world to develop resistance to DDT and Bacillus thuringiensis" **[snippet; review]** - rotating modes of action matters
- For diseases, the national package lists only chemical options (captan seed treatment/drench; zineb or mancozeb sprays) and cultural measures; no botanical or biological spray is offered for black rot, Alternaria or downy mildew - NIPHM-CC

### Inferences
- A DBM spray ladder supported by opened sources: NSKE 5% or neem-oil azadirachtin 0.03% (cheap, but least efficacious in the one HP trial), Btk 1 g/L (best organic-permitted option, 74-90% reduction), conserve Diadegma. Sprays should be directed to the leaf UNDERSIDE and applied in the evening (NIPHM-CC Do's; IJAgS-DBM sprayed in evening).
- The app should state plainly that no natural spray has been demonstrated against black rot, and that for Alternaria and downy mildew only the natural-farming sprays (lassi, jeevamrit) have anecdotal or in-vitro support.

### Gaps
- NSKE preparation recipe (kg kernel per litre, soak time) was not in the opened texts.
- No concentration for garlic-chilli extract, neem oil (emulsified) in ml/L for cole crops, karanj oil, soap solution, or potassium bicarbonate on cole crops; no copper (permitted in organic standards in some schemes) dose from an opened source.
- No botanical dose for aphids on cabbage/cauliflower from an opened source.
- Percent efficacy of NSKE in HP field conditions not retrieved (only the laboratory snippet).

## KQ5. Natural-farming (SPNF/ZBNF) formulations with doses for cole crops

### Takeaway
The UHF Nauni and KVK Solan papers give actual cauliflower schedules: beejamrit seed treatment 10-20%; ghanjeevamrit 1.5 t/ha; jeevamrit drench 10% weekly and foliar 5% every 5 days; agniastra and brahmastra alternately at 5% fortnightly (KVK Solan) or 3% (Nauni); khatti lassi 5% three times (curd initiation, curd development, maturity). Pest data exist for the Nauni trial (fewer DBM, butterfly, aphids than conventional plots) but disease-control numbers are absent. Khatti lassi at 1 L per 20 L water every 10 days is an HP agriculture officer's advice for wheat rust, not for cole crops.

### Cited Findings

**Recipes (UHF Nauni, Indian Farming 2023)**
- Beejamrit: 5 kg cow dung in cloth; 1 L water + 50 g lime overnight; squeeze dung bundle in that water thrice; add handful of soil, 5 L desi cow urine and lime water; coat seeds, dry and sow; "equally effective in controlling seed borne diseases" (no data) - UHF-NF
- Jivamrita: 10 kg cow dung, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour, handful of bund soil; claimed to prevent fungal and bacterial plant diseases (no data) - UHF-NF
- Neemastra: 100 L water, 10 L cow urine, 2 kg cow dung, 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves; gunny bag cover in shade 48 h; stir clockwise 1 minute morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sucking insects, small caterpillars - UHF-NF
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves, 500 g tobacco powder, 500 g green chilli chutney, 250 g garlic chutney in 20 L cow urine; boil on low flame under lid; keep 48 h stirring morning and evening; for sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (spray dilution not stated) - UHF-NF
- Brahmastra (1 acre): leaf chutney of any 5 plants (Darek, Sita Phal, Castor, Papaya, Mango, Guava, Datura, Duranta etc.) at 200 g per plant in 4 L cow urine, heat to boil, cool 48 h; "Mix 2.5-3 litres of solution in 200 litres of water and spray" - UHF-NF
- Acchadana (mulch) targets soil 25-32 degC and 65-72% moisture - UHF-NF
- KVK Solan table: Jeevamrut 10 kg cow dung + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg gram flour + handful soil in 200 L water, stirred thrice a day clockwise, kept 5-7 days in shade; Ghanjeevamrut 200 kg cow dung dried 4-5 days, powdered, sieved, plus 4 L jeevamrut, shade dried 2-4 hours; Beejamrut 5 kg cow dung + 5 L urine + 250 g lime + 20 L water + handful soil (12 h soak) - PA-KVK

**Cauliflower schedule in HP trials**
- KVK Solan (Pusa Snowball K1, 2023-24 and 2024-25): seeds treated with beejamrut at 10-20%; ghanjeevamrut 1.5 t/ha before sowing; fermented jeevamrut drench at 10% ("10 L in 90 L water") during field preparation and repeated at seven-day intervals to harvest; jeevamrut foliar spray 5% ("5 L in 95 L water") every 5 days from the third week after transplanting till harvest; "agniastr and bramhastr were sprayed at alternate weeks @ 5% and khatti lassi @ 5% was sprayed three times, first before curd initiation, second at curd development and third spray was given at maturity stage"; main crop equivalent yield 294.49 q/ha natural vs 261.73 chemical - PA-KVK. The text I read contains no pest or disease counts for this trial.
- Nauni (PSBK-1, 2018-19 and 2019-20): jeevamrit, ghanjeevamrit and beejamrit as fertilizers; "Darekastra @ 500 l ha-1, Bramhastra @ 3% and Agniastra @ 3% were used for pest management"; conventional plots received Captan 75% WP, malathion 50% EC and imidacloprid 17.8% SL. Result: less pest diversity, more natural enemies; DBM peak 0.54 vs 0.88 larvae/plant; butterfly peak 6.52 vs 16.00 (2019) and 6.86 vs 16.06 (2020); aphid peak 23.40 vs 27.74 (2018-19) and 18.88 vs 28.38 (2019-20); no significant yield or damage data in the abstract - IJE-Cauli. Confounded: ZBNF plots also had intercrops (pea, coriander, mustard trap crop).
- Cabbage (Nauni, 1275 masl, Nov-Mar, 2023-25): jeevamrit at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50% - 40% gave maximum chlorophyll, photosynthetic rate, antioxidant activity; response fell at 50%; the method (soil drench vs foliar) was not stated in the text I read and no pest/disease results are reported - IJAgS-Jeev
- Jeevamrut formulations suppressed mycelial growth (more than 90%) of *Alternaria alternata* in vitro (Pandia et al. 2019, as cited) - PA-KVK. In-vitro only; not on a cole crop.
- Cabbage in Nauni survey under ZBNF vs conventional: seasonal pest populations did not differ significantly **[snippet of a 2018-19 cabbage study at Nauni]** - [KrishiKosh, Insect-pests, natural enemies and soil microflora in cabbage under ZBNF and conventional farming](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/d392dbde-0a91-45cf-830b-f330615f1df2)

**Khatti lassi and dashparni**
- HP Deputy Director of Agriculture (Hamirpur): "spray a solution of one litre lassi with 20 litre water in intervals of ten days" for early yellow rust in organic wheat (Feb 2023) - [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/hamirpur-farmers-report-yellow-rust-in-wheat-crop-482937/amp). Wheat, not cole crop.
- Farmer testimony: "Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases" (farm growing peas, carrot, cucumber, french bean, coriander, radish, apple; anecdotal, no measurement) - UHF-NF. In Kinnaur, woolly apple aphid was "controlled by the spray of Dashparni ark" (no recipe or dose) - UHF-NF
- Sour buttermilk: only 15-20-day-old solution diluted 10-20 times should be used to avoid phytotoxicity **[snippet; non-HP]** - [ICAR-NAHEP, Cow based bioformulations](https://nahep.icar.gov.in/KMS/UploadKC1/b06b7c48-1f85-4697-a602-6b0f34c7fa74/Cow%20based%20bioformulations%20for%20pest%20management.pdf)

**Tobacco in agniastra**
- Agniastra contains 500 g tobacco powder (UHF-NF). The solanaceous-crop TMV caution (see the capsicum/brinjal note) was not checked for cole crops in this session; I found no source on tobacco-based sprays and any cole-crop virus, so this is an open question rather than a finding.

### Inferences
- The most defensible HP cauliflower natural-farming card is the KVK Solan schedule (it is a measured, replicated 2-year station trial) with the caveat that no disease-control figure was reported; the Nauni trial supports the claim that agniastra/brahmastra at 3% plus intercropping accompany lower caterpillar and aphid peaks, but is confounded.
- Dilution is inconsistent across sources for the same formulations: brahmastra 3% (Nauni trial) vs 5% (KVK Solan) vs 2.5-3 L per 200 L (UHF-NF, which is about 1.25-1.5% by volume, my arithmetic); agniastra 3% vs 5%. The app should print the trial dose it cites, not a merged number.
- Khatti lassi 5% (KVK Solan) and 1:20 = 5% (Tribune, wheat) coincide arithmetically (1 L in 20 L is 1/21, about 4.8%, my arithmetic), which may support a 5% khatti lassi default, but the 10-20x dilution of aged lassi from NAHEP differs and its source is a snippet.
- Honest message for black rot, clubroot and Sclerotinia: natural-farming sprays are not documented to control them; use seed health, rotation, lime and sanitation.

### Gaps
- No official PK3Y / spnfhp.nic.in documents could be opened; dashparni, sonthastra, khatti lassi recipes and cole-specific doses are not retrieved.
- Darekastra recipe not found (only the 500 L/ha dose in a trial).
- No HP trial quantifying natural-farming sprays against Alternaria, downy mildew or black rot on cole crops.
- No data on timing of agniastra relative to harvest (waiting period) or phytotoxicity on cabbage heads.
- A UHF Nauni project on native botanical formulations for disease management in cauliflower was mentioned in an earlier note for the sibling crop group, but no retrievable record was found here.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional practices

### Takeaway
Documentation of HP-specific traditional practice for cole crops is thin: one NIF award record for a Kullu farmer who uses two undisclosed local plants with cow urine against insects and fungus in cabbage and cauliflower, and farmer natural-farming testimonies from Kinnaur. The NIF validation trial (BHU Varanasi) was on okra, not cole crops.

### Cited Findings
- Kishan Singh, Kullu, HP (age 38 at the 2019 awards): "makes use of two local plants along with cow urine for the control of insects and fungus in cabbage, cauliflower and other vegetables"; powdered roots and leaves (plant names withheld for IP reasons); "effective at low volumes and can be stored in cool dry place for 5-6 months"; knowledge gained from family elders - NIF-Kullu
- NIF-facilitated validation at BHU (okra, not cole crops): mite population reduced by 67.2% and 69.6% (first and second spray at farmer's dose), shoot borer by 59.63%, fruit borer by 58.1% and 64.55%, whitefly by 42.1% and 47.1%, leafhopper by 47.86%; 78.4% yield increase over control; no adverse effects on crop, beneficials or non-targets - NIF-Kullu
- Kinnaur farmers (Nako village; Kalpa block): natural farming after Palekar training in Kufri (Shimla) in 2016; Atma Ram harvested 1.5 q cabbage in 2022; woolly apple aphid controlled by dashparni ark spray; another farm with vegetables saw jivamrita and khatti lassi as effective against fungal diseases - UHF-NF (anecdotal)
- Cow urine used by farmers as an indigenous pest-control method, diluted up to 10%; fresh urine can harm leaves, fermented stock generally preferred; an ICAR-IVRI report found harmful bacteria in cow urine samples (relevant to handling) **[snippet; general]** (from the search summary; no HP-specific cole source)
- Mechanical practices endorsed by formal packages: hand-collecting caterpillars and egg masses on the underside of leaves, removing webbed leaves, bird perches, light traps - NIPHM-CC

### Inferences
- A generic IKS card, "fermented cow urine with locally available bitter or aromatic plants", is supported only by the Kullu NIF record and the brahmastra/neemastra recipes; neither the plants nor the dilution for the Kullu practice is disclosed, so the card should point to brahmastra/neemastra recipes with their documented doses instead.
- Hand-picking egg masses and caterpillars is effective against Pieris and Spodoptera because the larvae are large and gregarious early; it is much less practical for DBM (1-1.5 cm larvae, scattered) (inference from NIPHM-CC morphology).

### Gaps
- No ITK paper (Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge, Himachal Journal of Agricultural Research) on cole-crop pest management in HP could be retrieved; no ash dusting, pine-needle or marigold border data for cole crops in HP.
- No quantity/timing for any traditional practice; no efficacy data for traditional use on cabbage or cauliflower.
