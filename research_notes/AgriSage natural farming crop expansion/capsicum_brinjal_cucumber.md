## KQ1. Which 4-6 problems per crop matter in HP, what do they look like on the leaf/plant, and what weather favours them?

### Takeaway
The candidate list is largely confirmed, with HP evidence strongest for capsicum Phytophthora leaf blight/fruit rot (12.5-72.7% incidence across 37 HP locations), solanaceous bacterial wilt (90-100% incidence in worst fields in Solan, Shimla, Kangra), brinjal shoot and fruit borer (peak July-August in Kullu), and cucumber downy/powdery mildew plus Bactrocera fruit flies. Recommended vision classes: capsicum = Phytophthora blight, anthracnose/dieback, bacterial leaf spot, powdery mildew (Leveillula), Cercospora leaf spot, leaf-curl/mosaic complex (+ wilt as whole-plant class); brinjal = shoot and fruit borer, Phomopsis blight, little leaf, bacterial wilt, hadda beetle, jassid/mite stippling; cucurbits = downy mildew, powdery mildew, mosaic (CMV), red pumpkin beetle, leaf miner, fruit fly (fruit, not leaf).

### Cited Findings

**Capsicum / chilli**
- HP status of Phytophthora: leaf blight and fruit rot of capsicum caused by *Phytophthora nicotianae* var. *nicotianae*; 37 HP locations surveyed, disease at all of them; incidence 12.5-72.7%, severity 11.3-55.4%; maximum in Mandi and Kullu districts **[snippet]** — [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ article 29974](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/29974)
- *P. capsici* is also documented on sweet pepper in HP (Singh 2015; Chaudhary & Banyal 2013, cited within) — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Phytophthora blight field scale used by IIHR (useful for severity labels): 0 = no symptoms; 1 = leaves slightly wilted with black lesions beginning on stems or 10-29% of plant diseased; 2 = 30-49%; 3 = 50-69%; 4 = 70-90%; 5 = dead plant. The epidemic is driven by heavy rain and waterlogging; protective measures were timed until first leaf lesion at fruit-development stage — IIHR-Phytophthora
- National package lists as regionally significant for HP: die-back and fruit rot (*Colletotrichum capsici*), Fusarium wilt (*F. solani*), and leaf curl virus — NIPHM-Chilli
- Anthracnose/die-back symptoms: small circular to irregular brownish-black scattered leaf spots; severe leaf fall; necrosis of branches from the tip backwards; necrotic tissue greyish-white with black dot-like acervuli; on fruit, small circular yellowish-to-pinkish sunken spots that elongate into ellipses, acervuli in concentric rings, fruit turns straw-coloured; ripe fruits more vulnerable than green. Favoured by 28 °C with RH >97%, frequent rain; seed- and debris-borne, rain-splash spread — NIPHM-Chilli
- Bacterial leaf spot (*Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria*): circular water-soaked spots turning necrotic with brown centre and chlorotic border; enlarged spots develop straw-coloured centres; lesions slightly raised on lower surface; severely spotted leaves yellow and drop; raised brown lesions on fruit; narrow streaks on stems. Seed-borne; spread by rain splash; favoured by moderate temperature, high RH, intermittent rain — NIPHM-Chilli
- Powdery mildew (*Leveillula taurica*): white powdery coating mostly on the LOWER leaf surface with corresponding yellow patches on the upper surface; drying and shedding of leaves. Cool dry weather favours conidial germination; high RH favours development — NIPHM-Chilli
- Cercospora leaf spot (*C. capsici*): circular spots with brown margins and grey centre ("frog-eye"); centre turns white and may drop out; leaves yellow and defoliate; dark brown irregular stem lesions with whitish centres — NIPHM-Chilli
- Alternaria leaf spot: small black lesions on older leaves, enlarging to ≥1/4 inch with concentric "bull's-eye" rings and yellow halo; warm, rainy, wet weather — NIPHM-Chilli
- Leaf-curl complex look-alikes: (a) yellow mite — leaves crinkle and curl DOWNWARDS, petiole elongated, inverted-boat shape, dark green, clustering of young tip leaves ("murda"); (b) thrips — leaves curl UPWARD, crumble and shed, fruits show light brown scars; (c) mosaic (TMV) — raised blisters, light/dark green mottling, leaves point to ground; (CMV) — reduced, narrowed lamina, mosaic, downward curling along midrib, small distorted fruit — NIPHM-Chilli
- HP polyhouse capsicum (Kangra, Mandi, Kullu, Hamirpur, Bilaspur; surveys 2011-2014): virus-diseased plants showed mosaic, mottling, chlorosis, upward or downward leaf curling, leaf and fruit deformation and stunting **[snippet]** — [Himachal Journal of Agricultural Research via i-Scholar](https://www.i-scholar.in/index.php/Himachal/article/view/154493)
- Damping off (*Pythium aphanidermatum*; wire-stem with *Rhizoctonia*): seedling mortality 25-75%; water-soaked collar lesion, seedlings topple in patches. Pythium favoured by heavy rain, over-irrigation, poor drainage, close spacing, soil temperature 25-30 °C; Rhizoctonia by 30-35 °C — NIPHM-Chilli
- Fusarium wilt: vein clearing, chlorosis, drooping, vascular browning; high soil moisture and temperature — NIPHM-Chilli
- Bacterial wilt in HP: survey showed highest incidence (90-100%) at Solan, Shimla and Kangra in tomato cv. Manish, tomato cv. Lal Sona and brinjal cv. Arka Kusumkar respectively; of 37 *Ralstonia* isolates, 21 came from tomato, 7 from brinjal, 8 from bell pepper and 1 from chilli **[snippet]** — [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ article 17345](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/17345)

**Brinjal**
- Shoot and fruit borer (*Leucinodes orbonalis*): larva bores into tender shoots near the growing point and seals the hole with excreta → wilting, drying and drop of young shoots ("dead hearts"); fruits show bore holes plugged with frass, tunnels filled with frass — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Kullu valley, HP: incidence appears early in May and continues to season end, peak in July and August on shoots and fruits; crop transplanted on 15 May had the lowest mean shoot infestation (9.87%) **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis, Management of brinjal shoot and fruit borer in Kullu valley of HP](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810181826)
- Phomopsis blight and fruit rot: on leaves, circular to irregular clearly defined greyish-brown spots with light centres, black pycnidia on older spots, leaves yellow and drop; stem lesions dark brown with grey centres, constriction/canker at stem base and toppling; on fruit, pale sunken spots with concentric rings turning to watery soft rot dotted with pycnidia, or black mummified dry rot via calyx. Seed- and debris-borne; maximum development about 26 °C in wet weather with high RH — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Little leaf (phytoplasma; vector leafhopper *Hishimonus phycitis*): starts on one branch, then whole plant; tiny, narrow, soft, smooth, yellowish leaves with very short petioles appearing stuck to the stem; shortened internodes and proliferating axillary buds give a bushy look; flowers absent or green; fruit rare, hard, mummified. Reservoir weeds: *Datura*, *Catharanthus roseus*, *Argemone mexicana* — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Bacterial wilt (*Ralstonia solanacearum*): sudden wilting and death; petioles of older leaves droop (epinasty), yellowing, stunting; vascular browning; whitish bacterial ooze from cut stem. Seed- and soil-borne; spread by irrigation water, soil, implements; favoured by high soil moisture, moderate soil temperature, continuous solanaceous cropping — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Hadda/epilachna beetle: grubs and adults scrape chlorophyll leaving ladder-like "windows" that dry and drop, then papery skeletonised leaves; all life stages sit exposed on the leaf — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Jassid/leafhopper: yellow spots, then crinkling, curling, bronzing and drying ("hopper burn"), feeding from lower surface — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Red spider mite (*Tetranychus*): white or yellow speckles, later leaf desiccation and webbing — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Whitefly/aphid: yellowing, honeydew and sooty mould; aphids curl and deform young leaves — NIPHM-Brinjal

**Cucumber / cucurbits**
- HP evidence for mildews: in a low-hill HP screening, downy mildew disease index ranged 9.53-52.77 and powdery mildew 6.78-36.66 across cucumber genotypes **[snippet]** — [IJEP, Screening of cucumber genotypes under low hill regions of HP](https://ojs.pphouse.org/index.php/IJEP/article/view/6452)
- Polyhouse cucumber in low-hill HP: downy mildew incidence 15-20% **[snippet]** — [Protected cultivation of cucumber under low hill conditions of HP (AGRIS)](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df7d61b766d82b1801ff4f)
- Downy mildew (*Pseudoperonospora cubensis*): yellow ANGULAR spots limited by veins on the upper surface, resembling mosaic mottling; purplish downy growth on the corresponding lower surface in moist weather; spots turn necrotic. Favoured by RH >90%, high soil moisture, frequent rain; wind and rain-splash sporangia — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Powdery mildew (*Erysiphe cichoracearum*, *Sphaerotheca fuliginea*): whitish to dirty-grey powdery growth on BOTH foliage surfaces, stems and young parts, eventually covering the whole leaf, then browning and drying. Favoured by morning RH >90% with cool dry weather — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Cucumber mosaic virus: mosaic on youngest expanding leaves; leaves curl downward, mottled, distorted, wrinkled, small; shortened internodes (bunchy veins); fruit misshapen, mottled, warty. Aphid-transmitted (*Aphis craccivora*, *Myzus persicae*); many weed and crop reservoirs — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Cercospora leaf spot: water-soaked specks becoming circular-irregular spots with pale brown/tan/white centre and purple to black margin, coalescing to scorch — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Fusarium wilt: vein clearing, chlorosis, drooping, vascular browning; high soil moisture and temperature — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Red pumpkin beetle (*Raphidopalpa/Aulacophora foveicollis*): adults bite numerous holes in leaves, cotyledons and flowers; early-sown cucurbits can need resowing; grubs feed on roots and fruits touching soil — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Serpentine leaf miner (*Liriomyza trifolii*): leaves with serpentine mines; drying and drop in severe cases; warm weather — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Fruit fly: maggots bore into fruit, rot from secondary bacteria, premature drop; damage greater in the monsoon — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- HP fruit-fly species from cue-lure trapping on vegetables: *Bactrocera cucurbitae*, *B. tau*, *B. scutellaris* **[snippet]** — [Indian Journal of Plant Protection, IJPP article 105823](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJPP/article/view/105823)
- Aphid *Aphis gossypii* is listed as regionally significant on cucurbits for HP — NIPHM-Cucurbits

### Inferences
- Critical look-alike pairs for the vision model: (1) capsicum powdery mildew (yellow blotch above, white growth BELOW) vs. cucurbit powdery mildew (white growth on TOP surface); a capsicum leaf must be photographed from below. (2) Cucurbit downy mildew (angular, vein-bounded yellow) vs. CMV mosaic (non-necrotic mottling with distortion) vs. angular leaf spot. (3) Capsicum bacterial spot (small, raised below, chlorotic halo) vs. Cercospora (grey/white centre "frog-eye") vs. early anthracnose spots. (4) Mite (down-curl, elongated petiole) vs. thrips (up-curl) vs. virus (mosaic + distortion). (5) Bacterial wilt (sudden, green wilting) vs. Fusarium wilt (slow, yellowing from lower leaves) vs. Phytophthora collar rot (black stem lesion).
- Wilt, little leaf and shoot-borer dead-heart are whole-plant/shoot classes, not single-leaf classes; the app should request a whole-plant photo for them.
- HP seasonality: monsoon (July-September) is the high-risk window for Phytophthora, anthracnose, bacterial spot, downy mildew, fruit fly and brinjal borer; cool-dry post-monsoon and polyhouse conditions favour powdery mildews and mites.

### Gaps
- No HP altitude-wise incidence data retrieved for any of these problems.
- Angular leaf spot (*Pseudomonas syringae* pv. *lachrymans*) and cucurbit anthracnose are not described in the national cucurbit package and no HP source was retrieved; their HP importance is unconfirmed.
- HP-specific incidence for capsicum powdery mildew, bacterial leaf spot and Cercospora not retrieved.
- The Indian Phytopathology abstracts were behind a login page; numbers above are from search summaries only.

## KQ2. Cultural practices (beds, drainage, mulch, solarization, rotation, sanitation, grafting, varieties, barrier and trap crops)

### Takeaway
The best-documented cultural package is raised beds plus plastic mulch plus drainage for Phytophthora (15-22 cm high beds, silver-black mulch) and nursery hygiene (solarization, pro-trays, raised nursery beds >15 cm). For viruses, phytoplasma and bacterial wilt, the sources offer only prevention: barrier crops, rogueing, weed removal, rotation, resistant varieties and grafting.

### Cited Findings

**Common nursery/field hygiene (all three crops)**
- Soil solarization of nursery beds: "polythene sheet of 45 gauge (0.45 mm) thickness for three weeks before sowing" — NIPHM-Chilli; identical wording in NIPHM-Brinjal and NIPHM-Cucurbits. (The gauge and mm figures in the source do not agree with each other; quote as printed and verify.)
- For bacterial wilt nurseries: solarization with a transparent polyethylene sheet 125 µm thick for 8-10 weeks during March-June — NIPHM-Brinjal
- An HP study exists on soil solarization vs. *Ralstonia* population and tomato bacterial wilt (title only retrieved; no numbers) — [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ article 18071](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/18071)
- Raised beds more than 15 cm high for drainage; raise seedlings in pro-trays/plug trays with sterilised potting mix; avoid excess watering and poorly drained spots — NIPHM-Chilli; NIPHM-Brinjal
- Deep summer ploughing and 2-3 weeks of sun exposure (May-June) — NIPHM-Chilli; NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Black LDPE mulch 30 micron between rows, edges buried 10 cm, with drip irrigation — NIPHM-Chilli; NIPHM-Brinjal

**Capsicum / chilli**
- Phytophthora integrated package (Bengaluru, kharif 2021, open field): raised bed 90-100 cm wide and 15-22 cm high with silver-black reflective mulch film (30-100 micron), soil drainage to prevent waterlogging in heavy rain, removal of low-lying or soil-touching diseased leaves, weed-host removal, staking on GI wire with 6-ft bamboo — IIHR-Phytophthora
- HP package of Singh (2015) for *P. capsici* in sweet pepper included mulching plus neem cake and *Trichoderma* soil application alongside fungicides (as summarised by IIHR authors) — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Anthracnose/die-back: pathogen-free seed, early removal of affected plants, no water stagnation in nursery or field, good drainage, remove infected debris, control weeds and solanaceous volunteers near nurseries; note turmeric intercrop is another host — NIPHM-Chilli
- Mosaic/leaf curl: healthy seed, rotation with non-hosts, control perennial weed hosts, rogue and destroy infected plants early, 4 rows of maize or sorghum as border crop against aphid vectors, cover seed bed with nylon net or paddy straw — NIPHM-Chilli
- Mites: chilli bordered by two rows of maize at every 0.5 acre (31.2 x 60 sq m) — NIPHM-Chilli
- Thrips: intercrop with *Sesbania grandiflora* as barrier; do not mix chilli with onion; sprinkle water over seedlings to check thrips multiplication — NIPHM-Chilli
- Intercropping chilli with maize, sorghum and cowpea at 10:3:1, or 2-3 border rows of sorghum/maize/bajra, for thrips **[snippet]** — [DPPQS advisory on chilli thrips](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/advisory_on_chilli_thrips.pdf)
- Fruit borer trap crop: marigold 100 plants/acre, 1 row per 18 rows of chilli, using 45-day-old marigold seedlings planted with the chilli; castor as border trap crop for *Spodoptera*; basil (*Ocimum*) as repellent — NIPHM-Chilli
- Bacterial leaf spot: field sanitation, seed from disease-free plants and healthy fruits (standard recommendation is the antibiotic streptocycline, not a natural option) — NIPHM-Chilli
- Choanephora wet rot: recommended spacing for air circulation — NIPHM-Chilli
- Resistant/tolerant chilli varieties (national list): thrips and mites — Pusa Jwala, Phule Jyoti; viral diseases and leaf curl — Pusa Sadabahar, Arka Harita, Arka Meghana, Arka Sweta, Hisar Shakti, Hisar Vijay, Pant C-1; powdery mildew — Arka Suphal, Arka Harita, Arka Meghana, Hisar Shakti, Hisar Vijay, Phule Mukta; fruit rot — Hisar Shakti, Hisar Vijay, TNAU Chilli Hybrid Co 1; Fusarium wilt — Phule Jyoti, Phule Mukta — NIPHM-Chilli
- In the IIHR trial all four sweet pepper genotypes (Arka Athulya, Arka Mohini, Indra, California Wonder) were equally susceptible to Phytophthora blight — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Bell pepper bacterial-wilt genetics work at CSK HPKV Palampur used EC 464107, EC 464115, Kandaghat Selection and Sweet Happy I as resistance/susceptibility standards (the summary does not say which is which) **[snippet]** — [Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences article 53759](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/53759)

**Brinjal**
- Shoot and fruit borer: avoid continuous brinjal/potato cropping; promptly remove and destroy infested shoots and fruits at regular intervals until final harvest; intercrop 2 rows brinjal with 1 row coriander or fennel; avoid ratooning; uproot and burn old plants before the new crop — NIPHM-Brinjal
- AVRDC South Asia strategy: (1) field sanitation to stop carry-over, (2) prompt excising and disposal of damaged shoots with larvae inside all season, (3) sex-pheromone traps, (4) withholding insecticides to let native natural enemies build up; pilots in Jessore (Bangladesh), Gujarat and Uttar Pradesh reduced damage below economically tolerable levels (not in Sri Lanka); pesticide use dropped by up to 75% **[snippet]** — [AVRDC/ECHO summary](https://www.echocommunity.org/resources/83ba0af0-5da2-4299-aba2-0a2412f44507); [DFID output page](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/implementation-and-promotion-of-an-ipm-strategy-for-control-of-eggplant-fruit-and-shoot-borer-in-south-asia)
- Kullu, HP: early transplanting (15 May) gave the lowest shoot infestation (9.87%) **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810181826)
- Phomopsis: seed from healthy fruits plus hot-water seed treatment at 50 °C for 30 minutes; avoid continuous brinjal; rotate with non-solanaceous crops; summer deep ploughing; destroy diseased debris; resistant varieties — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Bacterial wilt: resistant varieties; rotation with non-solanaceous crops (maize, soybean, wheat, rice, sesame) and green manuring; *Brassica* green manure as biofumigation; solarization (above) — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Little leaf: destroy affected plants in early stages; eradicate solanaceous weed hosts; plant tolerant varieties; vector control (no curative measure given) — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Leafhopper: okra as trap crop; remove affected parts — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Mites: nurseries away from infested crops; avoid water and nutrient stress; mulch and organic matter; uproot and burn the first few infested plants; keep field weed-free — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Thrips: raise seedlings under 50-64 mesh insect-proof net; mulch and reflective materials reduce *Thrips palmi* — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Resistant/tolerant brinjal varieties (national list): bacterial wilt — Pusa Purple Cluster, Pusa Anupama, Arka Anand, Arka Keshav, Arka Nidhi, Arka Neelkanth, JC-1, JC-2, Pant Samrat, Pant Brinjal Hybrid 1; shoot and fruit borer — HLB-12, JC-1, GBH-1, JC-2, Pant Brinjal Hybrid 1, PPI 1; Phomopsis — JC-1, JC-2, Pant Samrat, Pant Brinjal Hybrid 1, Azad B-2; little leaf — JC-1, JC-2 — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Arka Nidhi, Arka Keshav, Arka Neelkanth and SM 6-6 were resistant to bacterial wilt in IIHR screening **[snippet]** — [Journal of Horticultural Sciences article 158](https://jhs.iihr.res.in/index.php/jhs/article/view/158)
- Swarna Shyamli and Swarna Pratibha: wilt-resistant brinjal varieties released for Zone I (HP, J&K, Uttarakhand) **[snippet]** — [ICAR Krishi record 75164](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/75164)
- Grafting: cultivated brinjal grafted on wild *Solanum torvum* showed a resistant reaction to bacterial wilt **[snippet]** — [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ article 60167](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/60167)

**Cucumber / cucurbits**
- Downy mildew: trellis cucumbers; avoid overhead irrigation or irrigate only in late morning to shorten leaf wetness; control alternate weed hosts (wild cucumber, volunteer cucumbers) on field edges — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Powdery mildew: resistant varieties; increase air movement inside the canopy — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- CMV: 4 rows of barrier crop (maize or sorghum); avoid planting next to tomato, spinach and other susceptible crops; aphid control — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Fusarium wilt: pathogen-free seed, remove infected plants and debris, rotation, drainage, resistant varieties — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Red pumpkin beetle: deep summer ploughing exposes grubs and pupae — NIPHM-Cucurbits; plough just after harvest to destroy hibernating adults; collect and destroy adult beetles — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/cucurbitae/cucurbitae_2.html)
- Leaf miner: change sowing dates; castor, tomato or marigold as trap crop (table layout in source is ambiguous; this entry sits in the leaf-miner row) — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Fruit fly: early-maturing varieties, change sowing date, collect and destroy infested fruits, light raking of soil during fruiting and after harvest to expose pupae — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Resistant/tolerant: cucumber powdery mildew — Phule Shubhangi; bottle gourd anthracnose, powdery mildew, downy mildew, viral disease — Narendra Shishir (NDBG-202); pumpkin fruit fly — Arka Suryamukhi; bitter gourd downy mildew — Phule Green Gold — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- HP: seven cucumber genotypes (LC-C-4-21, LC-C-6-21, LC-C-7-21, LC-C-9-21, LC-C-12-21, LC-C-14-21 and Solan Srijan) were resistant to downy mildew; LC-C-9-21 and Solan Srijan recommended for low-hill HP **[snippet]** — [IJEP article 6452](https://ojs.pphouse.org/index.php/IJEP/article/view/6452)

### Inferences
- Solan Srijan (a UHF Nauni cucumber) is the only HP-released variety in these notes with a retrieved resistance claim; all other variety names are national lists and must be checked against HP availability before going on a card.
- Raised-bed height guidance converges: >15 cm (NIPHM) and 15-22 cm (IIHR). A card can safely say "15-20 cm or higher".
- For cucurbits the downy-mildew cultural measures (trellis, morning irrigation, airflow) are the same ones that reduce anthracnose and angular leaf spot, since all need leaf wetness; this is an inference, not a cited trial.

### Gaps
- Pine-needle or other organic mulch on capsicum/brinjal/cucurbits in HP: no source retrieved.
- UHF Nauni and CSK HPKV package-of-practices variety lists (e.g., HP capsicum and brinjal varieties with stated resistances) could not be opened.
- No HP data on capsicum grafting for bacterial wilt; the only capsicum rootstock result found was Brazilian (BRS Acará etc.) and is not applicable.
- Hisar Shyamal brinjal was reportedly evaluated in HP mid-hills (1996-98) for bacterial wilt and borer, but the result was not retrievable.
- Polythene specification for solarization in the NIPHM text is internally inconsistent ("45 gauge (0.45 mm)").

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
Verified doses exist for *Trichoderma* (seed 5 g/kg; root dip 10 g/L for 15 min; nursery drench 250 g/50 L per 400 m²; soil 1 kg in 25 kg FYM per acre), *Trichogramma* (brinjal borer 20,000/acre/week; chilli fruit borer 50,000/acre/week), pheromone traps (brinjal borer 10/acre for mass trapping) and sticky traps (4-5 to 10 per acre). Efficacy evidence against Phytophthora is partial: the bioagent-plus-raised-bed-plus-mulch package still relied on fungicides.

### Cited Findings

**Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Bacillus**
- *Trichoderma viride* 1% WP seed treatment 5 g/kg seed (thin paste, shade dry just before sowing) — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Seedling root dip: 10 g *T. viride* 1% WP per litre of water, dip roots 15 minutes — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Nursery: 250 g *T. viride* 1% WP in 50 L water drenched over 400 m² — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Main field: 1 kg *T. viride* 1% WP mixed with 25 kg FYM, broadcast over one acre, irrigate immediately (also listed for bacterial wilt along with neem cake) — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Cucurbits: *Trichoderma* spp. 2.5 kg/acre with FYM; neem or pongamia cake 100 kg/acre at last ploughing — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Chilli package: "Trichoderma as seed and nursery treatment and *Pseudomonas fluorescens* as seed, nursery treatment and soil application" is endorsed, but no dose is printed — NIPHM-Chilli
- Phytophthora blight (IIHR): seed treatment with *T. harzianum* Th-2 + *Bacillus subtilis* BS-2 at 10 g/kg seed; nursery cocopeat enrichment; FYM plus neem cake (18% oil) bio-enriched with the same two agents (1 kg bioagent per 100 kg neem cake) applied to the planting bed at 600 kg/acre — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Pot tests vs. *P. capsici* collar rot: suppression ranged 33.61-68.90%; *T. harzianum* Th-2 68.90%, *B. subtilis* BS-2 66.38%; *Pseudomonas fluorescens* IIHR-PF-2 showed no in-vitro efficacy; untreated inoculated control had 97.94 PDI — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Field result of the whole improved package (bioagents + raised bed + reflective mulch + drainage + sanitation + need-based fungicides): blight severity 28.40-30.19% vs. 48.43-49.21% under existing practice vs. 73.36-75.17% untreated; mean marketable yield 28.06 vs. 16.53 vs. 6.64 t/ha — IIHR-Phytophthora
- HP (Singh 2015, as cited): neem cake plus *T. harzianum* at 2.5 kg per 50 kg FYM per ha, with mulching, combined with fungicide sprays — IIHR-Phytophthora
- *T. harzianum* reduces the rate of progress of capsicum collar rot; *T. viride* reported effective against bell pepper blight **[snippet]** — [Phytophthora capsici on Capsicum, IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/81665)
- Neem cake 100 kg/acre at transplanting against nematodes and borer damage; in chilli beds in two splits (planting and 30 days after transplanting) for thrips — NIPHM-Chilli; NIPHM-Brinjal
- Root-knot nematode: 2 t FYM enriched with 100-200 kg neem or pongamia cake in the main field — NIPHM-Brinjal

**Brinjal shoot and fruit borer**
- Pheromone traps: 4-5/acre for monitoring and 10/acre for mass trapping, 10 m apart, from 20 days after transplanting; change septa regularly; place at or slightly above canopy — NIPHM-Brinjal
- *Trichogramma chilonis* 20,000/acre/week from 21 days after transplanting to crop end; tie Trichocards on sticks 4-5 m apart in the evening, one day before adult emergence — NIPHM-Brinjal
- IIHR bio-intensive module: pheromone traps 1 per 400 m²; weekly release of 50,000-60,000 *T. chilonis* per ha; two sprays of Bt at 1 ml/L at 10-day intervals at peak (50%) flowering — [IIHR technology note, India Science & Technology portal](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/technologies/biological-control-brinjal-shoot-and-fruit-borer)
- Entomopathogenic nematodes: 1.0 crore infective juveniles of *Steinernema glaseri* per acre — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Light trap (with exit for small natural enemies) 1/acre, run 6-10 pm, for stem borer — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Natural enemies to conserve: *Trathala flavo-orbitalis*, *Eriborus argenteopilosus*, *Bracon greeni*, *Pristomerus testaceus*; for hadda beetle *Pediobius foveolatus*, *Tetrastichus ovularum* (active March-July) — NIPHM-Brinjal

**Capsicum / chilli insects and mites**
- *Trichogramma pretiosum* 50,000 adults/acre/week from flower initiation to crop end for fruit borer (*Helicoverpa*) — NIPHM-Chilli
- Bt (printed as "B.t. var gallariae") 600-800 g in 400 L water/acre against *Spodoptera* — NIPHM-Chilli
- Blue sticky traps for thrips and yellow sticky/pan traps for whitefly at 4-5 traps/acre, 15 cm above canopy; home-made: tins painted yellow and coated with grease, Vaseline or castor oil — NIPHM-Chilli
- Pheromone traps 4-5/acre for *Helicoverpa* and *Spodoptera*, lures replaced every 2-3 weeks; light trap 1/acre; bird perches — NIPHM-Chilli
- Conserve predatory mites (*Amblyseius* spp.) and *Orius* bugs for thrips and mites — NIPHM-Chilli
- HP vegetable surveys recorded 25 mite species, of which 19 were predatory and 5 phytophagous; an anystid predatory mite was recorded on capsicum for the first time from HP **[snippet]** — [Journal of Biological Control article](https://informaticsjournals.com/index.php/jbc/article/download/34862/29610/87116)

**Brinjal sucking pests**
- Aphids: release first-instar *Chrysoperla carnea* 4,000/acre — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Yellow sticky/pan traps 10-12/ha for whitefly monitoring; blue for thrips — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Mites: conserve predatory mites (*Amblyseius* spp., *Phytoseiulus persimilis*), *Stethorus*, *Oligota*; perennial hedges such as pigeon pea are said to encourage predatory mites — NIPHM-Brinjal

**Cucurbits**
- Leaf miner: yellow sticky traps or cards 10/acre; conserve parasitoids (*Diglyphus*, *Chrysocharis*, *Neochrysocharis formosa*, *Opius phaseoli*) — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Aphids (CMV vector): first-instar *Chrysoperla carnea* 10,000/acre — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Fruit fly: cue-lure traps at 16/ha installed at flowering, plus weekly field sanitation and a 5% black jaggery bait, was the best module for melon fruit fly on cucumber; the bait in that module carried spinosad 0.004% at 8 L/ha, four sprays from fruit set **[snippet]** — [Plant Archives, Management of melon fruit fly infesting cucumber](https://www.plantarchives.org/SPL%20ISSUE%2020-2/211__1323-1328_.pdf)
- CSK HPKV Palampur evaluated locally available raw materials as bait against *B. tau* on cucumber (bait application technique traps both sexes); in laboratory oviposition-deterrence tests azadirachtin 0.015%, neem oil 3%, "Daman" 1% and clay 10% were effective **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis, Diversity and management of fruit flies in cucurbits](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810194783)
- The national package prints "methyl eugenol (0.1%) based trap" for cucurbit fruit fly — NIPHM-Cucurbits; HP studies used cue-lure — [IJPP article 105823](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJPP/article/view/105823)
- Red pumpkin beetle natural enemies listed: parasitoid *Celatoria setosa*; entomopathogenic nematodes shown but no dose — NIPHM-Cucurbits

### Inferences
- Unit conversion for cards: 16 cue-lure traps/ha is about 6-7 per acre; IIHR's 1 pheromone trap per 400 m² equals 25/ha or about 10/acre, matching NIPHM's mass-trapping rate.
- Cue-lure, not methyl eugenol, is the appropriate lure for *B. cucurbitae* and *B. tau*; the NIPHM cucurbit line appears to be an error and should not be copied to a card.
- Spinosad is a fermentation-derived insecticide; whether the app treats it as "natural" is a product decision. A jaggery bait without toxicant has no retrieved efficacy number.
- Against Phytophthora the bioagent package roughly halved severity relative to the grower standard but only together with fungicide sprays; a bioagent-plus-cultural-only card should be framed as risk reduction, not cure.

### Gaps
- No dose retrieved for *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (seed, dip or spray), *Bacillus subtilis* foliar sprays, *Ampelomyces quisqualis*, *Beauveria*, *Lecanicillium*, *Metarhizium* or predatory-mite releases on these crops. Do not print doses for these from this file.
- No HP field trial retrieved for *Trichoderma*/*Pseudomonas* sprays against cucumber downy or powdery mildew.
- Bt dose for brinjal borer is given only as "1 ml/L" of an unspecified formulation.
- "Leucin-lure" brand density not separately sourced; rates above are generic *Leucinodes* pheromone traps.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
NSKE 5% is the single most widely endorsed botanical across all three crops; other verified concentrations are neem seed powder extract 4% (chilli mites), neem oil 2 ml/L (brinjal budworm), neem oil 3% and azadirachtin 0.015% (fruit-fly oviposition deterrence, lab), and cow's milk 10% or stronger (cucurbit powdery mildew). No concentration for garlic-chilli extract, karanj oil, whey or soap was found in an opened source.

### Cited Findings
- NSKE 5% against *Spodoptera* eggs and first-instar larvae on chilli — NIPHM-Chilli
- Neem seed powder extract 4% at 10-day intervals when mite incidence is low on chilli — NIPHM-Chilli
- NSKE 5% for brinjal hadda beetle, leafhopper, mites and shoot and fruit borer; neem oil 2 ml/L for budworm — NIPHM-Brinjal
- NSKE 5% for red pumpkin beetle, serpentine leaf miner and fruit fly on cucurbits — NIPHM-Cucurbits
- IIHR used azadirachtin 1% EC (10,000 ppm) at 3 ml/L and neem soap at 10 g/L against sucking pests of sweet pepper (as background sprays, alongside synthetic insecticides) — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Fruit fly (*B. tau*) oviposition deterrence in the laboratory: azadirachtin 0.015%, then neem oil 3%, "Daman" 1%, clay 10% **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810194783)
- Milk for cucurbit powdery mildew (Bettiol, Brazil, zucchini in greenhouse): milk at 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50% tested once or twice weekly; 10% and above twice weekly controlled as well as conventional fungicides; once weekly needed 20-50%; a weekly ≥10% spray (1 part milk : 9 parts water) reduced severity by about 90%; above 30% a harmless fungus grew on leaves, so 10-30% is the practical range **[snippet]** — [Bettiol summary, agrar.de](https://www.agrar.de/agenda/bettiol.htm)
- A contrary view exists that milk spray "doesn't work" (commercial knowledge page, not opened) — [Vegalab](https://www.vegalab.com/crop-science/knowledge/myths/milk-spray)
- Kullu, HP brinjal borer: among organic products, cow urine 10% was reported effective (with Agniastra 5% and Brahmastra 5%); chlorantraniliprole 0.01% was the most effective overall **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810181826)
- Chilli powdery mildew: the national package gives only chemical options, including sulphur 80% WP at 1252 g in 300-400 L water/acre (mineral, generally allowed in organic standards) — NIPHM-Chilli
- Organic cucurbit mildew products abroad include botanical oils, potassium bicarbonate, copper and sulphur; powdery mildew is managed more effectively with organic products than downy mildew **[snippet]** — [Growing Produce, Mildew control in organic cucurbits](https://www.growingproduce.com/?p=34131)

### Inferences
- Milk at 10% (1:9) weekly is defensible for cucurbit powdery mildew cards with a "preventive / early-stage" label; the evidence is a greenhouse trial on a different mildew genus from capsicum's *Leveillula*, so do not transfer the claim to capsicum without a caveat.
- Downy mildew has no botanical with retrieved efficacy; the card should lead with cultural measures and resistant varieties.

### Gaps
- NSKE 5% preparation recipe (kg kernel per litre, soaking time) was not in the opened texts.
- No verified concentration for garlic-chilli extract, karanj/pongamia oil, whey, sour buttermilk as a stand-alone botanical, or soap solution on these crops.
- No Indian (let alone HP) trial of milk or whey on cucurbit or capsicum powdery mildew retrieved.
- Percent pest reduction for NSKE on any of these crops was not retrieved; the packages only list it.

## KQ5. Natural-farming (SPNF/ZBNF) formulations: recipes, trial results, limits, and the tobacco caution

### Takeaway
Recipes for beejamrit, jeevamrit, neemastra, agniastra and brahmastra are documented by UHF Nauni authors, but quantitative efficacy on capsicum, brinjal or cucurbit diseases is almost absent: the only HP number-bearing lead is the Kullu brinjal-borer thesis (Agniastra 5%, Brahmastra 5%, cow urine 10% "effective", inferior to the synthetic standard). No natural-farming document I could open carries a tobacco mosaic virus caution, yet plant-pathology sources say tobacco products can carry TMV to pepper, tomato and eggplant; agniastra (500 g tobacco powder) should be flagged on solanaceous cards.

### Cited Findings

**Recipes (UHF Nauni, Indian Farming 2023)**
- Beejamrit: 5 kg cow dung tied in cloth and hung in 20 L water for up to 12 h; 50 g lime in 1 L water left overnight; squeeze the dung bundle into the water thrice; add a handful of farm soil, 5 L desi cow urine and the lime water; coat seeds, dry and sow — UHF-NF
- Jeevamrit: 10 kg cow dung + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg pulse flour + a handful of bund soil, made up to 200 L with water, fermented in shade for 48 h; described as preventing fungal and bacterial plant diseases (no data given) — UHF-NF
- Neemastra: 100 L water + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg cow dung + 5 kg neem twigs with leaves; cover with gunny bag in shade 48 h; stir clockwise 1 minute morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sucking insects, small caterpillars — UHF-NF
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves + 500 g tobacco powder + 500 g green chilli paste + 250 g garlic paste in 20 L cow urine, boiled on low flame under a lid, then kept 48 h with stirring morning and evening; for sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (spray dilution not stated in this article) — UHF-NF
- Brahmastra (1 acre): leaf paste of any 5 plants (darek, custard apple, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta, etc.) at 200 g per plant, in 4 L cow urine, heated to boil, cooled 48 h; spray 2.5-3 L in 200 L water; for sucking insects and caterpillars — UHF-NF
- Mulching (acchadana) and whapasa are named as pillars; target soil microclimate 25-32 °C and 65-72% moisture — UHF-NF

**Recipe variants from other sources (they differ; do not merge)**
- Neemastra (TNAU): 5 kg neem leaves crushed in water + 5 L cow urine + 2 kg cow dung, ferment 24 h with intermittent stirring, filter and dilute to 100 L for one acre; for sucking insects and mealy bugs — [TNAU Agritech organic plant protection](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_ofk_pltprotection.html)
- Brahmastra (TNAU): 3 kg neem leaves in 10 L cow urine + 2 kg each of custard apple, papaya, pomegranate and guava leaves; boil 5 times until reduced by half; rest 24 h; filter; stores up to 6 months; dilute 2-2.5 L to 100 L for 1 acre; for sucking insects and pod/fruit borers — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_ofk_pltprotection.html)
- Dashparni extract (TNAU): fermented one month in a 500 L drum, shaded and covered, for one acre (ingredient list not captured) — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_ofk_pltprotection.html)
- Sour (fermented) buttermilk: described as a repellent and antifeedant that "cures many plant diseases"; only 15-20-day-old solution diluted 10-20 times should be used, to avoid phytotoxicity on tender leaves **[snippet; fetch of the page failed]** — [ICAR-NAHEP, Cow based bioformulations for pest management](https://nahep.icar.gov.in/KMS/UploadKC1/b06b7c48-1f85-4697-a602-6b0f34c7fa74/Cow%20based%20bioformulations%20for%20pest%20management.pdf)
- Neemastra 200 L variant (2 kg dung, 10 L urine, 10 kg neem leaf paste, 48 h) and Brahmastra at 6-8 L in 100-200 L water per acre **[snippet; exact source page among the results not confirmed]** — [Morung Express, Pest and disease management in natural farming](https://morungexpress.com/pest-and-disease-management-in-natural-farming)

**Trial and field evidence on these crops**
- Kullu, HP, brinjal shoot and fruit borer: among organic products Agniastra 5%, Brahmastra 5% and cow urine 10% were effective; the synthetic chlorantraniliprole 0.01% was the most effective treatment **[snippet; no infestation percentages retrieved]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810181826)
- UHF Nauni, capsicum, kharif 2016: 90% recommended nutrients + PGPR + liquid manures (panchagavya, jeevamrit, amritpani) gave the highest fruit yield, 26.96 t/ha; this is a nutrition trial, not disease control **[snippet]** — [Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry 2020](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020.v9.i1.10652/yield-and-fruit-quality-response-of-pgpr-bulky-and-folk-liquid-manures-on-capsicum-capsicum-annuum-l-in-western-himalayas-of-india)
- HP farmer testimony (Kinnaur/Shimla success stories): "Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases" on a farm growing apple, peas, cucumber, beans; anecdotal, no measurements — UHF-NF
- UHF Nauni agromet advisories tell natural-farming growers to spray Agniastra, Brahmastra and Neemastra for insect pests **[snippet]** — [UHF Gramin Krishi Mausam Sewa bulletin](https://uhf.ac.in/storage/uploads/docs/1785233552.pdf)
- A project titled "Application of native botanical formulations for disease management in tomato, capsicum and cauliflower" at UHF Nauni appears in search results, but the linked page showed no such content — [link returned by search](https://www.wii.gov.in/himachal-pradesh-project4)

**Where efficacy is low or only prevention exists**
- Viruses: only healthy seed, rotation, weed-host control, early rogueing, tolerant varieties, barrier crops and vector suppression are offered — NIPHM-Chilli; NIPHM-Cucurbits
- Little leaf phytoplasma: destroy affected plants early, remove solanaceous weeds, tolerant varieties, vector control — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Bacterial wilt: resistant varieties, rotation, biofumigation, solarization, neem cake, *Trichoderma* in FYM; no curative spray — NIPHM-Brinjal
- Phytophthora blight: even the improved integrated package left 28-30% severity and depended on fungicide sprays; all tested genotypes were susceptible — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Standard HP recommendations for capsicum Phytophthora are chemical (metalaxyl + mancozeb, cymoxanil + mancozeb giving 57.6% leaf-blight and 67.5% fruit-rot control, copper oxychloride 0.3%) — IIHR-Phytophthora
- Bacterial leaf spot: standard recommendation is streptocycline; non-chemical options are limited to clean seed and sanitation — NIPHM-Chilli

**Tobacco / TMV caution**
- TMV on chilli is "externally seed borne" and "mechanically transmitted" — NIPHM-Chilli
- Tobacco products, especially air-cured tobacco, can carry TMV; the virus infects pepper, tomato and eggplant and spreads through wounds and handling; flue-curing heat usually inactivates it **[snippet]** — [Missouri Botanical Garden, Tobacco mosaic virus](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/gardens-gardening/your-garden/help-for-the-home-gardener/advice-tips-resources/insects-pests-and-problems/diseases/viruses/tobacco-mosaic-virus); [Plantix, Tobacco Mosaic Virus](https://plantix.net/en/library/plant-diseases/200037/tobacco-mosaic-virus/)
- The UHF Nauni agniastra recipe uses 500 g tobacco powder and carries no TMV warning — UHF-NF

### Inferences
- Agniastra is boiled during preparation, which may reduce but is not shown to eliminate TMV (the virus is notably heat-stable); with no source testing this, a conservative card rule is: on capsicum, chilli, brinjal (and tomato) prefer neemastra or brahmastra, or make agniastra without tobacco, and wash hands after handling tobacco.
- Dashparni recipes commonly include tobacco as one ingredient, but I did not retrieve an ingredient list, so the same caution is suggested without a citation.
- Natural-farming sprays should be presented as preventive and suppressive; for Phytophthora, bacterial wilt, viruses and little leaf the honest message is prevention plus rogueing.
- Sour buttermilk dilution guidance differs between sources (10-20x dilution of aged lassi per NAHEP snippet; PK3Y practice not verified); do not print a single HP dose until spnfhp.nic.in material is checked.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed HP trial quantifying jeevamrit, khatti lassi, neemastra, agniastra, brahmastra or dashparni against capsicum Phytophthora, anthracnose, powdery mildew, bacterial spot, cucurbit mildews or brinjal Phomopsis was found.
- PK3Y/SPNF official package (spnfhp.nic.in) doses for khatti lassi, sonthastra, dashparni: not retrieved.
- Agniastra spray dilution is missing from the UHF article; the Kullu thesis used 5% but percent reduction values were not retrieved.
- ICAR-IIHR/IIVR "organic modules" for these crops: none retrieved beyond the IIHR bio-intensive borer module and Phytophthora package.
- No Indian source explicitly testing TMV carry-over through tobacco-based natural-farming sprays was found.

## KQ6. Documented traditional / indigenous (IKS) practices from HP and the Himalaya

### Takeaway
Documentation specific to HP for these three crop groups is thin: one National Innovation Foundation record of a Kullu farmer's cow-urine-plus-local-plants spray for vegetables, plus ash dusting for red pumpkin beetle documented from other Indian regions and botanical/"jholmol" practices from the Nepal mid-hills. Nothing retrieved gives measured efficacy.

### Cited Findings
- Kullu, HP: a farmer uses two local plants with cow urine against insects and fungus in cabbage, cauliflower and other vegetables, knowledge from family elders; effective at low volumes and storable in a cool dry place for 5-6 months **[snippet]** — [National Innovation Foundation record, Kishan Singh](https://nif.org.in/upload/innovation/10th/157537-kishan-singh.pdf)
- Ash is used by tribal farmers to control pumpkin beetles and aphids **[snippet]** — [Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry 2020 SP9(2)](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020/vol9issue2S/PartC/S-9-2-44-884.pdf)
- Ash dusting is practised on zaid (summer) vegetables against the red beetle in most districts of Uttar Pradesh **[snippet]** — [Vikaspedia, Indigenous technologies practised by farmers](https://vikaspedia.in/agriculture/best-practices/sustainable-agriculture/crop-management/indigenous-technologies-practised-by-farmers)
- A study of traditional management of red pumpkin beetle exists for Cachar district, Assam (content not opened) — [Indian medicine archive record](https://indianmedicine.eldoc.ub.rug.nl/62828/)
- Wood-ash dusting on nursery seedlings acts as an insect repellent and supplies minerals **[snippet]** — [TNAU Agritech organic tomato nursery](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_tomato_nursery.html)
- An ITK of broadcasting 5-10 kg wood ash or rice husk per acre with 3-5 L kerosene in the morning is reported **[snippet; kerosene is not a natural input]** — [Bulletin of the National Research Centre, table of ITKs](https://bnrc.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42269-024-01226-x/tables/4)
- Nepal mid-hills (Parbat, temperate vegetable pocket; 18 farmers interviewed, July 2021): most farmers used chemical insecticides; a few used neem oil, washing with chilli powder and detergent, and "jholmol" (fermented liquid bio-pesticide/manure); pests recorded included *Leucinodes orbonalis* on brinjal, *Bactrocera cucurbitae* and red pumpkin beetle on cucurbits, and *Scirtothrips dorsalis* on capsicum; literature cited there notes wood ash among locally available materials — [Prithvi Journal of Research and Innovation](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/1036/1cfe68aeeb7171705d50d4696b1fb6b4788b.pdf)
- General ITK inventory: cow or buffalo urine sprayed against paddy pests; cow-dung ash broadcast with irrigation to protect potato from frost **[snippet]** — [Vikaspedia](https://vikaspedia.in/agriculture/best-practices/sustainable-agriculture/crop-management/indigenous-technologies-practised-by-farmers)
- Mechanical tradition endorsed by formal packages: hand-picking hadda beetle grubs, pupae and adults from skeletonised leaves — NIPHM-Brinjal; collecting and destroying red pumpkin beetle adults — [TNAU Agritech](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/cucurbitae/cucurbitae_2.html)

### Inferences
- Ash dusting of cucurbit seedlings against red pumpkin beetle can be shown as a traditional practice "documented in Indian ITK inventories (UP, tribal regions, Assam)", not as an HP-documented one; no dose or timing (e.g., on dew-wet leaves in early morning) appeared in an opened source.
- The Kullu record supports a generic "cow urine with local bitter plants" IKS card for vegetables, but the two plants and the dilution were not retrieved.

### Gaps
- No HP-specific ITK paper (e.g., Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge, Himachal Journal of Agricultural Research) on capsicum, brinjal or cucurbit plant protection was retrieved; the search batch for it was cut off by the tool limit.
- No quantities or timing for ash dusting; no efficacy data for any traditional practice listed.
- The identity of the "two local plants" in the Kullu practice and its dose are unknown.
