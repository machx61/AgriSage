## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter for HP onion, what do they look like, what weather favours them, and what is the HP incidence?

### Takeaway
For HP onion the best-evidenced leaf problems are: (1) downy mildew (HP thesis, loss 12-75% quoted; Solan severity 4.78-58.65%); (2) purple blotch (the Neri trial found no variety fully resistant); (3) Stemphylium leaf blight (Palampur survey: severity 35-45% across five districts **[snippet]**); (4) onion thrips (silvering; the main insect in a Palampur study **[snippet]**); plus (5) Colletotrichum "twister" and viral/vector problems at lower confidence. Basal rot, white rot and maggot are root/bulb problems seen on foliage only as yellowing and tip dieback; rust and Botrytis leaf blight have only thin or indirect support, and leaf miner appears in HP only as a species record.

### Cited Findings

**Context for HP**
- Onion in HP is grown in the subtropical and dry temperate zones; it is "not the commercial crop of Himachal Pradesh but has the potential"; about 2,530 ha, 47,960 MT, 18.96 t/ha (PDOGR 2016, as cited). The thesis lists downy mildew, purple blotch, Stemphylium blight and smudge as the most prominent and widely distributed diseases in the area — UHF-DM-Thesis
- The national package lists as nationally significant: thrips, onion maggot, bulb mite, eriophyid mite, red spider mite; damping off, purple blotch, Stemphylium leaf blight, Colletotrichum blight/anthracnose/"twister", onion yellow dwarf, iris yellow spot. Regionally significant: downy mildew (tagged "Himachal Pradesh, Delhi"), Fusarium basal rot, white rot, pink root, black/green mould, bacterial rots — NIPHM-Onion

**1. Downy mildew (Peronospora destructor)**
- Leaf symptoms (national package): leaves turn pale green; cottony white mycelial growth on leaves; leaves go pale yellow to dark brown and dry up. Warm, humid climate favours it; fungus survives in soil and debris — NIPHM-Onion
- Leaf symptoms (HP thesis): pale spots turning light brown or purplish; as spots enlarge, leaves are girdled and a greyish-violet furry mould forms on the spot; plants dwarfed, distorted, pale; bulbs often spongy and fail to mature — UHF-DM-Thesis
- TNAU: white downy growth on the lower leaf surface with chlorotic areas above; requires cool moist nights, moderately warm cloudy days; survives in infected bulbs and as oospores in soil — TNAU-Hort
- HP severity: Solan district survey (2017-18), severity 4.78-58.65%, highest at Pandah, lowest at Dharampur; the disease "occurs regularly in a moderate to severe form in HP" since the first outbreak report (Singh et al. 1987) and "causes 12-75% losses in bulb yield" (Sugha & Singh 1991, as cited in the thesis) — UHF-DM-Thesis
- HP weather: in the Solan field study on cv. Palam Lohit, severity appeared in the last week of February (3.55%) and peaked at 72.76% in the second week of April 2018; mean maximum 21.5-26.6 C, mean minimum 6.7-17.23 C, relative humidity 52-68% and rainfall 1-4.5 mm cumulative favoured spread. In the lab, sporangial germination peaked at 15 C (73.96%) and at 100% RH (71.4%); RH above 95% was suitable — UHF-DM-Thesis
- The thesis concludes it is "a serious disease in low lying areas of Solan district" — UHF-DM-Thesis
- Older HP survey, 1990-93, seven districts: highest incidence in Chamba, lowest in Sirmour; worse in damp conditions **[snippet]** — [Indian Phytopathology, "Incidence of onion downy mildew and its impact on yield"](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/19784); a news item (2004) also says it is serious in Kangra, Mandi and Sirmour with 10-100% losses **[snippet]** — [Tribune Agriculture](https://www.tribuneindia.com/2004/20040202/agro.htm)

**2. Purple blotch (Alternaria porri)**
- Symptoms: small, sunken, whitish flecks with purple centres on leaves and flower stalks; lesions may girdle leaves/stalks causing drooping; infected plants fail to develop bulbs — NIPHM-Onion
- TNAU wording: minute white dots with irregular chlorotic spots on leaf tips, turning circular to oblong with concentric black velvety zones, coalescing and drying from the tip down; also on outer bulb scales; warm humid weather, rain and dew favour it — TNAU-Hort
- Weather: hot and humid, 21-30 C with RH 80-90% — NIPHM-Onion
- HP variety screening at Neri (low hills): no variety fully resistant; 'Agrifound Dark Red' moderately resistant with 13.78% incidence — Neri-Var
- HP management thesis (sub-tropical zone, Palampur/YSP): optimum infection conditions 25 C, 100% RH and 24 h leaf wetness **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh, Management of purple blotch of onion in sub-tropical zone of HP](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810155575)
- National loss range for purple blotch cited by Indian authors: 30-100% under field conditions — PA-2024 (not HP)

**3. Stemphylium leaf blight**
- Symptoms: small yellowish to orange flecks or streaks in the middle of leaves, becoming elongated spindle-shaped spots with a pinkish margin; infection on transplanted seedlings at the 3-4 leaf stage in late March/early April; damages the inflorescence stalk in seed crops. Favoured by warm (18-25 C) humid weather and 16 h or more of leaf wetness — NIPHM-Onion
- HP survey (CSK HPKV Palampur, 2018-21): disease in every onion-growing district; severity Una 45.4%, Bilaspur 42.5%, Mandi 37.5%, Kangra 36.9%, Hamirpur 35.2%; pathogen *Stemphylium vesicarium*; mycelial growth best at 25 C, sporulation at 15-20 C **[snippet; severity not incidence]** — [KrishiKosh, Epidemiology and management of Stemphylium blight of onion](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/dff22832-59f0-413b-8c89-6544f7bc4cad)
- Garlic, not onion: Stemphylium of garlic is a UHF Nauni thesis topic (Bhardwaj 2018) and a mid-hill HP perpetuation study exists (title only) — [UHF thesis, Epidemiology and management of Stemphylium blight of garlic (downloaded, abstract not read)](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/cabd4930-4734-4868-9446-0facef4e0eb8/content); [IPPJ 72492](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/72492)

**4. Thrips (Thrips tabaci) and silvering**
- Damage: feeding removes chlorophyll so the area looks white to silvery, as patches and streaks; severe injury gives a silvery cast and leaves can wither; tiny black "tar" spots (excrement) on heavily fed leaves; larvae feed on new leaves in the centre of the neck; adults about 1.5 mm — NIPHM-Onion
- Thrips vector iris yellow spot virus persistently; disease severity correlates positively with thrips numbers; IYSV is not seed-transmitted; sources are volunteers, culls, infected transplants and alternate hosts — NIPHM-Onion
- IYSV symptom: cream, elliptical (diamond-shaped) spots on leaves and scapes that can collapse at the spot — NIPHM-Onion
- Palampur, HP: a study of the onion insect complex found *Thrips tabaci* "eudominant" in Zone I (other species sub-recedent); rabi onion 2017 and 2018: thrips began mid-February, peaked at 4.93 and 4.65 thrips/plant in the second fortnight of April with 69.7% and 69.3% plant infestation; late-kharif onion: activity from the last week of October, peak 4.84/plant in mid-December and 86% plant infestation in the second fortnight of December; population positively and significantly correlated with maximum and minimum temperature **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh, Insect pest complex of onion in HP](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/88cf9e78-09c2-4381-bd5a-a8d2c1402e88/full)
- Other insects recorded on HP onion at Palampur in that study: *Altica* sp., *Chromatomyia horticola* (leaf miner), *Helicoverpa armigera*, *Spodoptera litura*, *Nezara viridula*, cricket and cockchafer species **[snippet]** — same KrishiKosh record
- High nitrogen (urea) increases thrips; thrips lacerate leaves, and "through lacerated wounds by thrips, there is a severe incidence of blast disease, caused by *Botrytis allii*, when crop is 70-90 days old" (text sits under hill-grown onion/garlic disorders; the source link in that paragraph is a NHRDF garlic page, so it may be garlic-derived) — NIPHM-Onion

**5. Colletotrichum blight / "twister"**
- Water-soaked pale yellow spots on leaves that spread lengthwise over the blade; leaves shrivel and droop; curling and abnormal elongation of leaves ("twister"); worst in warm (25-30 C), moist, high-organic-matter soils; little disease below 15 C — NIPHM-Onion

**Whole-plant / below-ground problems that show on foliage**
- Fusarium basal rot: yellowing, stunting, leaves dry from tip downward; roots pink then rot; bulb decays from the base; moist soil and 27 C favour it — NIPHM-Onion. TNAU adds 28-32 C and water stagnation — TNAU-Hort
- White rot: yellowing and dieback of leaf tips, then soft water-soaked bulbs with white fluffy mycelium and mustard-seed-like black sclerotia; most severe in cool, dry soils — NIPHM-Onion
- Onion maggot: only the larva damages; seedlings first wilt, then flaccid and die; later generations cause distorted growth and rot in bulbs — NIPHM-Onion (no HP incidence data found)
- Onion yellow dwarf: yellow streaks at the base of the first true leaves, then complete yellowing, crinkled flattened leaves that fall over; aphid-transmitted and carried by infected seed bulbs and volunteers — NIPHM-Onion
- Red spider mite: stippling on the upper leaf surface, silk webbing, bleaching; hot dry weather favours it — NIPHM-Onion. Eriophyid mite: stunting, twisting, curling and discoloration of foliage — NIPHM-Onion
- Damping off: pre- and post-emergence; collar rots and seedlings collapse; more prevalent in the rainy season with 60-75% damage; high soil moisture and moderate temperature — NIPHM-Onion

**Rust and Botrytis leaf blight**
- Rust is not listed among the national package's onion diseases; no HP onion rust source was retrieved. A TNAU list names "Onion leaf blight and purple blotch" as caused by *Botrytis* sp. and *Alternaria porri* (listed together) — TNAU-Hort

### Inferences
- Look-alike pairs for the vision model (symptom wording from the sources above; the pairing is my inference):
  1. Purple blotch (whitish sunken fleck with purple centre, later concentric black/velvety, tip dieback) vs. Stemphylium (yellow-orange flecks/streaks mid-leaf becoming spindle spots with pink margin; early-season on seedlings 3-4 leaf) vs. thrips silvering (white-silver streaks with black tar dots, insects present in the neck). In HP both blights can co-occur (both are listed as prominent); a leaf photo alone is unreliable and a sibling "check neck for thrips" prompt is useful.
  2. Downy mildew (pale patches with grey-violet fuzz, best seen at dawn/dew; whole leaf pale then collapses) vs. purple blotch (discrete lesions) vs. thrips (silver, no fuzz).
  3. IYSV (cream-yellow diamond spots on leaf/scape) vs. thrips silvering vs. Stemphylium flecks; IYSV needs thrips present.
  4. Colletotrichum "twister" (elongated, curling, water-soaked leaves) vs. eriophyid mite curling vs. onion yellow dwarf crinkling.
- HP seasonality: downy mildew in the Solan study built up Feb-April in cool-warm, low-rain weather; thrips peak April (rabi) or December (late kharif) at Palampur; purple blotch and Stemphylium are warm-humid (monsoon / March-April on seedlings) problems. Monsoon-season nursery raise brings damping off.
- Botrytis leaf blight is probably a minor entry for HP and should not be a first-tier vision class on this evidence; rust likewise.

### Gaps
- No HP altitude-wise incidence data (low, mid, high hills, dry temperate Lahaul/Kinnaur) for any onion disease.
- HP purple-blotch incidence numbers, onion rust, Botrytis leaf blight, maggot, leaf-miner and basal/white rot incidence in HP were not retrieved.
- The Palampur thrips and Stemphylium figures are from search summaries only (KrishiKosh pages did not open as text).

## KQ2. Cultural practices (beds, drainage, rotation, spacing, seed/bulb selection, nursery hygiene, varieties)

### Takeaway
The sourced cultural package is: raised nursery beds above 15 cm, solarised and fresh nursery sites, hot-water seed treatment (50 C for 20 min), 2-3 year rotation with non-host crops, sprinkler over drip/surface irrigation and reflective mulch for thrips, border/barrier crops, and sanitation. Variety choices in HP are limited: Agrifound Dark Red is only "moderately resistant" to purple blotch and no variety is fully resistant.

### Cited Findings
**Nursery and seed**
- Damping off: avoid excessive watering; raised beds more than 15 cm high for drainage; solarise nursery beds with transparent polythene for 30 days before sowing; avoid raising nursery repeatedly in the same plot; avoid excess soil moisture; level and drain land; sow clean, healthy seed on raised beds; avoid dense sowing; use well-decomposed FYM in the nursery — NIPHM-Onion
- Alternative solarisation spec printed in the same package: polythene "45 gauge (0.45 mm)" for three weeks before sowing (gauge and mm are inconsistent in the source; quote as printed); another entry says 25 gauge for 30 days in summer — NIPHM-Onion
- Purple blotch: use healthy seed; 2-3 year rotation with non-host crops; proper drainage; recommended N and P doses (no excess); hot-water soaking of seed at 50 C for 20 minutes; resistant varieties — NIPHM-Onion
- Stemphylium: collect and burn crop residues; long rotation with non-host crops; field drainage and lower plant density; hot-water seed soak 50 C for 20 min; Welsh onion (*Allium fistulosum*) lines are moderately resistant — NIPHM-Onion
- Seed-crop bulbs: select healthy bulbs for seed production; 3-4 year rotation; dispose of culls and refuse heaps from storage; avoid late planting, poor drainage, high fertiliser doses and frequent irrigation; expose seed-crop bulbs to sun for 12 days to destroy the fungus (the row sits beside the Stemphylium/Colletotrichum/downy mildew block, so the exact target is ambiguous) — NIPHM-Onion

**Field**
- Add well-rotten FYM 10 t/acre or vermicompost 4 t/acre; deep summer ploughing; soil test-based nutrients — NIPHM-Onion
- Excess nitrogen makes crops succulent and susceptible; avoid too-high N — NIPHM-Onion
- Thrips: avoid successive onion/garlic and preferred hosts (cabbage, cotton, tomato, cucumber, melons, pumpkins, strawberries); plant the new crop upwind of old plantings; adjust transplanting dates; reflective silver and aluminium-painted black mulches repel thrips (seed crop); sprinkler irrigation reduces thrips considerably compared with drip and surface irrigation; two rows of maize, or inner wheat plus outer maize row, around onion plots as barrier — NIPHM-Onion
- Mites/bulb mite: avoid close spacing; rotate; sanitation; do not plant onion after cole crops (decaying cauliflower can harbour very high bulb-mite populations); flood irrigation reduces mite levels in soil — NIPHM-Onion
- Ecological engineering: intercrops such as cowpea, maize, coriander, urdbean and 4 rows of sorghum or maize as guard crop; rotate with non-host cereal, cucurbit or crucifer; basil as repellent and marigold as ovipositional trap crop; bird perches at 20/acre; flowering insectary plants (buckwheat, sunflower, coriander, marigold, mustard, cowpea etc.); do not uproot naturally growing nectar weeds such as *Tridax*, *Ageratum* — NIPHM-Onion
- IYSV/thrips: plant healthy seedlings free of thrips and IYSV; 3-year or longer rotation; eliminate alternate hosts, culls and weeds in and around the field; sprinkler irrigation; avoid thin patchy stands and crop stress; use blue sticky traps — NIPHM-Onion
- Onion yellow dwarf: virus-free transplants, 3-year rotation, rogue diseased plants, isolate from volunteers, manage aphid vectors — NIPHM-Onion
- Fusarium basal rot: 4-5 year rotation; mixed cropping with tobacco and sorghum "is effective in reducing pathogen inoculum" (as printed; a tobacco-intercrop claim from the national package, not tested here); polythene solarisation for 30 days in summer; avoid injury; flood soil in the non-growing season; resistant varieties — NIPHM-Onion
- White rot: use disease-free areas; rotation with cereals; hot-water bulb treatment at 49 C; solarisation at 35 C for 18 h or 45 C for 6 h reduces disease by 50-70% (as printed) — NIPHM-Onion
- Pink root: rotation with cereals/green manure; solarisation; Welsh onion lines resistant; late planting reduces disease — NIPHM-Onion
- Storage rots: cure onions in the field two days then shade-dry 10-15 days; store with aeration; avoid bruising; artificial curing in the rainy season — NIPHM-Onion

**Varieties (HP)**
- Neri (Hamirpur, 620 m, 2010-12): bulb yield Palam Lohit 275.00 q/ha, Nasik Red 240.67, N-53 239.25, Agrifound Dark Red 232.37; none fully purple-blotch resistant; 'Agrifound Dark Red' moderately resistant (13.78% incidence); recommended for low hills: Palam Lohit, Nasik Red, Agrifound Dark Red — Neri-Var
- Kharif onion at Neri (2011-12): Agrifound Dark Red 229.03 q/ha; best transplanting date 25 July (197.54 q/ha) from six dates 5 July to 25 August; N-53, Nasik Red, Agrifound Light Red also tested; the page reports nothing on disease — [Agriculture Journal, Varieties and planting time on kharif onion, lower Shivalik hills of HP](https://www.agriculturejournal.org/volume5number1/effect-of-different-varieties-and-planting-time-on-kharif-onion-production-in-lower-shivalik-hills-of-himachal-pradesh/)
- National table of tolerant types: purple blotch — TNAU hybrid CO 2; thrips — TNAU hybrids CO 2, CO 3, CO 4 — NIPHM-Onion (not HP-adapted)

### Inferences
- Transplanting date, spacing and a fixed upwind order are only weakly supported for HP; the one HP-specific agronomic rule is the Neri kharif result (25 July) and it was yield-based, not disease-based.
- A card can safely say "raised beds 15 cm or more, no repeated nursery on the same plot, 2-3 year rotation with non-host crops, hot-water soak of seed at 50 C for 20 min" (all from one national package) and should present the varieties as "tested at Neri, none immune".

### Gaps
- No HP data on mulch (pine needle, black plastic) in onion; no HP spacing-disease study.
- UHF Nauni / CSK HPKV package-of-practices onion pages could not be opened; their variety and disease advice is unverified.
- Hot-water seed treatment: no check whether the 50 C / 20 min value was validated for HP seed lots (it is a national package figure).

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
The verified doses are modest: Trichoderma harzianum seed treatment 8 g/kg (plus 100 kg/ha enriched soil application) and 10 g/kg for combined T. harzianum + P. fluorescens seed treatment; T. viride 4 g/kg bulbs and 2.5 kg/ha basal for basal rot; Azotobacter/PSB 240 g each per acre; Trichogramma pretiosum 40,000/acre; nematodes 20-120 crore IJ/acre; blue pan/sticky traps 4-5/acre. No source I could open gives a dose for Pseudomonas foliar sprays, Bacillus, Beauveria, Metarhizium or predator releases on onion, and none of the dosed biocontrol results is from HP.

### Cited Findings
**Seed, bulb and soil biocontrol**
- National package: apply Trichoderma spp. and *Pseudomonas fluorescens* as seed/seedling/planting material, nursery treatment and soil application (no dose printed); farmer-prepared biopesticides for own use do not need registration — NIPHM-Onion
- Biofertiliser seed/seedling treatment: Azotobacter and PSB cultures at 240 g each per acre — NIPHM-Onion
- Basal rot (*Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cepae*): treat bulbs with *Trichoderma viride* at 4 g/kg; apply *T. viride* 2.5 kg/ha basally with VAM 12.5 kg/ha — TNAU-Hort
- Purple blotch (Andhra Pradesh field trial, var. Bellari Red, not HP): in all treatments seed treated with *T. harzianum* at 8 g/kg seed plus soil application of enriched antagonist at 100 kg/ha; sprays started immediately after first symptoms and repeated at 15-day intervals (four sprays). Percent disease index: Panchagavya 10% spray 19.54; neem leaf extract 10% spray 20.67 (text swaps this with 20.96 in places); Panchagavya then neem leaf extract 20.96; mancozeb 75% WP 2.5 g/L 23.26; seed treatment with T. harzianum alone (the "control") 40.25. Bulb yield 6,680, 5,580, 5,180, 3,740 and 2,040 kg/ha; benefit:cost 1:3.62, 1:3.16, 1:2.88, 1:2.06, 1:1.48. The "control" was not untreated (it received Trichoderma seed treatment) and the paper contains internal text/table inconsistencies — PA-2024
- Purple blotch/thrips, combined seed treatment: *T. harzianum* + *P. fluorescens* at 10 g/kg of seed gave the best germination and bulb yield; a prophylactic spray of Neembicidine (a neem product) at 0.3% and *T. harzianum* at 1% was reported as most effective against disease or thrips; the abstract page does not name the state or region — [Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry 9(3), Purple blotch disease in onion: management through biological agents](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020.v9.i3.11541/purple-blotch-disease-in-onion-management-through-biological-agents)
- Dharwad thesis (not HP): *T. harzianum* seed treatment 10 g/kg plus sprays of Panchagavya 5% and NSKE 5% gave the lowest disease index and best yield in that trial **[snippet]** — summarised in a search result for [KrishiKosh Dharwad purple blotch thesis (item 5a4b0242)](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/5a4b0242-1022-49c7-bdb4-d91e081b278e)
- Thrips IPM module in Tamil Nadu: soil neem cake at 250 kg/ha at transplanting, *P. fluorescens* foliar sprays, plus *Lecanicillium lecanii* (2 x 10^8 CFU/g) at 2.0 g/L and azadirachtin 1500 ppm at 3.0 ml/L; *P. fluorescens* foliar at 5.0 g/L raised phenols/tannins **[snippet; the module also contained synthetic insecticides]** — [Madras Agricultural Journal / Krishikosh thrips thesis results](https://www.masujournal.org/view_journal.php?id=433)

**Insects**
- *Trichogramma pretiosum* 40,000/acre, 4-5 releases at weekly intervals from flower initiation (listed against gram pod borer/tobacco caterpillar in onion); entomopathogenic nematodes *Steinernema feltiae* 20-120 crore infective juveniles/acre; pheromone traps 4-5/acre (lures replaced every 2-3 weeks); light trap 1/acre; bird perches 20/acre — NIPHM-Onion
- Blue pan water/sticky traps 15 cm above canopy for thrips, 4-5 per acre; home-made blue-painted tins coated with grease, Vaseline or castor oil — NIPHM-Onion
- Natural enemies to conserve: thrips parasitoid *Ceranisus menes*; predators syrphid flies, minute pirate bug (*Orius tantilus* etc.), predatory thrips *Aeolothrips fasciatum*, damsel bug, lacewings, coccinellids (*Cheilomenes sexmaculata*), spiders; for maggot the braconid *Aphaereta pallipes*, rove and ground beetles; for mites *Amblyseius* spp., *Phytoseiulus persimilis*, *Stethorus*, *Oligota*, and *Beauveria bassiana* (listed as an entomopathogen, no dose) — NIPHM-Onion
- Habitat plants for natural enemies (carrot family, sunflower family, buckwheat, alfalfa, marigold, spearmint; cosmos; French bean for predatory thrips; sunflower, buckwheat, cowpea for braconids; basil as repellent) — NIPHM-Onion

### Inferences
- The HP-relevant bioagent evidence for onion is slim: the best-dosed purple-blotch study is from Andhra Pradesh and the thrips module study from Tamil Nadu. A card can carry "Trichoderma seed treatment 8-10 g/kg" with the label "dose from South-Indian field trials, not HP-tested".
- Where reference to purple blotch efficacy is made, the Andhra trial compared sprays against a Trichoderma-seed-treated control, so the numbers show spray benefit on top of seed treatment, and the PDI ordering between Panchagavya and neem leaf extract is not well separated statistically (text/table conflict).
- Because thrips both damage leaves and vector IYSV and open wounds for blights, the blue trap + barrier crop + sprinkler + conserve predators route is the only NIPHM non-chemical thrips package; no biological spray with a verified dose is available for thrips.

### Gaps
- No verified dose for *P. fluorescens* spray or soil, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium*, *Lecanicillium* (except the snippet), or predator releases on onion; no HP field trial of any biocontrol agent on onion leaf diseases opened.
- Onion fly/maggot biocontrol: only natural-enemy names, no dose.
- Efficacy of Trichoderma against downy mildew was not retrieved (mildew is an oomycete; no source tested it).

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
The NIPHM onion package gives no botanical dose at all. The sprayable natural concentrations I could verify for onion are panchagavya 10% and neem leaf extract 10% (Andhra, purple blotch), Neembicidine 0.3% and T. harzianum 1% (Phytojournal), and chitosan as an HP-tested plant-defence elicitor against downy mildew (0.05% in the table). HP-specific botanical results (cow urine, Datura, garlic, NSKE) exist only as snippet-level leads without concentrations.

### Cited Findings
- Panchagavya 10% spray and neem leaf extract 10% spray (four sprays, 15-day interval, from first symptoms) cut purple blotch PDI to 19.54 and 20.67 versus 40.25 in the seed-treatment-only plots (Andhra Pradesh, not HP); mancozeb 2.5 g/L gave 23.26 — PA-2024
- Neembicidine 0.3% prophylactic spray and *T. harzianum* 1% spray (state not stated) — [Phytojournal 9(3)](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020.v9.i3.11541/purple-blotch-disease-in-onion-management-through-biological-agents)
- HP, downy mildew (Nauni, Solan, cv. Palam Lohit; spray applied once at the 3-4 leaf stage before disease, then twice at 15-day intervals after disease appearance): chitosan gave 81.9% disease control (severity 12.5% vs. 69.5% control), plant height 43.9 cm and bulb yield 26.7 t/ha; potassium orthophosphate 74.2% control; BABA 67.7% control; systemic/combination fungicides gave 71.8-79.6% control. The methods text lists concentrations as chitosan 5 ppm, potassium orthophosphate 20 ppm and BABA 10 ppm, whereas the extracted results table shows chitosan 0.05%, potassium orthophosphate 0.10% and BABA 0.3% (inconsistent; verify before quoting). The thesis recommends "three protective sprays of chitosan ... at 15 days interval" for farmer fields — UHF-DM-Thesis. (Chitosan and potassium phosphonate-type salts are plant-defence products; whether the app counts them as "natural" is a product decision.)
- HP sub-tropical zone purple blotch thesis: foliar cow urine alone reduced incidence by up to 91.33% and disease index by up to 93.57% in field trials; among extracts, *Datura stramonium* aqueous extract was best in pot trials; garlic and NSKE gave high in-vitro inhibition; salicylic acid at 20 mM and 10 mM best among eight resistance inducers; synthetic standards (trifloxystrobin + tebuconazole, azoxystrobin + difenoconazole) cut incidence by up to 85.95% and 82.61% **[snippet; no concentrations retrieved]** — [KrishiKosh, Management of purple blotch of onion in sub-tropical zone of HP](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810155575)
- Latur (Maharashtra) in-vitro ranking of plant extracts against *A. porri*: NSKE 5% best at 44.24% inhibition over control, ahead of *Mentha arvensis*, garlic, ginger and *Vitex negundo*; mancozeb at par with NSKE and mentha **[snippet; laboratory only]** — [Plant Archives / Phytojournal search result](https://www.phytojournal.com/archives/2020/vol9issue3/PartAA/9-3-222-259.pdf)
- TNAU forecasts give neem oil 3% (30 ml/L) with Teepol 1 ml/L and NSKE 5% for leafhoppers/thrips and neem oil 3% for whitefly vectors in onion-related advisories; these are generic TNAU pest forecasts, not onion-HP, and I could not open the onion-specific text **[snippet]** — [TNAU Centre for Plant Protection Studies forecast, April 2023](http://www.agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/pdf/2023/CPPS-PSR%20Report-April%202023%20(1).pdf)
- TNAU conventional recommendations for orientation: purple blotch — tebuconazole 1.5 ml/L, mancozeb 2 g/L or zineb 2 g/L; downy mildew — mancozeb 2 g/L, three sprays starting 20 days after transplanting at 10-12 day intervals (not organic options) — TNAU-Hort
- Onion foliar nutrition from the national package for deficiency look-alikes: urea 1% or DAP 2% (N), K2SO4 1% (K), MnSO4 0.3%, ZnSO4 0.5%, FeSO4 0.5% foliar sprays; useful because nutrient tip-burn can mimic fungal tip dieback — NIPHM-Onion
- Sulphur and copper: TNAU lists copper oxychloride 2.5 g/L and soil drench 2.5 g/L for basal rot in the same chapter, and the May 2023 TNAU forecast copper oxychloride 2.5 g/L for purple blotch **[snippet]**; NIPHM-Onion's chemical options for purple blotch/Stemphylium are difenoconazole, kitazin, mancozeb and zineb, so no organic-eligible mineral spray is endorsed in the package — TNAU-Hort; NIPHM-Onion
- Whey / milk: not found for onion in any source (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The HP-tested natural-leaning option with the strongest data for a leaf problem is chitosan vs. downy mildew, but it is a purchased input; the home-made options (cow urine, panchagavya, neem leaf extract) have only out-of-state trials (panchagavya, neem leaf) or concentration-free snippets (cow urine in HP).
- Natural sprays should be framed as preventive/early-stage and used with sanitation and drainage; the Andhra panchagavya/neem results compare against a seed-treated control rather than a no-treatment control.

### Gaps
- No verified NSKE %, neem oil % or garlic extract % for onion from a source I opened with an HP link; no verified cow urine concentration for the 91.33% HP result.
- No source for whey/sour milk/buttermilk on onion, no sulphur dose for onion, no copper-based organic recommendation for HP onion.
- No natural-product efficacy data for onion thrips from HP.

## KQ5. Natural-farming (PK3Y/SPNF) formulations with doses for onion and alliums; trial results

### Takeaway
Recipes for beejamrit, jeevamrit, neemastra, agniastra and brahmastra are documented by UHF Nauni authors (Indian Farming 2023) but carry no onion-specific dose or efficacy. The only onion-specific HP natural-farming trials found are two UHF Nauni studies known only from abstracts or titles (nursery seed treatments with Drek + cow urine 20%; a 2022-24 onion seed-crop trial on diseases). Official PK3Y/SPNF doses for khatti lassi and dashparni for onion could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
**Recipes (UHF Nauni, Indian Farming 2023)**
- Beejamrit: 5 kg cow dung tied in cloth and hung in 20 L water for up to 12 h; 50 g lime in 1 L water left overnight; squeeze the bundle into the water three times; add a handful of soil, 5 L desi cow urine and the lime water; coat seeds by hand, dry well, sow — UHF-NF
- Jeevamrit: 10 kg cow dung + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg pulse flour + a handful of bund soil; make up to 200 L in a barrel; ferment in shade 48 h with stirring; described as preventing fungal and bacterial plant diseases (no data) — UHF-NF
- Neemastra: 100 L water + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg cow dung + 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves; cover with gunny bag in shade 48 h; stir clockwise 1 min morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sap-sucking insects and small caterpillars (no spray dilution printed) — UHF-NF
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves + 500 g tobacco powder + 500 g green chilli chutney + 250 g garlic chutney in 20 L cow urine, boiled on low flame under a lid, kept 48 h, stirred morning and evening; for sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (no dilution printed) — UHF-NF
- Brahmastra (1 acre): leaf paste of any 5 plants (Darek, Sita Phal, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta) at 200 g per plant in 4 L cow urine, heated to boil, cooled 48 h; mix 2.5-3 L of the solution in 200 L water and spray; for sucking insects and caterpillars (the dilution line is printed at the bottom of the Brahmastra column in the layout) — UHF-NF
- Mulching (acchadana) and whapasa described as pillars; soil target 25-32 C and 65-72% moisture — UHF-NF
- Kinnaur farmer testimony: "Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases" on a farm growing apple, peas, carrot, cucumber, beans, coriander, radish; Dashparni ark controlled woolly apple aphid on another farm; garlic is listed as a crop on a third farm; anecdotal, no measurements, no onion — UHF-NF
- Khatti lassi dilution (HP Agriculture Department advisory, Hamirpur, for wheat yellow rust): "Farmers following organic farming should spray a solution of one litre lassi with 20 litre water in intervals of ten days" — [Tribune, Hamirpur farmers report yellow rust in wheat crop](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/hamirpur-farmers-report-yellow-rust-in-wheat-crop-482937/amp). (Opened; it is for wheat and does not state lassi age; not an onion recommendation.)
- Other recipe variants exist (TNAU neemastra and brahmastra, Dashparni ark 200-500 ml per 10 L water from a commercial guide) **[snippet; not opened or not HP]** — [Organic Mandya, Dashparni ark](https://www.organicmandya.com/farming/methods/dashparni-ark/)

**Onion-specific natural-farming work at UHF Nauni (abstract-level)**
- "Standardization of nursery production technology in onion under natural farming system" (Bhardwaj, Seed Science & Technology, Nauni, 2022-23): experiment 1 tested 21 seed treatments including beejamrit, jeevamrit, plant extracts and cow urine; Drek + cow urine at 20% for 30 min gave the best germination (86.74%), seedling length 16.10 cm, maximum emergence (82.70%) and the lowest pre-emergence (17.30%) and post-emergence (0.66%) damping off; experiment 2 used soil-applied jeevamrit at 10% every 15 days with foliar saunthastra and buttermilk at 3% **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810203993)
- "Effect of natural farming components on growth, diseases, seed quality and economics in onion" (UHF Nauni and partners, two-year trial 2022-24, onion seed crop; Sciforum preprint): only the title and opening of the abstract were visible; disease results not retrieved **[snippet; page returned 403]** — [Sciforum paper 24943](https://sciforum.net/paper/24943)
- UHF Nauni agromet bulletins advise natural-farming growers to spray Agniastra, Brahmastra and Neemastra for insect pests **[snippet]** — [UHF Gramin Krishi Mausam Sewa bulletin](https://uhf.ac.in/storage/uploads/docs/1785233552.pdf)
- Spiti (HP): henbane used as a herbal input where neem is not locally available **[snippet, one search summary of naturalfarming.niti.gov.in; page refused connection]** — [NITI natural farming, Himachal Pradesh](https://naturalfarming.niti.gov.in/himachal-pradesh/)

**Where natural methods do not control the problem**
- IYSV and onion yellow dwarf: only healthy transplants, rotation, rogueing, vector management, blue traps are offered — NIPHM-Onion
- Fusarium basal rot, white rot, pink root: preventive (rotation, solarisation, drainage, clean planting material, Trichoderma on bulbs/soil); no curative natural spray — NIPHM-Onion; TNAU-Hort
- Downy mildew: in the HP thesis the field-effective sprays were chitosan and fungicides; the thesis does not test jeevamrit, lassi or botanicals — UHF-DM-Thesis
- Bacterial rots and storage rots: curing, drying and bruise avoidance only — NIPHM-Onion
- Tobacco caution: agniastra contains 500 g tobacco powder; no onion or virus caution was found in any opened source; the agniastra spray dilution is not printed in UHF-NF — UHF-NF

### Inferences
- On the evidence, the natural-farming approach for HP onion is mainly nursery hygiene (cow-urine/Drek seed soak, jeevamrit drench) plus preventive sprays; for blights and downy mildew there is no HP-quantified natural-farming spray recommendation I could cite.
- A conservative card dose set that is supported by an opened HP source: Beejamrit and Jeevamrit and Neemastra/Agniastra/Brahmastra recipes (UHF-NF), Brahmastra 2.5-3 L in 200 L water (UHF-NF), lassi 1 L in 20 L water every ten days (HP department advisory, wheat). Neemastra and Agniastra spray dilutions are NOT given in UHF-NF and must not be inferred.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed HP trial quantifying jeevamrit, khatti lassi, neemastra, agniastra, brahmastra or dashparni against onion purple blotch, Stemphylium, downy mildew or thrips was found.
- Official PK3Y/SPNF (spnfhp.nic.in, naturalfarming.niti.gov.in) doses for khatti lassi, dashparni, sonthastra and neemastra spray dilution were not retrieved.
- Disease and yield numbers of the 2022-24 Nauni onion natural-farming trial were not retrieved.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional / indigenous practices for onion pests and diseases

### Takeaway
I found no HP-specific documentation of traditional onion pest or disease practices. The nearest items are an India-wide ITK validation of onion/garlic extracts, an HP Kinnaur/Lahaul-Spiti ITK survey that did not list onion practices in its abstract, and generic regional ITKs (ash, cow urine) not tied to onion.

### Cited Findings
- National study of indigenous technical knowledge on alliums in plant protection: onion and garlic extracts were effective in indigenous pest management; 36 formulations scored by 30 experts (30.56% scored above 120 and 61.11% between 100 and 120 out of 150); the authors say in-vitro standardisation of formulations is necessary; the abstract lists no individual recipe or dose — [Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge](https://or.niscpr.res.in/index.php/IJTK/article/view/5725)
- A 2021 Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge study documented tribal farming knowledge in Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti (120 farmers, six villages; categories included soil and water management, storage, horticulture); the abstract lists no onion or garlic pest practice **[snippet]** — search result for the IJTK Kinnaur-Lahaul survey (no direct URL opened)
- HPKV Palampur note on an indigenous practice of knotting garlic tops before harvest to improve storage life (garlic, post-harvest, not pest control) **[snippet]** — [Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/2821/921/5780)
- Kullu farmer using two local plants with cow urine against insects and fungus in cabbage, cauliflower and other vegetables, storable 5-6 months **[snippet; from the sibling crop note, plants and dose unknown]** — [National Innovation Foundation record, Kishan Singh](https://nif.org.in/upload/innovation/10th/157537-kishan-singh.pdf)
- Cow-urine, ash and garlic-tobacco mixtures as ITKs in other states (Haryana, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh): ranks/effectiveness only, nothing for onion in HP **[snippet]** — e.g. [Phytojournal 2020 ITK table](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020/vol9issue2S/PartC/S-9-2-44-884.pdf)
- Mechanical tradition endorsed by the formal package: field sanitation, burning residues, removal of culls and volunteers, hand removal of diseased plants (rogueing), thorough water spray to wash mites — NIPHM-Onion

### Inferences
- The HP-documented IKS card for onion would have to be built from: Drek (*Melia azedarach*) plus cow urine seed soak (the UHF nursery thesis snippet; "Drek" also appears as a Brahmastra leaf ingredient in UHF-NF, species identity not given in the sources), jeevamrit drench, and the general recipes in UHF-NF; there is no cited traditional onion practice to present as "HP elders' knowledge".

### Gaps
- No HP ITK paper specific to onion pests or diseases; no ash dose or timing for onion; no efficacy data for any traditional practice.
- The identity of the two plants in the Kullu cow-urine practice and their dilution are unknown.
