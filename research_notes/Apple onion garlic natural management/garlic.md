## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter for HP garlic, what do they look like, what weather favours them, and what is the HP incidence?

### Takeaway
For HP garlic (Sirmaur, Solan, Kullu, Mandi, Kangra), the best-documented leaf problem is Stemphylium blight (survey values of 24-48% in Sirmaur and Solan localities; the disease appears every year), followed by purple blotch (Alternaria porri; present in HP, but no HP incidence number found), rust (11-32% incidence across 35 genotypes at Nauni; "yellow rust" outbreak reports from Rajgarh, Sirmaur), thrips, and soil-borne basal/white rot that shows up on leaves as tip yellowing then whole-plant collapse (2-30% incidence in the 2019-21 survey). Downy mildew, eriophyid mite and leaf miner are real on Allium but I found no HP garlic data. Recommended vision classes: Stemphylium/purple blotch (a combined "leaf blight complex" with look-alike warning), rust, thrips silvering, soil-borne rot yellowing/wilt (whole-plant photo), plus "other".

### Cited Findings

**HP garlic geography**
- In HP, Sirmour, Solan, Kullu, Mandi and Kangra are the main garlic-producing districts and supply garlic seed for the whole state, but most varieties are low-yielding — IJAgS-LongDay
- Garlic is planted early October and harvested in the first week of May at Nauni (planting 5 Oct 2022 and 7 Oct 2023; harvest first week of May 2023 and 2024), so the leaf-disease window runs roughly from February to harvest — IJAgS-LongDay

**Stemphylium blight (Stemphylium vesicarium)**
- HP incidence: appears every year in moderate to severe form in garlic areas of Solan and Sirmaur; survey values were Karganu 48%, Nauradhar 42.6%, Mariyog 40% (Sirmaur, "severe") and Lavighat 30.6%, Sihardi Brahmna 29.3%, Sandrol 24% (Solan, "moderate"); the thesis calls it the major disease in Sirmaur (the percentage metric, incidence versus severity, is not stated in the summary text) — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- Leaf symptoms in garlic: tip necrosis with numerous small white flecks that enlarge into sunken lesions with a bright yellow margin; later small, light, irregular to oval, yellow to brown, non-delineated lesions; centres turn light brown to tan, then dark olive-brown to black when the fungus sporulates; lesions lengthen along the blade, coalesce and blight the whole leaf — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- Weather in the lab: optimum 25 °C, 72 h leaf wetness, RH above 90%; in the field, mean air temperature 21.7 °C, mean RH 58.4% and cumulative rainfall of 31.1 mm coincided with maximum severity (19.4%); temperature, rainfall and humidity together explained 75% of disease progress; abstract says dry and moist weather with intermittent rain favoured it — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- Persistence: pathogen found invariably soil-borne and seed-borne to some extent in mid-hill HP; sexual fruiting bodies (pseudothecia of *Pleospora allii*) found on garlic debris buried at different depths **[snippet]** — [Katoch & Kumar 2017, Indian Phytopathology 70(3):294-296](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/72492)
- Onion data: infection of transplanted seedlings at 3-4 leaf stage in late March-early April; small yellowish-to-orange flecks or streaks in the middle of leaves become elongated spindle-shaped spots with a pink margin; warm (18-25 °C) humid weather and 16 hours or more leaf wetness favour it; survives in debris and soil **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion

**Purple blotch (Alternaria porri)**
- HP: documented as a garlic disease in an old HP fungicide trial (1989-1992; iprodione and metalaxyl + mancozeb best), but I found no HP incidence survey **[snippet]** — [IJAgS 18867 abstract](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/18867)
- Symptoms: small, sunken, whitish flecks with purple centres on leaves and flower stalks; lesions can girdle the leaf/stalk and cause drooping **[onion data]**; soil- and debris-borne; favoured by hot humid weather of 21-30 °C and RH 80-90% **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion
- Purple blotch and Stemphylium look alike, and are "only distinguishable by diagnostic services" while management is the same; Stemphylium tends to arise later in the season as a secondary pathogen on dead or dying tissue **[snippet; Maine extension, not HP]** — [UMaine garlic IPM newsletter](https://extension.umaine.edu/highmoor/?p=12406)
- Bangladesh field data: variety BARI Rashun-3 showed incidence 40.00% and severity 92.00% at 90 days after sowing (shows the disease can be severe on susceptible varieties) — Dhaka-PB

**Rust (Puccinia allii)**
- At Nauni, rust incidence across 35 genotypes ranged 11.00-32.00%, lowest in genotype LGC-3 (11.00%) — IJAgS-LongDay
- Garlic growers in Rajgarh, Sirmaur reported leaves turning yellowish with "yellow rust" and "a heavy toll" on production (May 2023); the article gives no pathogen name, loss figure or advice — [FreshPlaza, 11 May 2023](https://www.freshplaza.com/europe/article/9528125/garlic-growers-upset-due-to-yellow-rust-disease/)
- Symptoms: small flecks expanding to oval to diamond-shaped, reddish to dull-orange pustules on leaf blades; severely affected leaves yellow and dry; active in cool weather of about 14-24 °C; weedy Alliums can carry inoculum **[snippet; University of California IPM, not HP]** — [UC IPM Onion and Garlic: Rust](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/onion-and-garlic/rust/)
- Pakistan field trial: 10 cm plant spacing had more rust incidence/severity (79 and 55.66%) than wider spacing; authors suggest 20 cm row spacing **[snippet; not HP]** — [Researchers Links rust spacing paper](https://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Prospects-of-Entomopathogenic-Bacteria-and-Fungi-for-Biological-Control/20/1/894)

**Soil-borne rots that show on leaves (basal rot Fusarium, white rot Sclerotium cepivorum, blue mould)**
- HP survey 2019-21 in Solan, Sirmour and Kullu: basal rot most prevalent, then white rot, then blue mould rot; incidence highest at Lanacheta village, Sirmour (29.96%), lowest at Nauni, Solan (2.11%); Sirmour significantly higher than the other districts; 2019 had the highest year-mean (14.71%), 2020 the lowest (3.38%) — PST-SoilBorne (pathogen names are not in the abstract; they come from its reference list)
- Leaf/plant symptoms: basal rot starts as leaf yellowing and stunting, with leaves drying from the tip downward; roots turn pink then rot; bulb decays from the base; favoured by moist soil and about 27 °C. White rot: yellowing and dieback of leaf tips, then scales, stem plate and roots destroyed, white fluffy growth with mustard-seed-like black sclerotia; sclerotia survive in soil for many years; most severe in cool, dry soils **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion
- Sirmaur, Trans-Giri (March 2025): leaves turning yellow and the plant drying completely within two to three days, with shrivelled and decaying bulbs; cause not identified (fungal or bacterial infection, soil-borne pathogens or environmental stress suspected); recommended fungicides failed to stop spread; KVK Dhaulakuan was to diagnose — [Tribune, 20 March 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/unidentified-disease-threatens-garlic-crop-in-trans-giri-region/amp)

**Thrips (Thrips tabaci), mites, downy mildew, leaf miner**
- Thrips symptoms: feeding areas white to silvery, patches and streaks, leaves take a silvery cast and wither, tiny black "tar" spots of excrement on heavily fed leaves **[onion data]**; thrips are noted for garlic as causing silvering, curling, tip drying (Gujarat garlic paper) — NIPHM-Onion; Navsari-Thrips
- Thrips peaked at 51.27 per plant in the last week of April in a Peshawar garlic trial **[snippet; not HP]** — [Researchers Links garlic thrips paper](https://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Management-of-Garlic-Thrips-Thrips-Crop/14/1/3773)
- Eriophyid mite (*Aceria tulipae*): feeding on young leaves and between bulb layers causes stunting, twisting, curling and discoloration of foliage; life cycle 7-9 days **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion. Garlic-specific: leaf twisting and stunting early season near the growing point; major damage is in storage **[snippet; not HP]** — [AGES dry bulb mite / garlic gall mite page](https://ages.at/en/plant/plant-health/pests-from-a-to-z/dry-bulb-mite)
- Downy mildew (*Peronospora destructor*): NIPHM lists it as a pest of regional significance for onion in HP and Delhi; leaves pale green, cottony white growth, then pale yellow to brown and dry; warm humid weather **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion. For garlic: first sign a fine furry grey-white to purple growth on older leaves, spreads fast when humid **[snippet; UC IPM, not HP]** — [UC IPM downy mildew on onions and garlic](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/downy-mildew-on-onions-and-garlic/)
- Leaf miner (*Liriomyza* spp.) on garlic: serpentine mines and linear feeding punctures; confirmed on garlic in Brazil **[snippet; not HP]** — [UC IPM Leafminers](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/onion-and-garlic/Leafminers)

### Inferences
- Look-alike pairs for the vision model: (1) Stemphylium vs purple blotch: both start as flecks on mid-leaf and merge into blight; a farmer-level card cannot reliably separate them and the management is the same, so merge into one "leaf blight complex" class. Hint: purple blotch lesions are sunken whitish with a purple centre; Stemphylium has a bright yellow margin and goes olive-black when sporulating. (2) Rust (orange powdery pustules that rub off) vs yellow tip dieback from Stemphylium or from soil-borne rot (no pustules). (3) Thrips silvering with black dots vs eriophyid twisting/curling vs nutrient yellowing. (4) Soil-borne rot yellowing (starts at tips, whole plant, pull-test shows rotted roots/basal plate) vs foliar blight (spots first).
- Peak HP leaf-disease window is likely February-May for rust and blight (planting in October, harvest first week of May; the outbreaks reported were March and May). This is an inference from the reports, not a measured epidemic curve.
- Rust has now been reported from Nauni (trial) and Rajgarh (news) but no HP source gave a loss figure or management advice.

### Gaps
- No HP incidence data for purple blotch, downy mildew, thrips, eriophyid mite or leaf miner on garlic; no Kullu or Mandi leaf-disease survey for garlic found (Kullu appears only in the soil-borne survey).
- The Stemphylium survey percentage metric (incidence versus severity) is unclear in the thesis summary.
- Katoch & Kumar (2017) was seen only as an abstract snippet.
- The Kullu garlic economics article could not be opened; the Trans-Giri 2025 outbreak is not diagnosed.
- Garlic downy mildew and rust symptom descriptions come from non-HP US extension snippets.

## KQ2. Cultural practices (clove/seed selection and treatment, rotation, raised beds, drainage, mulching, sowing date, varieties)

### Takeaway
Verified HP facts are planting in early October at 20 x 10 cm with well-sprouted cloves, black polythene mulch (mid-hill HP trial), and variety data: Kandaghat Selection is moderately resistant to Stemphylium (the only HP-documented tolerance), and no variety is immune. The rest (rotation, drainage, sanitation, debris burning, sprinkler irrigation, barrier crops) is borrowed from the onion package and is practical advice, not measured in HP garlic.

### Cited Findings

**Sowing, spacing, clove selection (HP)**
- Nauni trial: well-sprouted cloves, planted 5 October 2022 and 7 October 2023, 20 cm between rows and 10 cm between plants, harvested first week of May, following the "Package of Practices for Vegetable Crops" of UHF Nauni (the package itself was not retrieved) — IJAgS-LongDay
- Black polythene mulch was the best treatment for most characters including bulb yield in a mid-hill HP weed-management study; authors suggest it as a cost-effective method; season and numbers not seen **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh, Studies on weed management in garlic](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/705a7379-98dd-4e9f-84a6-f97f46c0557d)

**Varieties and reactions (HP and cited literature)**
- Of 12 garlic genotypes screened for Stemphylium under artificial inoculation at Nauni, none was immune or resistant; Kandaghat Selection had the lowest severity (15.56%), rated moderately resistant; SG-18 and SG-17 rated moderately susceptible — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- Neri (Hamirpur) local genotype evaluation: LGCOHF-32, LGCOHF-18, LGCOHF-31 and Kandaghat Selection showed field resistance to Stemphylium blight **[snippet]** — [KrishiKosh, Genetic evaluation of local garlic genotypes, low hills HP](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810192381)
- Nauni check variety is Solan Selection; LGC-20 (259.45 q/ha), LGC-2 (240.25 q/ha) and LGC-22 (239.69 q/ha) were highest yielding (52.27%, 48.51%, 45.78% over the check); rust lowest in LGC-3 (11.00%) — IJAgS-LongDay (yield and rust are separate traits; no genotype was reported as both high-yielding and rust-free)
- Literature cited inside the thesis (not HP trials): HG-1 then G-1 had the least Stemphylium and purple blotch (Pandey et al. 1989) while dark-green-leaved varieties were severely affected; G-1, G-4 and G-305 promising (Srivastava 2005); G-294, G-324, G-351, G-368, G-369, G-176 and G-189 rated resistant in field (Mishra 2009) — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- Karnal (Haryana) AINRP trials 2006-10 found lines G-189, G-176, G-302, G-304, G-369, G-366, G-222 and G-378 "promising compared to check varieties" in a multi-trait assessment (disease resistance among them) — [Vegetos, accessions for Stemphylium resistance in northern India](https://www.vegetosindia.org/article/evaluation-of-garlic-accessions-for-growth-yield-quality-and-resistance-to-stemphylium-blight-disease-in-northern-india)

**Sanitation, rotation, drainage (all from the onion package, **[onion data]**)**
- Stemphylium: field sanitation (collect and burn crop residues), long rotations with non-host crops, proper drainage and reduced plant density — NIPHM-Onion
- Purple blotch row (table layout ambiguous): healthy seed, 2-3 year rotation with non-host crops, drainage, recommended N and P only, resistant varieties — NIPHM-Onion
- Basal rot / white rot rows (merged, so unclear which applies where): 4-5 year rotation with non-host, summer solarization with polythene sheet (25 gauge) for 30 days, deep summer ploughing, proper drainage, avoid injury during cultural operations, flood the soil in the non-growing season for basal rot, use disease-free areas, avoid late planting, poor drainage, high fertilizer doses and frequent irrigation — NIPHM-Onion
- Thrips: avoid successive garlic planting and alternate hosts (cabbage, cotton, tomato, cucumber, melons, pumpkin, strawberry); plant new crop upwind of older plantings; reflective silver or aluminium-painted mulch repels thrips (seed crop); sprinkler irrigation reduces thrips more than drip or surface irrigation; two rows of maize (or wheat inner and maize outer) as barrier — NIPHM-Onion
- Mites: avoid planting after cole crops (cauliflower may harbour high bulb mite counts), avoid successive onion/garlic crops, flood irrigation reduces soil mites, a thorough water spray washes mites off — NIPHM-Onion
- Ecological engineering: intercrops such as cowpea, maize, coriander, urdbean, 4 rows of sorghum or maize as guard crop around onion/garlic; "ecological engineering of garlic with repellent plants: Ocimum/basil and marigold as ovipositional trap crop"; bird perches at 20/acre (the last items sit in the caterpillar rows) — NIPHM-Onion
- Hot-water soaking at 50 °C for 20 minutes is listed for ONION seed, not garlic cloves; no garlic clove hot-water protocol was found — NIPHM-Onion

**Cropping systems under HP natural farming**
- Surveyed apple-based NF households in HP grow garlic with French bean (kharif) and with pea, potato, kidney bean, onion or capsicum (rabi and orchard combinations), e.g. "Pea + Garlic + Kidney French bean", "Potato + Garlic + Kidney French bean", "Apple + Pea + Garlic + Onion" — [Chandel et al. 2023, Indian Farming 73(8):26-28](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/137924/51997/379639)

### Inferences
- A defensible HP card: plant sprouted, sound cloves in early October at about 20 x 10 cm, mulch, keep drains open, choose Kandaghat Selection where Stemphylium is the concern, burn or bury-deep diseased residue after harvest, and rotate out of Allium. Only the planting date/spacing and Kandaghat reaction are HP-measured.
- The Pakistani spacing result for rust (wider spacing, less rust) is consistent with the thesis advice to reduce plant density, but is not HP data.
- Cole-crop and Allium rotation advice must be reconciled with HP cropping systems that intercrop garlic with pea/bean; no rotation trial for HP garlic was found.

### Gaps
- No HP data on pine-needle or other organic mulches; no clove size grade, clove-treatment protocol or altitude-wise sowing window (only Nauni's 5-7 October). UHF/CSK package-of-practices pages were not reached.
- Raised bed height for garlic and drainage specifics: none beyond "proper drainage".
- No HP rotation length for white rot; NIPHM gives 4-5 years for the merged basal/white rot row.
- Whether Kandaghat Selection is also tolerant to purple blotch or rust: not stated in any opened source.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
The only verified garlic doses are *Trichoderma harzianum* clove treatment at 10 g/kg cloves (Dharwad purple blotch trial) and entomopathogenic fungi at 0.007% (60 g per 10 L) against thrips (Navsari). Biocontrol gave partial control: Beauveria, Metarhizium and Lecanicillium were "moderate", cow urine 5% the least effective. For basal/white rot in HP, a Nauni pot study reports *Trichoderma* control around 61-63%, but I could not read it and no dose was retrieved.

### Cited Findings

**Trichoderma and other bioagents**
- Clove seed treatment: *T. harzianum* at 10 g/kg cloves by dry treatment (cloves then shade-dried before sowing) was applied to all treated plots in a Dharwad purple blotch trial (cv. DWG1); the best schedule combined this with two foliar fluopyram + tebuconazole sprays at 50 and 65 days after sowing (PDI 13.34 vs 34.00 in the control); the paper says treatments combining cow urine, nimbecidine (azadirachtin 0.03%) and *T. harzianum* "also showed significant impact" but the text I extracted gives no cow-urine or nimbecidine concentration, and I could not separate the effect of the clove treatment from that of the sprays (all treated plots got Trichoderma). The abstract reports 5.77 t/ha for the best treatment while the results text and table give 4.81 t/ha, an internal inconsistency — Dharwad-PB
- Foliar Trichoderma on garlic purple blotch (Bangladesh): "Trichoderma harzianum liquid solution" sprayed three times at 15-day intervals from first disease at 30 days after sowing gave disease incidence 33.33/43.33/43.33% at 30/45/60 DAS and severity 13.33/18.67/19.33%, against control 86.67/96.67/100% incidence and 28.67/81.00/100% severity; mancozeb 80% WP at 2 g/L gave 53.33/56.67/70.00% incidence and 18.00/46.00/55.33% severity, and copper oxychloride (Sulcox 50% WP) at 3 g/L gave 43.33/50.00/56.67% incidence and 11.33/44.00/44.00% severity; no Trichoderma concentration or formulation is stated — Dhaka-PB
- Egypt, garlic purple blotch field trials: *Trichoderma album* beat *T. harzianum*; *Bacillus megaterium* generally beat the Trichoderma species; the copper fungicide beat all biocides **[snippet; not HP]** — [Biocides as an alternative control method of garlic purple blotch, J. Egyptian Plant Pathol.](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_91694.html)
- White rot (*S. cepivorum*) of garlic: a Nauni pot-culture study reported *Trichoderma virens* and *Trichoderma* sp. strain 1 gave 63.17% and 61.13% disease control against 75.65% for tebuconazole **[snippet; this appears to be the KrishiKosh thesis "Studies on biological control of white rot of garlic", whose page I could not open; no dose seen]** — [KrishiKosh record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/e2b87fd6-f36e-40e5-aa9f-daea3446713d)
- Egypt, garlic white rot: a three-species *Trichoderma* combination cut incidence by 50.0% (2016/17) and 40.0% (2017/18) in field trials **[snippet; not HP; the search result page was probably this BNRC paper but I did not open it, so the attribution is unconfirmed]** — [BNRC garlic white rot paper](https://BNRC.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s42269-019-0064-3.pdf)
- Package-level advice: apply *Trichoderma* spp. and *Pseudomonas fluorescens* as seed/seedling/planting-material, nursery and soil treatment; farmer-made biopesticides for own use do not need registration; no dose is printed for garlic or onion **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion
- Mustard comparison only: *Trichoderma* mixture at 2x10^9 cfu/g, seed treatment 10 g/kg, soil 2.5 kg/ha pre-incubated in FYM, foliar 0.2% at 50 and 70 days after sowing against Sclerotinia **[not garlic, not HP]** — [IJAgS 24970](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/24970)

**Thrips: entomopathogenic fungi (Navsari, Gujarat, Rabi 2024-25, cv. Gujarat Garlic-7, two sprays 14 days apart, 500 L/ha)**
- *Metarhizium anisopliae*, *Beauveria bassiana* and *Lecanicillium lecanii*, each as a 1.15% WP formulation at 0.007% (60 g/10 L); mean thrips after the second spray: L. lecanii 10.34, M. anisopliae 10.98, B. bassiana 11.33 per plant; "moderate control" overall — Navsari-Thrips
- Fresh cow urine 5% (1000 mL/10 L) gave 11.82 thrips/plant after the second spray and was the least effective treatment — Navsari-Thrips
- Brazil: *B. bassiana* plus potassium silicate looked more promising than *B. bassiana* alone **[snippet; not HP]** — [Repositorio IF Goiano](https://repositorio.ifgoiano.edu.br/handle/prefix/1226?mode=full)

**Traps, predators**
- Blue sticky/pan traps 4-5 per acre, 15 cm above the canopy for thrips monitoring **[onion data]**; the package lists conservation (not release doses) of *Orius*, predatory thrips, syrphids, lacewings, coccinellids, spiders and the thrips parasitoid *Ceranisus menes*; predatory mites and *Stethorus* for mites — NIPHM-Onion
- Pheromone traps 4-5/acre, light trap 1/acre, *Trichogramma pretiosum* 40,000/acre 4-5 times weekly from flower initiation, and *Steinernema feltiae* 20-120 crore IJ/acre are listed for the onion caterpillar complex (*Helicoverpa*, *Spodoptera*, cutworm) **[onion data; caterpillars are not a main garlic leaf problem in HP]** — NIPHM-Onion

### Inferences
- Treat the 10 g/kg *T. harzianum* clove treatment as the only dose-backed biological step for garlic; it is a protective seed/soil step and not shown to cure foliar blight. The Bangladesh result (foliar Trichoderma beating mancozeb) is from a single site and unnamed dose, so cannot be turned into a card dose.
- Biocontrol of thrips on garlic is weaker than neem products (see KQ4); a "moderate control" statement is all the data support for fungal sprays.
- Rust and Stemphylium blight: no biocontrol agent with data was retrieved.

### Gaps
- No dose for *Pseudomonas fluorescens*, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Beauveria* or *Trichoderma* in HP garlic; no HP field trial of any biocontrol in garlic; the Nauni white rot study was not readable.
- Cow urine and nimbecidine concentrations in the Dharwad combination schedule not extracted.
- No Allium-specific predatory mite or lacewing release rate retrieved.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations

### Takeaway
For thrips on garlic, NSKE 5% (500 g/10 L) cut thrips to 6.28-8.07 per plant versus 18.45-24.95 in the control and gave a yield of 62.50 q/ha versus 40.00 (Gujarat), the best-cost botanical; azadirachtin 0.04% was slightly better but poor value. For purple blotch/Stemphylium, no concentration-defined botanical was retrieved and copper/Bordeaux was weak against Stemphylium in vitro, so natural methods cannot be promised to control the blight complex.

### Cited Findings

**Thrips (Navsari-Thrips; Gujarat Garlic-7; not HP)**
- Azadirachtin 10,000 ppm at 0.04% (400 mL/10 L) lowest thrips: 7.36 per plant after the first spray and 5.28 after the second; NSKE 5% (500 g/10 L) 8.07 after the first spray and 6.28 after the second; Novel Plus 2% (200 mL/10 L; a commercial product whose composition is not given) 8.96 and 7.53; control highest (18.45 first spray, up to 24.95 second) — Navsari-Thrips
- Yield: control 40.00 q/ha; azadirachtin 64.91; NSKE 62.50; Novel Plus 52.98. Net return highest for NSKE (Rs 105,025/ha); azadirachtin ICBR only 1:1.16 because of cost; Novel Plus ICBR 1:17.65 — Navsari-Thrips
- Another garlic trial (Peshawar, 2017-18) found emamectin best with neem seed extract and neem oil next among botanicals **[snippet; no concentration seen]** — [Researchers Links garlic thrips paper](https://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Management-of-Garlic-Thrips-Thrips-Crop/14/1/3773)

**Purple blotch / Stemphylium**
- Botanicals tested as 100% (1:1) crude leaf extract, heated at 60 °C for 15 min, three sprays at 15-day intervals from 30 DAS: *Lantana camara* gave the lowest incidence (26.67/33.33/33.33% at 30/45/60 DAS), neem (*Azadirachta indica*) leaf 56.67/56.67/60.00% (control 86.67/96.67/100%); severity at 60 DAS: neem 30.67% vs control 100%; Ocimum sanctum 53.33% incidence at 60 DAS; *Spilanthes* 60.00%; *Raphanus raphanistrum* 56.67% — Dhaka-PB (a 1:1 crude extract is not a farmer-ready concentration)
- Organic manures reduced garlic purple blotch incidence relative to untreated control in an Allahabad (UP) field trial: farmyard manure, goat manure, neem cake and vermicompost, with one unnamed treatment (T2) best; no rates in the abstract — [JPP 2020, 9(2):2048-2050](https://phytojournal.com/archives/2020.v9.i2.11155/efficacy-of-organic-amedments-against-the-purple-blotch-of-garlic-casued-by-alternaria-porri-ellis-cif)
- Onion trial: NSKE 5% gave 44.24% control of purple blotch pathogen over control and *T. harzianum* spray the lowest severity (37.67%) **[snippet; onion, abstract-only]** — [IJPPR record](https://mail.i-scholar.in/index.php/Ijppr/article/view/80850)
- In vitro mycelial inhibition of *S. vesicarium* by copper oxychloride (Blitox) was only 21.66% and Bordeaux mixture 21.34%, compared with mancozeb 82.13%, tebuconazole 100% and carbendazim + mancozeb 100% (lab only; no copper field trial in the thesis); field control in HP was shown only for tebuconazole (Folicur 0.1%, three sprays at 10-day intervals from disease onset, 71.53% reduction, bulb yield 145.7 q/ha), Companion 0.25% (68.93%) and Tilt 0.1% (64.13%) — Nauni-Stemph thesis
- In Bangladesh, copper oxychloride at 3 g/L gave 44.00% severity at 60 DAS vs 100% control, so copper helped somewhat against purple blotch in field — Dhaka-PB
- Mancozeb is not a natural input; the national onion package's only chemical lists for Stemphylium/white rot use mancozeb or zineb 600-800 g/acre **[onion data]** — NIPHM-Onion

**Rust and others**
- No garlic rust botanical concentration, no milk/whey or sulphur dose on garlic, and no downy mildew botanical was found in an opened source.
- NSKE, neem oil or any botanical for eriophyid mite or leaf miner on garlic: not found in an opened source (the onion package gives chemical sprays only).

### Inferences
- NSKE 5% is the only botanical with an opened, garlic-specific, concentration-defined result, and only for thrips; its recipe is "500 g per 10 L" in the trial, i.e. 50 g/L.
- Natural methods cannot control severe Stemphylium/purple blotch on a susceptible variety once blight has set in: the best organic spray evidence is a crude extract in one Bangladeshi trial, and the HP thesis shows copper is a weak option in the lab. Prevention (variety, drainage, residue removal, rotation) matters more.
- Rust: no natural curative step retrieved; the card should say so and advise scouting, wider spacing and tolerant genotypes.

### Gaps
- No sulphur, milk, whey, buttermilk, soap, karanj oil or garlic-chilli extract trial on garlic (rust, mildew or blight) with a concentration.
- NSKE preparation (kernel weight, soaking time) not in the opened garlic papers beyond "500 g/10 L".
- No HP field trial of NSKE, neem oil or azadirachtin on garlic thrips.

## KQ5. Natural-farming formulations with doses; HP PK3Y/SPNF/UHF sources

### Takeaway
Recipes for Beejamrit, Jeevamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra and Brahmastra are documented by UHF Nauni authors for general HP natural farming, with a Brahmastra spray dilution (2.5-3 L in 200 L water) but no spray dilution for Neemastra or Agniastra, and nothing garlic-specific. No HP source gave a garlic dose, a khatti lassi dose or a Dashparni recipe. Cow urine alone at 5% did poorly against garlic thrips.

### Cited Findings

**Recipes (UHF-NF; HP general, not garlic-specific)**
- Beejamrit (seed/planting material coating): 5 kg cow dung tied in cloth; 1 L water with 50 g lime left overnight; squeeze the dung bundle into the water three times; add a handful of farm soil and 5 L desi cow urine plus the lime water; spread over seeds, mix by hand, dry, then sow. The article does not mention garlic cloves — UHF-NF
- Jeevamrit: 10 kg cow dung + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg pulse flour + a handful of bund soil, stirred and fermented in shade for 48 hours; described as promoting soil microbes and "preventing fungal and bacterial plant diseases" (no data) — UHF-NF
- Neemastra: 100 L water + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg cow dung + 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves, covered with gunny bag in shade for 48 hours, stirred clockwise for 1 minute morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sucking insects, small caterpillars; no spray dilution stated — UHF-NF
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves + 500 g tobacco powder + 500 g green chilli chutney + 250 g garlic chutney in 20 L cow urine, boiled on low flame under a lid, kept 48 hours and stirred morning and evening; for sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers; no spray dilution stated — UHF-NF
- Brahmastra (1 acre): any five leaf plants (darek, custard apple, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta) at 200 g leaf paste each, in 4 L cow urine, heated to boiling, cooled 48 hours; mix 2.5-3 L of the solution in 200 L water and spray; for sucking insects, weevils and caterpillars — UHF-NF
- Mulching (acchadana) and whapasa are the other two pillars; target soil 25-32 °C and 65-72% moisture — UHF-NF
- Neemastra, Agniastra and Brahmastra are listed as HP plant-protection products; the 2023 note describes them only as made "from cow dung, cow urine and green chillies" — [Indian Farming 73(8):26-28](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/137924/51997/379639)

**Trial and field evidence**
- Jeevamrit at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50% was tested on cabbage in HP mid-hills; 40% gave maximum chlorophyll and antioxidant activity and 50% went past the optimum (a physiology study; method of application not seen; cabbage, not garlic) — [Verma et al. 2026, IJAgS 96(6):712-718](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/172673/67626/548104)
- HP farmer testimony (Kinnaur/Shimla): Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi "effective in controlling fungal diseases" on a farm growing apple, pea, carrot, cucumber, bean, coriander, radish (no garlic, no measurements); another Kinnaur farm that grows garlic among its crops controlled woolly apple aphid with Dashparni ark (no recipe, no garlic claim) — UHF-NF
- Cow urine 5% against garlic thrips (Gujarat) was the least effective treatment — Navsari-Thrips

**Other recipe variants (not HP)**
- Buttermilk-based preparation (Tamil Nadu): 500 mL to 1 L of a fermented mixture (5 L buttermilk, 1 L tender coconut water, 1-2 kg arappu leaves, 500 g waste fruit, 7-day ferment) in 10 L water, said to add resistance to fungal diseases; this is not khatti lassi and has no trial data **[snippet]** — [TNAU Agritech, Arappu buttermilk](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/org_farm/orgfarm_cropproduction_plantprotection_arappu.html)
- A CSK HPKV bio-pesticide table lists Darekastra + Jeevamrit at 10% against aphids/leafhoppers on okra in HP, not garlic **[snippet]** — [source page not confirmed in my results]
- The UHF Agniastra recipe includes 500 g tobacco powder; I found nothing in the opened sources on tobacco residues or virus transmission to Allium, so no caution is cited either way.

### Inferences
- Beejamrit on garlic cloves is a plausible, recipe-backed seed-treatment step (the UHF article says treated seeds control seed-borne diseases) but no source applied it to garlic cloves; present it as "general PK3Y practice" without a garlic efficacy claim.
- Given the Gujarat result (NSKE 5% and azadirachtin much better than cow urine 5%), Neemastra/Agniastra-type sprays are likely to give weaker thrips control than NSKE; this is an inference, since neither was tested on garlic.
- No Stemphylium or purple blotch benefit of any "astra" or Jeevamrit is supported by data; a card should call them preventive, not curative.

### Gaps
- No official PK3Y/SPNF/UHF source for garlic-specific inputs, for khatti lassi dilution, Dashparni ark or sonthastra recipes (spnfhp.in files I could open were certification documents; the NAHEP cow-based formulations PDF did not download).
- Spray dilution of Neemastra and Agniastra not stated in UHF-NF.
- No data on jeevamrit/beejamrit effects on any garlic disease.

## KQ6. Documented HP traditional / indigenous practices for garlic pests and diseases

### Takeaway
I found no documented HP traditional practice specific to garlic pests or diseases. The closest HP evidence is generic natural-farming practice (beejamrit seed coating, Dashparni ark on an apple pest, farmers who grow garlic in NF systems) and garlic-based sprays used on other crops.

### Cited Findings
- HP natural-farming households grow garlic mixed with apple, pea, French bean, potato, onion and capsicum, so garlic is routinely under PK3Y-type management (no pest practices recorded) — [Indian Farming 73(8):26-28](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/137924/51997/379639)
- Beejamrit is described as an old practice of treating seed with "local cow urine, cow dung and soil from the farm", with treated seeds "equally effective in controlling seed borne diseases" — UHF-NF
- A Kinnaur farmer with garlic in her model used Dashparni ark against woolly apple aphid; no garlic use is recorded — UHF-NF
- In the Allium literature, garlic itself (crushed) is an ingredient of Agniastra (250 g garlic chutney in the UHF recipe above) — UHF-NF
- Ash dusting of vegetables against beetles and aphids is documented in ITK inventories from UP and tribal regions (not HP, not garlic) **[snippet]** — [Vikaspedia, Indigenous technologies practised by farmers](https://vikaspedia.in/agriculture/best-practices/sustainable-agriculture/crop-management/indigenous-technologies-practised-by-farmers)

### Inferences
- For a farmer card, label any ash, cow-urine or local-plant spray on garlic as "traditional, not tested on garlic in HP", and do not give a dose.

### Gaps
- No HP ITK paper (Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge, Himachal Journal of Agricultural Research, NIF database) on garlic was reached; no HP traditional clove storage or seed treatment documented.
- Searches for HP garlic practices with cow urine or jeevamrit on cloves returned nothing for garlic.
