## KQ1. Which 4-6 leaf-visible problems matter most for HP apple, what do they look like, what weather/altitude favours them, and what incidence data exist?

### Takeaway
Recommended vision classes for HP apple leaves: (1) apple scab, (2) Marssonina leaf blotch (the main premature-leaf-fall disease), (3) Alternaria leaf blotch/spot, (4) powdery mildew, (5) woolly apple aphid (cottony colonies on twigs/shoots, leaf-petiole level), (6) mite bronzing (European red mite / two-spotted mite); San Jose scale is mainly a twig/fruit problem and is a secondary class. HP-specific incidence data are strongest for Marssonina (survey incidence 72-100%), Alternaria (11-50% in Shimla), powdery mildew (25-54% in Kinnaur) and the woolly aphid/mite/scale blocks surveyed in Shimla and Kinnaur; no altitude-resolved incidence was found for any of them.

### Cited Findings

**Apple scab (Venturia inaequalis)**
- Symptoms on leaf: first on the LOWER side as velvety brown to olive-green or mousy-black spots; outlines sharpen, turn dark brown to black and appear on both sides; leaves turn yellowish brown and drop prematurely in midsummer; "sheet scab" = whole leaf covered with velvety black lesions. On fruit: dark olive-green spots, usually calyx end, later circular, brown-black, corky, fruit knotty with cracks — GIZ-IPM
- Cycle: overwinters as pseudothecia in fallen leaves; ascospores released March-April cause primary infection; conidia cause repeated secondary infections to harvest. Cool temperatures of 18.2-23.8 C with minimum leaf wetness of 9 h (primary) and 5.9 h (secondary) favour infection — GIZ-IPM
- Risk factors listed: >10% of shoots scabbed in autumn, dense canopies, strong late-terminating growth, rainy winter/spring, prolonged snow cover, leaf-wet periods around flowering — GIZ-IPM
- Trial authors describe scab as "devastating", favoured by "cool, moist climate during early spring", persisting in "shady and moist conditions"; citing older work, insufficient control can cause loss up to 70% of production value (Gupta 1992) and 100% loss if unmanaged (Belete & Boyraz 2017) — IJEP-scab
- HP outbreak 2020: scab reported in Kullu (Banjar, Ani, Manikaran, Lag valley, parts of Manali), Mandi (Jenjhehi, Gada Gushain, Janjehli), Shimla (Kharapathar, Nawar valley, Mandalgarh, Chhohara valley, described as most affected) and Kinnaur; growers blamed delayed sprays during lockdown; no percentages or altitudes given — [Tribune, 27 Jul 2020](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/scab-infection-hits-apple-crop-118409)
- A UHF statement that scab was seen only in orchards where the recommended spray schedule was not followed **[snippet via search summary; original article not opened]** — [Tribune, scab infection coverage](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/apple-scab-hits-shimla-belt-hard-120037/amp)
- Trial site context: unsprayed Starking Delicious orchard "having consistently high apple scab incidence" at Barshaini, Manikaran valley, Kullu, 2016-17 — IJEP-scab

**Marssonina leaf blotch (Diplocarpon mali / Marssonina coronaria) = "premature leaf fall"**
- HP history: first recorded 1992 in Kotkhai; epiphytotic in 1996; now affecting "more than 90 per cent of orchards"; in some orchards "no foliage was left on the trees by the harvest time" — Goyal-Marssonina
- Symptoms: leaf spots on upper surface of mature leaves, 5-7 mm, grayish brown, often tinged purple at the periphery, small black acervuli visible; coalesce, surrounding area turns chlorotic, then defoliation. In HP symptoms appear after heavy rains in June-July as sudden yellowing with brown to dark-brown circular spots that coalesce into blotches; pathogen attacks the lower part of the tree first; fruits are seen hanging from defoliated branches — Goyal-Marssonina
- GIZ-IPM description: dark green irregular spots on mature leaves becoming brownish in summer, then dark brown-black and coalescing into a big blotch with pin-head acervuli; yellow surround, severe defoliation in the rains; fruit stays small and poorly coloured; next-year spurs affected (about one-third of flowers of such plants bloom in November-December). Optimum 20-25 C; leaf wet at least 4 h; symptoms 8-12 days after infection; if leaves stay wet >40 h blight may appear. "In integrated cultivation, Red Delicious has been the most severely infested variety. All varieties grown in Northern India are infested" — GIZ-IPM
- Leaf-litter inoculum in Shimla/Kullu: new conidia formed in old acervuli from June (May at Kotkhai and Rohroo: 4.8x10^3 and 2.4x10^3 conidia/ml); no ascospore stage (apothecia) found in any sample; conidia release correlated positively with rainfall and RH, negatively with max temperature; peak catch on 7 Aug 2014 at 19 C with ample rain and RH >85% in the preceding 24 h — Goyal-Marssonina
- Survey of 10 HP locations: incidence 100% at Seobag (Kullu) to 72% at Sangla (Kinnaur); severity 68.2% at Patlikuhal (Seobag) down to 23.2% at Kumarsain (Narkanda, Shimla) **[snippet; KrishiKosh/ICAR abstract not opened]** — [KrishiKosh record 1/5810146916](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810146916)
- Epidemic outbreak paper: leaf ageing then average temperature above 20 C with at least 70% RH continuously for 5-6 days drives yellowing and defoliation; dark brown blotch on the upper leaf side appears in the second week of June under humid weather **[snippet; paper behind login]** — [Indian Phytopathology, IPPJ 11109](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/11109)

**Alternaria leaf blotch / spot**
- Symptoms (HP manual): late summer and rains, circular dark-brown spots with dark purple edges, sometimes irregular dark brown to black; brown elongated lesions on petioles; heavily infected leaves yellow and fall; sunken circular brown fruit spots 6-8 weeks before harvest. Needs 10-12 h leaf wetness at about 20-25 C; optimum for infection and sporulation 25-30 C; overwinters in dead leaves, twig injuries, dormant buds; fruit mummies and dead wood are inoculum sources — GIZ-IPM
- Timeline from the neighbouring J&K high-density orchards (NOT HP): Alternaria leaf blotch first noticed second week of May as light-brown circular 0.5-2.0 mm spots, coalescing into 6.0-10.0 mm blotches by fourth week of May, leaves yellow and defoliate by last week of June; Alternaria leaf spot appears third week of May as circular brown lesions with purple margin, silvery grey by late June; pathogens are weak and mostly infect wounded leaves; A. alternata, A. tenuissima and A. arborescens involved — [Rashid et al. 2025, Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences 95(8):989-995 (SKUAST-K, Srinagar, 1685 m)](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/155250/62990/494987)
- Shimla incidence 11.00-50.00% and severity 6.20-25.90% across 2015-16; most prevalent in Jubbal in 2022-23 **[snippet via search summary; KrishiKosh/i-Scholar records not opened]** — [KrishiKosh Alternaria thesis](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810207107); [i-Scholar IJAS 180170](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/Ijas/article/view/180170)

**Powdery mildew (Podosphaera leucotricha)**
- Symptoms: white powdery mass on the LOWER side of young leaves with chlorotic patches above; later both surfaces; leaves crinkle, curl or roll upward along the edges, narrow appearance; infected shoots stunted with shortened internodes and silver-grey growth, die-back; flower buds covered with white powder; pollinizer varieties more affected. Spores germinate at 10-25 C and RH 70-100%; fastest in April-May at 18-22 C with intermittent rain. Risk factors include "dry, warm climate", summer shoot growth, "hill and mountain locations" — GIZ-IPM
- Kinnaur survey 2020 and 2021: incidence about 25.42-53.80%, severity about 39.75-66.25%; starts first week of May, peaks in July; 18-24 C and 50-65% RH favour it; less susceptible cultivars Red Delicious, Royal Delicious, Red Velox, Auvil Early Fuji, Oregon Spur II; extremely susceptible Granny Smith, Golden Delicious and the Gala group; altitudes of sites not given (screening at KVK Sharbo) — IJEP-PM (page summary)

**Woolly apple aphid (Eriosoma lanigerum)**
- Description: small purple insects 1.5-3.0 mm in colonies covered in white woolly wax visible from a distance; feed on twigs, branches and roots; galls on roots and shoots; honeydew causes russet and sooty growth on fruit; aerial attack April-May to October-November, moves to roots in winter — GIZ-IPM
- Infested nursery plants are the main source of spread; plants have "pale green leaves and whitish cottony patches on stems and branches"; multiplication conspicuous June-August; aphid first recorded in Shimla hills in 1909; "no specific monitoring procedures or treatment thresholds have been developed" — IH-Spiti-WAA
- HP status: in Kullu valley and the middle valley of Kinnaur the aerial population is "managed successfully" by the introduced parasitoid Aphelinus mali (introduced mid-1930s); in Shimla, Mandi, lower and upper Kullu, Kinnaur and Spiti "this insect pest appears in epidemic form and needs regular insecticidal applications" — IJE-phosmet
- Survey incidence (Frontiers-NF Table 3, 140 farms, Chaupal/Jubbal in Shimla and Nichar/Pooh in Kinnaur): woolly aphid incidence 29.85-50.08% on natural-farming (NF) orchards and 17.5-40.83% on conventional (CF) orchards; the abstract's "50.08% vs 17.5%" pairs numbers from different blocks — Frontiers-NF

**Mites (European red mite Panonychus ulmi; two-spotted spider mite Tetranychus urticae)**
- European red mite: female red to chocolate brown with fine white spots, 0.4 mm; overwintering eggs spherical, pinkish-red with a hair from the centre, on spurs and twigs October-March; hatch mid-April to end June; 5-7 or more generations; dry hot weather favours; symptoms are yellowing/bronzing of leaves with white minute specks; no webbing — GIZ-IPM
- Two-spotted mite: pale yellow/green with two dark patches; dense webbing on leaf surface; bronzing; numerous yellow spots on the upper surface; leaves turn copper and curl inwards — GIZ-IPM
- Mite incidence in Frontiers-NF Table 3: 13-26.15% (NF) and 5.92-22.88% (CF) across the four blocks — Frontiers-NF
- European red mite "has emerged as a serious pest in apple orchards of HP" **[snippet; 2023 paper reached only through a search summary]** — [search result summary, InformaticsJournals JBC](https://informaticsjournals.com/index.php/jbc/article/download/15068/12853/15717)

**San Jose scale (Quadraspidiotus perniciosus) and other apple problems**
- Symptoms: grey scale shell over a lemon-yellow insect; needle-tip brown spots on twigs, dark grey crust when severe; halo-like red colouration or measles spots around feeding points, especially twigs and fruit; four overlapping generations April-November; peak infestation March-June; overwinters as "black cap" — GIZ-IPM
- Frontiers-NF Table 3 San Jose scale incidence: 11.83-26.86% (NF) and 10.25-25.75% (CF) — Frontiers-NF
- Same table: leaf folder 13.69-41% (NF) vs 5.5-15.73% (CF); apple root borer 22.73-42.38% (NF) vs 29.83-34.33% (CF) — Frontiers-NF. (No description of leaf folder symptoms was found in an opened HP source.)
- Other diseases in the Frontiers-NF survey (non-leaf): canker, root rot, collar rot; Alternaria severity 1.83-24.95% and "Marsonina" 0.41-8.7% incidence in blocks where recorded; powdery mildew 0.25-1.53% (low in this sample) — Frontiers-NF
- HP manual also covers blossom thrips (flower/bud rasping, distorted one-sided blossoms), white root rot and collar rot (below-ground, not leaf-visible, but cause leaf yellowing, bronzing, drooping and small leaves) — GIZ-IPM

**Altitude/zone anchors**
- Apple grown at 1500-2700 m asl with 1000-1250 mm well-distributed rain (national statement in the HP manual) — GIZ-IPM
- Frontiers-NF sample covered Zone III (High Hills Temperate Wet: Chaupal, Jubbal) and Zone IV (Dry Temperate High Hills: Nichar, Pooh); block altitudes not reported — Frontiers-NF
- One SPNF apple orchard at Theog (Shimla) is at 2300 m — [HP Agriculture Dept success story, Satya Devi](https://agriculture.hp.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Satya-Devi_Theog_Shimla_Eng.pdf)

### Inferences
- Look-alike pairs for the vision model: (1) scab (velvety olive-brown, often lower side, early spring, fruit also scabbed) vs Marssonina (dark green/brown spots with tiny black dots, upper side of mature leaves, June-August, lower tree first) vs Alternaria (dark brown with purple edge, petiole lesions, late summer); (2) Marssonina vs Alternaria blotch both end in yellowing and drop — a photo of the whole shoot (defoliation from the base) and month of year help separate them; (3) powdery mildew (white coating, upward leaf rolling, shoots with silver tips) vs mite bronzing vs woolly aphid honeydew/sooty growth; (4) European red mite (no webs, bronzing, red adults) vs two-spotted mite (webbing, yellow stippling, copper leaves).
- Timing of occurrence in HP (from the opened sources): scab March-June cool wet spell; powdery mildew April-July; Marssonina after heavy June-July rains through August; Alternaria late May-August (J&K data) and rainy season (HP manual); mites peak July-August (GIZ-IPM, KQ3); woolly aphid conspicuous June-August.
- Because the natural-farming vs conventional survey (Frontiers-NF) shows higher aphid, mite, leaf-folder and scale incidence on several NF blocks, the app should not promise that natural farming eliminates pests.

### Gaps
- No HP incidence/severity figures for scab (no survey numbers found, only outbreak news), and none that are altitude-resolved for any disease or pest.
- Marssonina and Alternaria HP incidence numbers are search-snippet only.
- Leaf folder description and thresholds; two-spotted mite incidence in HP; fire blight is mentioned in news but not verified for HP.
- No HP threshold values found for woolly aphid (explicitly stated as not developed).

## KQ2. Cultural practices (pruning, sanitation, leaf litter, urea, airflow, mulching, varieties/rootstocks)

### Takeaway
Orchard-floor sanitation is the main non-chemical scab/Marssonina/Alternaria lever and is explicitly in the HP spray schedule: collect fallen leaves into a compost pit or spray 5% urea on the orchard floor (10 kg per 200 L water) at pre-leaf-fall in October-November. Pruning for air and light, early shoot closure, rogueing powdery-mildew shoots, and avoiding excessive winter pruning cuts (woolly aphid) complete the package. Rootstock evidence for woolly aphid is lab-screened (MM 111 and MM 106 low infestation), and scab-resistant varieties exist but no HP field evidence was found.

### Cited Findings

**Leaf-litter sanitation and urea**
- HP schedule note: "The fallen leaves of apple should be collected and decomposed in a compost pit or 5% urea sprayed on orchard floor to ensure fast decomposition of infected leaves" — Spray-2023
- "Foliar diseases: the pathogen overwinters on the fallen leaves ... orchard sanitation by removing weeds and bushes and by removing the fallen leaf litter ... is one of the most important measures to reduce the initial inoculum. Adopt proper pruning ... for adequate air circulation and rapid drying of foliage" — Spray-2023
- Powdery mildew: "Prune off the silver terminals and destroy them" — Spray-2023
- Urea 5% (10 kg / 200 L water) at pre-leaf-fall in October-November "destroys the overwintering inoculum" (stated for scab, Marssonina and Alternaria); an alternative given is 5 kg/ha of urea "if scab-infected leaves are found on more than 10% of the shoots in the fall ... shortly before leaf fall" (count the nitrogen in the annual balance) — GIZ-IPM. (The two quantities are as printed; they are not the same kind of rate.)
- Fallen leaves raked into the lane in late autumn or shredded with a flail mower speeds decomposition; doing it only in spring is less effective; copper should not be used after harvest so as not to disrupt leaf decomposition — GIZ-IPM
- Sprays of Bacillus subtilis strain BIMV 262 or Microsphaeropsis ochracea on the orchard floor accelerate leaf decomposition (products from the European literature, listed in the HP manual; the dose per 200 L is in a table that came out scrambled: "Bacillus subtilis 200 gm" and "Microsphaeropsis ochracea 200 gm" appear in the dosage column) — GIZ-IPM
- HP study by Thakur & Sharma (1994-96): 5% urea at leaf abscission on scabbed leaves speeded litter breakdown, 59 micro-organisms isolated from treated litter, several reduced pseudothecial initials and ascospore production **[snippet; Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences 69(2), 1999 record not opened]** — [ICAR Krishi record 74529](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/74529)
- J&K contrast: SKUAST-K advised 3-4% urea because of reported bud phytotoxicity at higher rates **[snippet; news article not opened]** — [Kashmir Reader](https://kashmirreader.com/2021/10/18/clearing-misunderstandings-vis-a-vis-post-harvest-urea-application-in-apple/)

**Pruning, canopy, weeds, irrigation**
- Proper pruning for air circulation and light penetration is listed under non-chemical management of scab, Marssonina, Alternaria and powdery mildew; effective weed management in the rainy season reduces Marssonina development — GIZ-IPM
- Scab: loose tree structure and calm growth reduce susceptibility; promote early shoot closure; use drip irrigation, or if overhead sprinklers are used keep trees from staying wet longer than 8 h — GIZ-IPM
- Powdery mildew: cut or tear off mildew-infested shoots during winter pruning and in the season ("chemical control alone is not sufficient"); encourage shoot closure in summer — GIZ-IPM
- Canker and mummies: remove infected parts, mummified fruits, dead twigs and pruned branches; balanced fertilisers on leaf/soil analysis; irrigation in hot dry spells — Spray-2023
- Woolly aphid: moderate fertilisation, avoid excessive winter pruning cuts (prefer a few large cuts over many small ones), encourage early shoot termination, remove water sprouts and cover pruning cuts and wounds with recommended paints, prune and burn infested parts, plant aphid-free nursery stock — GIZ-IPM
- San Jose scale: remove and burn heavily infested branches; open canopy so sprays reach all surfaces; cracked bark hinders coverage — GIZ-IPM
- Mites: risk factors are dry hot weather, proximity to roads (dust, higher temperatures), a spray sequence damaging to predatory mites and indiscriminate broad-spectrum insecticides; carry predatory-mite-rich shoots from other trees into locally infested trees; hang colonised shoots or insert colonised coir ropes/felt bands in winter — GIZ-IPM

**Mulching and ground cover**
- Dry grass mulch in tree basins plus live mulch (bean, pea, cereals) between basins, and eight intercrops (pea, rajmash, wheat, cabbage with marigold, garlic, fenugreek); garlic, marigold and cabbage on orchard borders as repellent / trap crops — IH-Mashobra
- Species-rich undergrowth and alternate-row mulching to keep flowering herbs for beneficials; do not mulch or use herbicide during flowering (blossom thrips) — GIZ-IPM
- No source retrieved shows that mulch or intercrops reduce scab, mildew or aphid counts in HP apple.

**Varieties and rootstocks**
- Lab/glasshouse screening at IARI Regional Station Shimla (1900 m), 12 accessions, 0-4 rating of aerial woolly aphid colonies on potted plants, mean rating over July-Nov: zero for Northern Spy, Malus prunifolia var. Maruba and var. Ringo Asami (called "immune"); MM 111 0.42; MM 106 0.50; M. baccata Shillong 0.50; M. baccata Kinnaur 1.42; M. sargentii 2.50; Nagasaki zumi 2.96; M. baccata Dhak 3.17; M. baccata Kashmir and M. pumila 3.42 (highest). The authors note resistance of MM-series lines has broken down in some countries — IJAgS-WAA-res
- HP manual lists resistant rootstocks for woolly aphid: MM 111, M793, MM778, MM789, MM106, M21, M25 — GIZ-IPM
- Collar rot: MM 106 is "highly susceptible", M 7 and M 26 susceptible; resistant rootstocks M4, M9, MM116, MM115; no rootstock is resistant to white root rot — GIZ-IPM. (So MM 106 is a trade-off: low woolly-aphid rating but high collar-rot susceptibility.)
- Scab-resistant ("vf") varieties named in the HP manual: Co-op 12, Co-op 13, Priscilla, Macfree, Florina, RedPop, Giga, Topaz, Bonita, Modi; the manual warns that the single-gene resistance "has already been overcome by the scab fungus" in many regions and such varieties should still be sprayed in heavy-rain periods — GIZ-IPM
- Powdery mildew cultivar response in Kinnaur (see KQ1): Granny Smith, Golden Delicious and Gala group highly susceptible; Red/Royal Delicious, Red Velox, Auvil Early Fuji, Oregon Spur II less susceptible — IJEP-PM
- San Jose scale in Kinnaur: Golden Delicious more susceptible than Royal Delicious, Red Delicious and crab apple **[snippet; search summary of a Kinnaur study]** — [search result: ICAR IJAgS record 30078](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/30078)

### Inferences
- The most defensible card for scab/Marssonina/Alternaria is "end-of-season litter management": collect and compost leaves or spray 5% urea (10 kg per 200 L) on leaves/orchard floor before leaf fall in October-November, plus prune for airflow. It is the only non-chemical measure that appears in both the official HP spray schedule and the HP training manual.
- The woolly-aphid rootstock data are from potted plants in a glasshouse (aerial colonies only), so they support "choose MM 111 / MM 106 rootstock for new plantings" but not a claim about field control of root colonies.
- Scab-resistant varieties are listed in an HP manual but no HP field performance or availability data were found; do not present them as a recommendation for HP orchards without checking nursery availability and market acceptance.

### Gaps
- No HP trial quantifying the reduction in scab or Marssonina inoculum from urea or leaf removal (the Thakur & Sharma record is a snippet and does not give percentages in the opened summary).
- No HP data on pine-needle, grass or other mulch effects on foliar disease or aphid pressure.
- Rootstock availability in HP nurseries and field performance data for MM 111 vs MM 106 not found.
- The pre-leaf-fall urea concentration differs by region (HP 5%, Kashmir 3-4%); no HP source discusses phytotoxicity.

## KQ3. Biological control with doses

### Takeaway
The best-documented biological control in HP apple is conservation of the parasitoid Aphelinus mali (aerial woolly aphid) and other predators; no HP release dose is given anywhere I could open. For mites, the HP manual gives one release rate: three releases of the predatory mite Neoseiulus longispinosus at a predator-prey ratio of 1:30. San Jose scale: the coccinellid Chilocorus infernalis at 10-20 beetles/tree. Microbial products (Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Beauveria) have almost no foliar-disease dose in HP sources; Beauveria and Verticillium lecanii gave only about 22-29% reduction of woolly aphid colonies in the UHF Nauni trial, versus 80-90% for thiamethoxam/chlorpyriphos.

### Cited Findings

**Woolly apple aphid: Aphelinus mali and predators**
- A. mali: "Woolly aphids can be managed by the release of nymphal parasitoid Aphelinus mali"; syrphids (Episyrphus balteatus, Eupeodes corollae) target superficial colonies; conserve A. mali, coccinellids, syrphids and chrysopids; European earwig is an omnivorous predator; flowering plants on borders feed natural enemies; sticky bands around the stem in April-May and October; keep basins weed-free; "Fungal pathogen Verticillium lecanii is the known fungal pathogen of E. lanigerum" — GIZ-IPM (no release number or fungus dose printed)
- Introduction history and range: A. mali introduced in Kullu valley in 1937; well established in some areas but reinfestation from root colonies beyond its reach prevents complete control; coccinellids, syrphids and chrysopids also exercise some control — WAA-thesis; also IJE-phosmet (mid-1930s, Kullu and middle Kinnaur)
- Spiti (Tabo) field trial, 2020-21 and 2021-22, three blocks of 40 Royal Delicious trees; sprays counted at 7 days after treatment (DAT). Colonies per twig before to after: conventional (chlorpyriphos 20 EC) 6.36 to 0.10 with parasitoid wasps 1.20 to 0.00 (parasitoid wiped out); "organic" (azadirachtin; text says 0.003%, the table says 0.03%) 7.48 to 4.48 with parasitoid 2.74 to 2.88; natural farming (Agniastra with Thuklang) 6.06 to 2.96 with parasitoid 1.54 to 0.82. Authors attribute the A. mali build-up in the organic block to abundant host colonies and suspect the odour of cow urine/jeevamrit repels the wasp in the NF block — IH-Spiti-WAA
- Biopesticide trial at Matiana (Shimla), Oct-Nov 2016, aerial colonies: mean reduction in colony count over 28 days for the higher concentrations of Beauveria bassiana (2x10^10 cfu/L) 29.17%, fish oil (1.0%) 31.32%, neem oil (2.0%) 28.95% and azadirachtin (0.02%) 29.83%, all at par; the earlier-recommended thiamethoxam 0.025% and chlorpyriphos 0.04% gave about 90% reduction at 28 DAT. "The biopesticides did not prove much effective in managing E. lanigerum but were found comparatively safe to natural enemies" — WAA-thesis
- Same thesis, safety to A. mali (adult emergence): neem oil 1.0% 60-80%; chlorpyriphos only 15-20%; fish oil safest to the ladybirds Coccinella septempunctata (0% mortality at 72 h) and Hippodamia variegata (10-15%) — WAA-thesis
- The thesis tested B. bassiana at 1x10^10 and 2x10^10 cfu/L and the Verticillium lecanii product at concentrations listed in its Table 1; the table text was scrambled in extraction, so the V. lecanii dose is not quoted here.

**Mites**
- Peak July-August predators: Stethorus punctum, Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi, Orius sp., predatory mites (Amblyseius longispinosus, A. fallacis, Zetzellia mali, Neoseiulus longispinosus); "Three releases of the predatory mite, Neoseiulus longispinosus, at the predator-prey ratio of 1:30, have proved helpful in controlling the infestation" — GIZ-IPM
- A predator-friendly approach: just 1/3 of leaves with predatory mites can keep spider mites in check; 0.5-1 predatory mite per leaf can hold spider mites below the damage threshold; predatory mites are very sensitive to sulphur and many pesticides — GIZ-IPM
- In HP, Chrysoperla zastrowi sillemi is the more common lacewing than C. carnea — GIZ-IPM
- A 2005 survey of 9 HP districts recorded 10 predatory-mite species on fruit crops (including E. finlandicus, N. longispinosus), predator numbers "very scarce" because of insecticide/acaricide use **[snippet; search summary of Thakur & Dinabandhoo]** — [JBC article 15068](https://informaticsjournals.com/index.php/jbc/article/download/15068/12853/15717)

**San Jose scale**
- Parasitoids Aphytis sp. and Encarsia perniciosi; the predator Chilocorus infernalis at 10-20 beetles/tree "is quite effective"; reduce summer pesticide use to protect parasitoids; pheromone traps from pink bud to harvest for monitoring crawlers/males — GIZ-IPM
- Earlier HP biocontrol work (Rawat et al. 1988) lists Chilocorus bijugus, Pharoscymnus flexibilis, Encarsia perniciosi and Aphytis proclia; four exotic predatory beetles released earlier had not established **[snippet; scalenet records]** — [ScaleNet Rawat et al. 1988](https://scalenet.info/references/RawatThPa1988/); [ScaleNet Thakur et al. 1989](https://scalenet.info/references/ThakurRaPa1989/)

**Soil/root pathogens (not leaf-visible but cause leaf yellowing)**
- Collar rot and white root rot: biocontrol agents Trichoderma harzianum, T. viride, Bacillus subtilis, B. megaterium (collar rot) and T. viride, T. harzianum, B. subtilis and Pseudomonas fluorescens (white root rot) are recommended; no dose printed. Soil amendments of neem leaves/cake, Lantana leaves, cabbage leaves and deodar needles; mustard cake or mulch with mustard plants for collar rot — GIZ-IPM
- The HP schedule says only "Application of bio-control agents/bio-pesticides can be used against collar rot, root rot and root borers" with no dose — Spray-2023
- Apple root borer: entomopathogenic nematodes Steinernema pravassos and Heterorhabditis spp. are listed with no dose (text truncated in the extraction I read) — GIZ-IPM

**Traps**
- Sticky bands for woolly aphid (above); pheromone traps for San Jose scale monitoring (above); methyl-eugenol pheromone trap for fruit fly and light traps for apple root borer (June-July, adults killed in kerosinised water) — Spray-2023; GIZ-IPM

### Inferences
- For woolly aphid the honest HP message is: conserve A. mali (avoid broad-spectrum sprays in April-June; the HP schedule itself says to minimise insecticidal sprays then to protect lady beetles and syrphid flies), keep wounds painted, use sticky bands, and accept that root colonies persist.
- Natural-farming sprays may suppress colonies (Spiti: 6.06 to 2.96 per twig at 7 DAT) but the parasitoid in the same block also fell (1.54 to 0.82); the trial was one dry-temperate site with one season-pair; the Frontiers-NF survey shows higher aphid incidence on NF orchards in 3 of 4 blocks. The efficacy of NF sprays on woolly aphid is therefore promising but not established.
- Microbial biopesticides (Beauveria, Verticillium) gave only about a quarter to a third reduction in the one HP trial, so they should be presented as supportive, not as a cure.

### Gaps
- No dose for Trichoderma, Pseudomonas, Bacillus for apple foliar disease; the Trichoderma/Bacillus basin application rate for collar/white root rot is not in any opened HP source.
- No dose for A. mali field release, Chrysoperla release or Beauveria/Lecanicillium on mites in HP apple.
- No HP source found for Chrysoperla release numbers on apple aphids.
- The Verticillium lecanii dose and the exact concentrations tested in the WAA thesis for neem oil/azadirachtin at the low level (neem oil 1.0% is stated; fish oil 0.5/1.0%, azadirachtin 0.01/0.02%) could not be re-verified against the scrambled Table 1.

## KQ4. Botanicals and permitted organic sprays with concentrations and phenological timing

### Takeaway
HP sources give few botanical concentrations. Verified numbers: horticultural mineral oil (HMO) 4 L per 200 L (2%) at half-inch green tip to tight cluster for San Jose scale and mite eggs, and 2 L per 200 L (1%) at walnut size for mites; wettable sulphur 600 g per 200 L at late dormancy for powdery mildew; Bordeaux mixture 2 kg copper sulphate + 2 kg quicklime per 200 L post-harvest (canker/collar rot); copper oxychloride 600 g per 200 L post-harvest. Neem oil (up to 2%) and azadirachtin (0.02%) gave only about 29% reduction of woolly aphid colonies in the HP trial. No HP-validated botanical for scab, Marssonina, Alternaria or powdery mildew was found; severe scab cannot be controlled by natural methods alone.

### Cited Findings

**Phenological stages used by HP schedule:** silver tip, green tip, half-inch green tip, tight cluster, pink bud, bloom, petal fall, fruit set (pea stage), fruit development (walnut size) — Spray-2023

**Spray oils**
- HP insect schedule: Horticulture Mineral Oil 4 L per 200 L water at half-inch green tip (San Jose scale and mite); HMO 2 L per 200 L at fruit development (walnut size) as an alternative for mites — Spray-2023
- HMO 4 L per 200 L between half-inch green and tight cluster for San Jose scale; if missed, in May use "2 liters of summer oil per 200 liters of water"; the same manual says that "due to the environmental impact, Horticultural Mineral Oil should be prohibited. Only use the product in exceptional cases if the infestation is particularly high"; HMO at 1% (2 L/200 L) at walnut stage for mites; the dormant oil also kills overwintering European red mite eggs and two-spotted mite adults — GIZ-IPM
- Avoid captan within 15 days before and after oil — Spray-2023
- Older HP trial (Bajaura, Royal Delicious, 30-50% twig infestation): 2% miscible oil before flowering at green tip followed by an insecticide after flowering; also suggested alternate-year oil sprays **[snippet via search summary]** — [IJAgS record 30078](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/30078)

**Sulphur, copper, lime sulphur**
- Powdery mildew: four-spray programme "starting with wettable sulphur (600 gm / 200 litres water) after pruning at late dormancy followed by another three sprays at green tip, petal fall and after 2 weeks of petal fall" with systemic fungicides (the later three sprays in the programme are conventional fungicides) — GIZ-IPM
- Sulphur and lime sulphur are listed among "alternative products" usable in summer under lower infection pressure ("good alternatives"); sulphur is "preventive"; lime sulphur "curative" with side effect on Alternaria leaf spot and core rot; the dosage column of the table is scrambled in extraction so no lime-sulphur or sulphur summer dose is quoted. Predatory mites are sensitive to sulphur — GIZ-IPM
- Copper: post-harvest copper oxychloride 600 g per 200 L or Bordeaux mixture (copper sulphate 2 kg + quicklime 2 kg per 200 L water); repeat at bud swell (February-March) if disease was severe; Bordeaux paste (copper sulphate 800 g + lime 1000 g in 10 L water) or Bordeaux paint (copper sulphate 1 kg + lime 2 kg + linseed oil 3 L) on pruning cuts and canker wounds; stem whitewash with lime + copper sulphate in March and November — GIZ-IPM. (Contradiction inside the same manual: the scab section says copper should not be used after harvest so as not to disrupt leaf decomposition.)
- Collar rot and white root rot: scarify wounds near the collar and apply Bordeaux paint or other copper paint in winter — Spray-2023
- Neither the 2023 HP spray schedule nor the HP manual gives a foliar Bordeaux mixture or lime sulphur concentration for scab or Marssonina in the growing season.
- A non-HP product label (Canada, 30% calcium polysulphide) gives about 0.9% (183 mL per 20 L) for scab/mildew pre-pink to second cover and about 3.7% for dormant clean-up **[snippet via search summary; foreign label, not suitable for a card]** — [search result: US EPA / labels](https://www3.epa.gov/pesticides/chem_search/ppls/033955-00097-20010920.pdf)

**Neem and other botanicals on woolly aphid (HP trial)**
- Matiana (Shimla), Oct-Nov 2016: neem oil 1.0% and 2.0%, azadirachtin 0.01% and 0.02%, fish oil 0.5% and 1.0%, B. bassiana 1x10^10 and 2x10^10 cfu/L; higher concentrations all gave only about 28-31% mean reduction in colony counts; neem oil with 0.01% emulsifier — WAA-thesis
- "Organic farming" arm in Spiti used azadirachtin (0.003% in text, 0.03% in table); colonies per twig fell only from 7.48 to 4.48 at 7 DAT — IH-Spiti-WAA

**Urea**
- Urea 5% (10 kg per 200 L) pre-leaf-fall in October-November (see KQ2) — GIZ-IPM; Spray-2023

**Timing links to disease cycles (from the HP manual and schedule)**
- Scab: preventive cover from budding through the primary scab season; in scab-prone areas keep 12-14 days between sprays up to the primary scab stage — Spray-2023; GIZ-IPM
- Marssonina: protective sprays from walnut stage to harvest at 15-20 day intervals; Alternaria: from pea stage to harvest — GIZ-IPM (the listed products are conventional fungicides)
- Hail damage: 200 g boric acid + 500 g zinc sulphate + 250 g quicklime per 200 L within 3-4 days (a nutritional/safener measure, not a fungicide) — Spray-2023

**Laboratory botanical lead (not HP field-validated)**
- Garlic clove extract at 10% and 15% gave 100% in-vitro inhibition of the Alternaria/premature-leaf-fall pathogen **[snippet via search summary; paper not opened]** — [search result: apple blotch papers](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/Ijas/article/view/180170)

### Inferences
- No botanical or approved organic spray in the opened HP sources controls scab or Marssonina once infection pressure is high. Sulphur and copper are the only non-synthetic fungicide classes named in the HP manual for apple, and the HP schedule itself relies on synthetic fungicides; HP's official statement that scab appeared "only in orchards where the recommended spray schedule had not been followed" (snippet) suggests the app should say natural sprays alone are not enough in a scab year.
- The card for scale and mite eggs can quote HMO (4 L/200 L at half-inch green tip) but should add the manual's own caution about environmental impact and the 15-day gap with captan.
- A card for powdery mildew can quote "wettable sulphur 600 g per 200 L at late dormancy" and prune-off of silver shoots; the later in-season sulphur rate was not retrieved.

### Gaps
- No HP concentration for NSKE, neem oil, garlic, chilli, whey, milk or soap for apple foliar diseases; no in-season sulphur or lime-sulphur dose from an HP source.
- Foliar Bordeaux mixture strength for apple scab/leaf blotch in HP not found in an opened source.
- Whether sulphur/copper sprays are compatible with the PK3Y natural-farming rules was not checked.

## KQ5. Natural-farming formulations with doses for apple (PK3Y / SPNF / UHF Nauni), trial results

### Takeaway
The one HP source with apple foliar doses is the Mashobra SPNF trial (2020-24): jeevamrit 10 L in 100 L water, khatti lassi 5 L in 100 L, "Ramban" 7 L jeevamrit + 3 L lassi in 100 L, agniastra 5 L in 100 L, brahmastra 5 L in 100 L and dashparni ark 5 L in 100 L (woolly aphid), sprayed every 15 days or as needed; sonthastra and darekastra undiluted; jeevamrit soil drench 3-5 L per plant every 21 days; ghanjeevamrit 200-400 g per plant. Quantified efficacy data are almost absent: one Spiti woolly-aphid trial (Agniastra with Thuklang cut colonies from 6.06 to 2.96 per twig in 7 days) and a farm survey showing higher aphid and leaf-folder incidence under NF.

### Cited Findings

**Doses from the Mashobra SPNF apple trial (2020-24, M9/M7/MM111/seedling rootstock combinations, Gala, Red Chief, Royal Delicious etc.)**
- Natural preparations were sprayed "at 15-day intervals or as and when required": for fungal foliar diseases sonthastra, khatti lassi, "Ramban Rognashi" and jeevamrit; for insect pests darekastra, brahmastra and agniastra; for woolly apple aphid dashparni ark — IH-Mashobra
- Foliar application: jeevamrit 10 L in 100 L water; khatti lassi 5 L in 100 L water; Ramban 7 L jeevamrit + 3 L lassi in 100 L water; sonthastra no dilution; darekastra no dilution; agniastra 5 L in 100 L water; brahmastra 5 L in 100 L water; dashparni ark 5 L in 100 L water; Saptdhanya Ankur spray at 80% flowering, at fruit set and 15 days before harvest to enhance fruit quality — IH-Mashobra
- Soil: jeevamrit drench every 21 days, 3 L per plant (rootstock plants) and 5 L per plant (seedling plants), applied in 3-4 inch trenches outside the basin covered with dry grass (WAPASA); ghanjeevamrit at field preparation 200 g per plant (rootstock) and 400 g per plant (seedling), and 1 t/ha at intercropping — IH-Mashobra
- Poudhlep (protective trunk coating) applied four times a year: March (first/second week), May (second/third week), last week of September/first week of October, and 21 December-14 January (ingredients not given) — IH-Mashobra
- Reported outcomes: higher organic carbon, microbial load, AMF spores, earthworms, firmness, ascorbic acid; "variable but notable effectiveness in managing pests and diseases"; net returns Rs 12,74,640.25/ha (SPNF) vs Rs 8,30,840.01/ha (CF) in 2023. No disease or pest percentages are given — IH-Mashobra
- Consistency check: the Mashobra paper also names Beejamrit seed treatment and describes SPNF as "Astras"; neemastra and agniastra are described only in general terms there — IH-Mashobra

**Recipes from UHF Nauni (Indian Farming 2023)**
- Jeevamrit: 10 kg cow dung + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg jaggery + 2 kg pulse flour + a handful of bund soil, made up to 200 L with water, ferment in shade 48 h; described as preventing fungal and bacterial plant diseases — UHF-NF
- Neemastra: 100 L water + 10 L cow urine + 2 kg cow dung + 5 kg small neem twigs with leaves, covered with a gunny bag in shade 48 h, stirred morning and evening; for whitefly, aphid, jassid, sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars — UHF-NF
- Agniastra: 2 kg crushed neem leaves + 500 g tobacco powder + 500 g green chilli chutney + 250 g garlic chutney in 20 L cow urine, boiled on low flame under a lid, kept 48 h with stirring morning and evening; for sap-sucking insects, small caterpillars, pod borers (dilution not stated in this article) — UHF-NF
- Brahmastra (1 acre): leaf paste of any 5 plants (darek, sitaphal, castor, papaya, mango, guava, datura, duranta etc.) at 200 g per plant in 4 L cow urine, heated to boil, cooled 48 h; "Mix 2.5-3 litres of solution in 200 litres of water" — UHF-NF
- Note the dilution difference: brahmastra 2.5-3 L per 200 L (UHF-NF, per-acre field crops) vs 5 L per 100 L (IH-Mashobra, apple). These are not reconciled in the sources. Beejamrit (5 kg dung in cloth in 20 L water up to 12 h; 50 g lime in 1 L water overnight; squeeze dung bundle thrice; add handful of soil, 5 L cow urine and the lime water) is a seed/seedling treatment — UHF-NF
- A 2% agniastra foliar spray rate and 6-8 L per 200 L brahmastra for general crops is given in an ICAR guide for non-HP conditions **[snippet via search summary]** — [Indian Farming 75(8) 2025, ICAR NEH](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/174013/63853/503122)

**Spiti Agniastra for woolly aphid (Tabo, UHF Nauni)**
- Composition: leaves of the local herb Thuklang (Hyoscyamus niger) 5 kg, cow urine 20 L, tobacco powder 500 g, green chilli paste 500 g, garlic paste 250 g; cooked at low flame to boiling, kept 48 h, stirred clockwise 2-3 minutes morning and evening; applied as "5 litres of concentrated Agniastr in 200 litres of water"; concentrate to be used within three months. Thuklang (Khurasani ajwain/henbane, Solanaceae, alkaloids hyoscyamine and scopolamine, "poisonous in large quantities") replaced neem, which is not locally available at 3000-3600 m — IH-Spiti-WAA
- Result: NF colonies per twig 6.06 pre-count to 2.96 at 7 DAT, versus 0.10 (chlorpyriphos) and 4.48 (azadirachtin); "significantly equal yield over conventional farming"; no season-long series or replicated SE shown — IH-Spiti-WAA

**Farm-level evidence**
- Kinnaur NF orchardist: woolly aphid "was observed but it was controlled by the spray of Dashparni ark. Although the yield was less but the quality of fruit was better" (anecdote) — UHF-NF
- Kinnaur / Shimla farmer: "Jivamrita and Khatti Lassi were effective in controlling fungal diseases, whereas Saptdhyankur enhanced the colour and shine in apple" (anecdote) — UHF-NF
- Theog (Shimla, 2300 m): jeevamrit, ghanjeevamrit and dashparni ark used on "certain plants" then entire 2 bigha from 2019; apple output reported up from 50 to 180 boxes and "saved her apples from damage by hail and pests"; input cost from Rs 20,000 to about Rs 2,000 (self-reported) — [HP Agriculture Dept success story](https://agriculture.hp.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Satya-Devi_Theog_Shimla_Eng.pdf)
- HP-wide: more than 2.5 lakh farmers certified under CETARA-NF; NF practising farmers "reported comparatively lower pest intensity" (polyhouse and mixed crops, not apple-specific) — IF-HP-NF

**Survey comparison, 140 apple farms (70 NF, 70 CF) in Shimla and Kinnaur blocks**
- Pest incidence higher on NF in most pest-block cells (see KQ1 numbers); disease incidence mixed — NF lower on some (Marssonina Jubbal 1.99 vs 8.7; collar rot) and higher on others (Alternaria Pooh 8 vs 1.83, Nichar 8.84 vs 4.19; canker in Nichar and Pooh); yield about 1.59% higher and variable cost 46.76% lower under NF; authors say NF inputs named were Jeevamrit, Beejamrit, Neemastra, Agniastra, Bramhastra and fermented plant extracts, without any doses; observational study, confounded by block — Frontiers-NF

**Tobacco caution for apple**
- Agniastra contains tobacco powder; the TMV concern raised for solanaceous vegetables (previous note) does not apply to apple, but Thuklang (Hyoscyamus) is a poisonous Solanaceae and the sources give no PPE or pre-harvest interval guidance for agniastra on apple fruit — IH-Spiti-WAA

### Inferences
- A defensible app card for natural-farming apple foliar sprays can quote the Mashobra rates (khatti lassi 5%, jeevamrit 10%, agniastra/brahmastra/dashparni ark 5% of spray volume) and the 15-day interval, labelled "from the UHF Nauni / RHR&TS Mashobra SPNF trial; efficacy not quantified".
- Because the Spiti agniastra dilution (5 L per 200 L = 2.5%) is half the Mashobra figure (5 L per 100 L = 5%), the card should show the source for each and not merge them.
- None of the natural-farming sprays has HP trial evidence of controlling scab, Marssonina, Alternaria or powdery mildew; the only support is farmer testimony and a non-quantified trial statement.

### Gaps
- Official PK3Y recipe and dose sheet (spnfhp.nic.in) not accessible.
- Recipes for khatti lassi (fermentation days, buttermilk ageing), sonthastra (dry ginger), darekastra, dashparni ark and saptdhanya ankur were not in an opened HP source; do not print ingredient lists for them.
- No replicated HP trial of jeevamrit/lassi vs scab, mildew, Marssonina, or Alternaria with disease percentages.
- Spray timing by phenological stage for the NF sprays (e.g., green tip vs pink bud) was not given; only "15-day intervals or as and when required".
- The Kinnaur/Spiti trials are in the dry temperate zone and may not transfer to the wet mid-hills of Shimla.

## KQ6. Documented traditional / indigenous practices (HP)

### Takeaway
Little HP apple-specific indigenous pest/disease knowledge was found in an opened source. The documented HP "traditional" items are the cow-urine plant decoction drench for collar rot, cow-dung/clay paste and Bordeaux/Chaubatia paints on wounds and trunks, whitewashing stems, use of local weeds/leaves as soil amendments, and the Spiti use of the local herb Thuklang in agniastra.

### Cited Findings
- Cow-urine decoction: "Drenching cow urine decoction containing Eucalyptus, Melia and Vitex leaves (10 litres/200 litres water) per plant during spring, rainy and post rainy season is also quite effective" for collar rot — GIZ-IPM (HP manual; this source does not state it is traditional, and it gives no efficacy data)
- Cow dung paste (fresh cow dung 1 kg + clay soil 1 kg + water 1 L) on canker wounds is recommended — GIZ-IPM
- Soil amendments of neem leaves/cake, Lantana leaves, cabbage leaves and deodar needles for white root rot — GIZ-IPM
- Spiti: use of the wild herb Thuklang (Hyoscyamus niger, found at 3000-3600 m on open ground and roadsides) in place of neem in agniastra against woolly apple aphid — IH-Spiti-WAA
- Manual removal / mechanical practices recommended in the HP manual: sticky bands for woolly aphid, hand-collecting and killing apple root borer adults, light traps, opening tree basins in dormancy to collect grubs, exposing roots to winter sun for white root rot — GIZ-IPM
- Documentation of ITK in tribal HP exists (study of 120 farmers in Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti, validated with key informants, categories include horticultural crops) but the apple-pest practices are not given in the abstract that I could see **[snippet via search summary; ICAR Krishi record 67074 not reachable]** — [ICAR Krishi record 67074](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/67074)

### Inferences
- The "cow urine + local plant decoction" pattern (Eucalyptus/Melia/Vitex drench; Thuklang-based agniastra) links PK3Y inputs to local flora, so an app could present local-leaf variants of brahmastra/agniastra as acceptable, citing the Spiti precedent, with the caveat that local plants differ by altitude.

### Gaps
- No HP ethnobotanical or ITK paper on apple foliar disease or aphid management could be opened.
- No dose or efficacy for any traditional practice beyond those above; no data on ash, smoke, or bird-perching practices in HP apple orchards.
- The identity and dilution of the "two local plants" in other Kullu cow-urine sprays (see the earlier vegetables note) remain unknown for apple.
